\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Genome assembly aims to reconstruct a genome from the short DNA sequences
produced by sequencing. Under suitable conditions, the overlaps between these
sequences allow us to recover the original genome uniquely. But does this also
guarantee that the true genome is the one most likely to produce the particular
collection of sequences we observed? We investigate when this is the case
and when it fails.

We give an oriented, error-free counterexample in which every distinct
read of the true genome is observed and the original matched-string
coverage-and-bridging condition holds, yet a longer incorrect genome has
strictly higher exact likelihood. Same-length molecular counterexamples
show that the failure is not solely an artefact of varying genome length.

For a fixed truth with branching read support, the probability
that the truth maximizes unrestricted exact likelihood is
$O(n^{-(E-V)/2})$. More starkly, we exhibit a primitive,
repeat-restricted genome and a finite read sample for which
the unrestricted likelihood supremum is \emph{not attained
by any genome}. We show that its supremum always equals an
optimization over balanced read frequencies and is attained
by a finite genome exactly when some optimal flow is rational
and connected. For any \emph{specified} genome and finite
read sample, we give an exact rational-potential certificate
deciding whether it is an unrestricted ML maximizer,
without enumerating competing genomes. Unrestricted
ML may nevertheless fail to define an estimator.

On the positive side, repeat bridging collapses a
same-length, complete-support candidate class to the
truth's rotations: likelihood ties are a consequence of
structural uniqueness, not an extra optimization result.
For primitive truths satisfying the explicit P2 repeat
restriction, \emph{every} candidate shorter than twice
the true length has a different read law unless it is
a rotation of the truth, with no repeat restriction on
competitors. Exact ML over this finite length window
then succeeds with exponentially high probability.
We also give an always-attained, fixed-truth-consistent
MAP estimator under a summable geometric length prior,
and distinguish these results from the stronger
population identifiability and finite-read guarantees
for fully P2-restricted classes.

\end{abstract}
