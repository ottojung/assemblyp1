\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Genome assembly seeks to reconstruct the genome from which a collection of
short DNA reads originated. What does the observed evidence justify us in
concluding about the true genome, and what extra premises alter that
conclusion?

This paper distinguishes three claims that are easy to conflate but are not
interchangeable. \emph{(i) Deterministic reconstruction}: Shomorony et al.\
(2016) prove that a Not-So-Greedy algorithm recovers the true circular genome
under truth-relative information-feasibility conditions --- a statement about
what the reads contain, not about any likelihood ranking. \emph{(ii) Conditional
Bayesian certainty}: given an external promise that the true genome-and-reads
pair satisfies a specified event $E$, and only if the resulting supported
truth fibre is singleton up to genome equivalence, the posterior concentrates
on the truth --- a conditional statement that uses $P(E \mid \text{candidate},
\text{observed reads})$ and a correctly updated prior, not unrestricted
likelihood. \emph{(iii) Ordinary maximum likelihood}: maximizing
$P_D(R)$ over a stated candidate universe without access to $E$ as side
information --- a principled probability-based baseline that does not promise
finite pointwise correctness.

We prove a combinatorial rigidity theorem showing that, under bridging
conditions, the truth's spectrum is the unique positive circulation of its
window-support graph; on the same-length oriented slice this makes the truth
the unique likelihood maximizer up to circular rotation. We exhibit finite
counterexamples in which a competitor is strictly more likely --- informative
boundaries, not paradoxes --- showing that the deterministic guarantee does not
transfer to the unconditional likelihood-ranking objective. At the population
level, the truth is always a maximizer (a Gibbs/KL identity), and among
primitive P2 genomes it is the unique one.

Together, these results clarify what each type of evidence justifies, and what
additional premises are needed to strengthen the conclusion.
\end{abstract}
