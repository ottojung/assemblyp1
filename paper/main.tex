\large Structural reconstruction, statistical identifiability,\\
and finite-sample likelihood reliability}

\author{Vitaliy Mysak\\\href{mailto:assemblyp1@vau.place}{\texttt{assemblyp1@vau.place}}}

\date{\today}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Maximum likelihood chooses the genome under which the observed sequencing
reads are most probable. But reads contain two kinds of evidence: their
overlaps constrain how the genome can be assembled, while the frequency of
each read influences which genome receives the highest likelihood.
Does enough information for reconstruction also make the true genome the
most likely one?

We show that it need not. Even when a collection of reads satisfies strong
coverage and repeat-bridging conditions, a different genome can assign those
reads greater likelihood. We also prove a positive result under additional
restrictions: for a class of same-length genomes with oriented reads, the
overlap structure forces the true genome to be the unique likelihood
maximizer, up to rotation.

We then ask what can be learned if the distribution of reads is known exactly,
rather than estimated from a finite sample. For primitive genomes satisfying
explicit restrictions on repeats, we prove that this distribution identifies
the genome uniquely up to rotation. For finite samples from this class, we give an exponentially decreasing
upper bound on the probability of a wrong maximum-likelihood reconstruction.
\end{abstract}
