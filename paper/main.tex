\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Maximum likelihood offers a natural way to infer a genome from sequencing
data: choose the genome under which the observed reads are most probable.
But is the genome that generated the reads necessarily the most likely one?
It is also the conceptually simplest principled benchmark: for a fixed finite
candidate universe, the correct read-generation model, a uniform prior over
rotation classes, and zero-one loss, maximum likelihood maximizes the
prior-averaged probability of returning the true genome at every sample
size, among deterministic and randomized rules alike
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bayes-optimal}). That certificate is prior-averaged, not
per-genome, and does not extend to arbitrary priors or losses; how the
likelihood rank then behaves on particular read samples is the real
question. We investigate it in two settings: when only a finite sample
of reads is available, and when the underlying distribution of reads is
known exactly.

For finite samples, we examine a question raised by Shomorony et al.\ (2016):
do the conditions that guarantee genome reconstruction also guarantee that
maximum likelihood selects the true genome? We separate two readings of it.
Restricting to the feasible fiber, given that the truth satisfies it, is a
corollary of the reconstruction theorem; maximizing the original unrestricted
likelihood over the original candidate universe is the genuinely nontrivial
comparison, and it can fail. We establish conditions under which the answer is
yes, but also construct counterexamples in which the reads satisfy the original
reconstruction conditions and yet an incorrect genome has greater likelihood.
Thus, the information needed to reconstruct a genome does not always make it
the most likely explanation of the data.

When the read distribution is known exactly, the true genome always maximizes
expected log-likelihood. We establish conditions on genomic repeats under which
this distribution uniquely identifies the true genome. We further prove an explicit quantitative guarantee for finite samples: over the identifiable primitive-P2 candidate class the
probability that the exact maximum-likelihood rule errs decreases exponentially
in the number of independent reads, with a rate that is explicit but
deliberately loose (exponential in the read length). The theorem bounds the
\emph{error} probability, not the likelihood, and it does not claim that
per-truth finite-sample success is monotone in the sample size; we supply a
kernel-checked two-genome witness to the contrary.
\end{abstract}
