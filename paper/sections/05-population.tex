\section{Population maximum likelihood and uniqueness}
\label{sec:population}

The finite failures of Section~\ref{sec:finite} have two different causes, and
separating them is what motivates the model of this section.

\emph{Structural non-identifiability} (Example~\ref{ex:ABAB}) is the failure to
determine the genome from the read distribution at all. Bridging conditions
address it, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} shows how far they go on the
same-length slice.

\emph{Finite-sampling fluctuation} is a different phenomenon. Even a candidate
that is structurally admissible can beat the truth because the realized sample
is unrepresentative. The next example isolates this mechanism in its purest
form.

\begin{example}[Finite frequencies can beat structural admissibility]
\label{ex:frequencies}
Let $S=AABBC$ ($G=5$) be a primitive circular genome and $L=3$. Realize the
starts $(0,3)$, giving the observed reads $\{AAB,BCA\}$. Let $D=AABC$ and note
$|D|=4$. Both $S$ and $D$ are primitive and satisfy P1 (no repeated
$(L-1)=2$-mer). The realized sample satisfies the truth-side
information-feasibility condition, yet under the candidate-intrinsic exact
multinomial objective
\[
  \frac{\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(D;x)}{\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(S;x)}
  = \frac{(1/4)^2}{(1/5)^2} = \frac{25}{16} > 1 .
\]
The wrong genome wins because the realized sample consists only of reads to
which the shorter candidate assigns probability $1/4$ while the truth assigns
$1/5$. Structural admissibility has not failed; the empirical frequencies have
fluctuated.
\statusline{\kernelcheck{} in \texttt{FiniteSamplingCounterexample}
for the finite witness and strict comparison; the arithmetic is also
independently exact-checkable. This does not refute a historical statement,
since its candidate restrictions are project-level.}
\end{example}

The lesson is that strengthening the repeat axioms again would target the wrong
mechanism. The repair that removes empirical fluctuation is to replace the
finite sample by its population limit.

\subsection{Population likelihood}

\begin{definition}[Population read distribution and likelihood]
\label{def:population}
For a circular word $D$ and read length $L\le|D|$, the population read
distribution is
\[
  p_D(w) \;=\; \frac{\spec_L(D)(w)}{|D|},
\]
a probability distribution on length-$L$ words. For a fixed truth $S$, the
\emph{population log-likelihood} of a candidate $D$ is
\[
  \ellpop_S(D) \;=\; \sum_{w} p_S(w)\log p_D(w),
\]
with $\ellpop_S(D)=-\infty$ if some word with $p_S(w)>0$ has $p_D(w)=0$.
\statusline{\projchoice. This is the infinite-read (population) analogue of the
Medvedev--Brudno exact objective; it is a project-level formulation motivated by
the finite analysis, not an assumption of either historical paper.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Gibbs/KL identity; the truth is always a population maximizer]
\label{lem:gibbs}
For any candidate $D$,
\[
  \ellpop_S(D)-\ellpop_S(S) = -\KL(p_S\,\|\,p_D) \;\le\; 0,
\]
with equality if and only if $p_D=p_S$. Consequently, over \emph{any} candidate
class containing $S$, the truth is a population maximum-likelihood genome, with
no repeat condition required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\ellpop_S(S)=\sum_w p_S(w)\log p_S(w)$, so
\begin{align*}
  \ellpop_S(D)-\ellpop_S(S)
    &=\sum_w p_S(w)\log(p_D(w)/p_S(w))=-\KL(p_S\|p_D),
\end{align*}
where the right side is $+\infty$ precisely when $\ellpop_S(D)=-\infty$. Gibbs'
inequality \cite{cover2006} gives $\KL\ge0$ with equality iff the distributions
agree. The last claim is immediate since $S$ is in the class.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:gibbs} is the conceptual heart of the repair. At the population
level the maximizer statement is free; the entire content of a uniqueness theorem
is then \emph{spectrum identifiability}: for which candidate classes is
$p_D=p_S$ equivalent to $D\approx S$?

\subsection{P2 plus primitivity remove spectrum scaling}

Population equality compares \emph{normalized} spectra. The project uses two
candidate-intrinsic admissibility predicates to cross the gap to ordinary
spectrum equality.

\begin{definition}[P1 and P2]
\label{def:P1P2}
Fix the read length $L$. An oriented circular word $D$ satisfies
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{P1} if no length-$(L-1)$ word occurs more than once in $D$;
  \item \textbf{P2} if $D$ has no triple repeat of length $\ge L-1$ and every
        interleaved maximal repeat pair has a constituent of length
        $\le L-2$.
\end{itemize}
P1 is stronger than P2 (no repeated $(L-1)$-mer implies the P2 repeat bounds).
Both are project-level, candidate-intrinsic conditions; neither is a historical
assumption.
\statusline{\projchoice. The threshold in P2 is chosen to match the
complete-spectrum uniqueness theorem at $K=L-1$ below.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Primitive P2 spectra have gcd one]
\label{lem:scaling}
Let $S$ be a primitive P2-admissible circular word and $L\ge2$ with
$L\le|S|$. Let $c=\spec_L(S)$ viewed as an integer vector and let
$g=\gcd\{c(w):c(w)>0\}$. Then $g=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Suppose $g>1$. Work in the order-$(L-1)$ de Bruijn graph: vertices are
$(L-1)$-mers and each occurrence of a length-$L$ word is a directed edge
with multiplicity $c(w)$. Because $S$ spells a closed trail using every
edge exactly once, this edge multiset is balanced (in-degree equals
out-degree at every vertex) and weakly connected on its support.
Dividing every multiplicity by $g$ gives $c'=c/g$, still integer-valued.
Balance is a homogeneous linear condition, so it is preserved by division;
the support is unchanged ($c'(w)>0\iff c(w)>0$), so weak connectivity on
the support is preserved as well. Hence $c'$ is a balanced connected edge
multiset and spells an Eulerian closed trail $W$ with
$\spec_L(W)=c'$ and $|W|=|S|/g$.
Repeating $W$ gives $W^g$ with $|W^g|=|S|$ and
$\spec_L(W^g)=g\cdot\spec_L(W)=c=\spec_L(S)$: each cyclic length-$L$
window of $W$ lifts to exactly $g$ windows of $W^g$.
Thus $S$ and $W^g$ share the same ordinary $L$-spectrum.
$S$ satisfies P2, hence Ukkonen's condition at $K=L-1$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:BBT} applied to $S$ forces $S\sim W^g$ up to rotation.
But $W^g$ is a nontrivial whole-genome power while $S$ is primitive, and
primitivity is rotation-invariant --- a contradiction. So $g=1$.
Crucially, uniqueness is applied only to $S$; nothing requires $W^g$
itself to satisfy P2.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Normalized equality implies ordinary equality for primitive P2]
Let $S,D$ be primitive P2-admissible with $p_D=p_S$ for some $L\ge2$.
Then $\spec_L(D)=\spec_L(S)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$p_D=p_S$ rewrites as $|S|\,\spec_L(D)=|D|\,\spec_L(S)$, so the two
integer spectrum vectors are proportional. By the lemma both have gcd
$1$ over their positive entries, and proportional gcd-one vectors are
equal (indeed $|S|\mid|D|$ and $|D|\mid|S|$, so $|S|=|D|$).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why primitivity alone is not enough]
The unrestricted statement --- primitivity plus $p_D=p_S$ implies
$\spec_L(D)=\spec_L(S)$ --- is false. At $L=2$, the primitive words
$S=AAB$ ($|S|=3$) and $D=AAABAB$ ($|D|=6$) have
$\spec_2(S)=(AA{:}1,AB{:}1,BA{:}1)$ and
$\spec_2(D)=(AA{:}2,AB{:}2,BA{:}2)$, hence identical normalized spectra
$p_S=p_D=(1/3,1/3,1/3)$ but distinct ordinary spectra. The lemma above
uses P2 essentially, via the complete-spectrum uniqueness step.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The complete-spectrum uniqueness step}

The remaining step is a complete-spectrum uniqueness statement. The classical spectrum-reconstruction analysis has roots in Ukkonen's
condition and Pevzner's proof of the associated word-transformation
conjecture \cite{ukkonen1992,pevzner1995}, with an assembly-oriented
formulation in \cite{bresler2013}. The specialized Lean proof now
discharges the needed step internally, so the theorem below is
not an external premise of
Theorem~\ref{thm:population}.

\begin{theorem}[Complete-spectrum uniqueness under Ukkonen's condition]
\label{thm:BBT}
\srcfact{} \cite{ukkonen1992,pevzner1995,bresler2013}. Construct the $K$-mer graph from the complete
$(K+1)$-spectrum of a circular genome. If the genome satisfies Ukkonen's
condition --- no triple repeat and no interleaved repeat pair of length at least
$K$ --- then the graph has a unique Eulerian cycle, which spells the genome up
to cyclic rotation.
\end{theorem}

Setting $K=L-1$ aligns the theorem's condition exactly with P2: maximal triple
repeats then have length at most $L-2$, and every interleaved maximal-repeat pair
has a constituent of length at most $L-2$.

\begin{remark}[The complete-spectrum step is kernel checked]
\label{rem:internal-bbt}
The kernel-checked result \popml{} proves long-window uniqueness for primitive
P2 truths using a support-descent argument on the condensed graph, and its
axiom audit reports only the three standard axioms. Theorem~\ref{thm:BBT} records the classical identifiability step. The finite same-length rotation theorem is also proved in Lean.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Population uniqueness for the oriented primitive P2 class}

\begin{theorem}[Population uniqueness, oriented primitive P2]
\label{thm:population}
Let $L\ge2$. Among primitive P2-admissible oriented circular candidates, the
true genome $S$ is the unique population maximum-likelihood genome up to cyclic
rotation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $D$ be primitive and P2-admissible with $\ellpop_S(D)=\ellpop_S(S)$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:gibbs}, $p_D=p_S$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:scaling} both spectra
have gcd one, hence the corollary gives
$\spec_L(D)=\spec_L(S)$. Both $S$ and $D$ satisfy P2, hence Ukkonen's condition
at $K=L-1$, so the complete-spectrum uniqueness step applied to the complete
$(L)$-spectrum gives a unique Eulerian cycle and therefore $D\sim S$. The truth
is a maximizer by Lemma~\ref{lem:gibbs}. The complete-spectrum step is
supplied by Theorem~\ref{thm:BBT} in the source-faithful presentation, and is
proved internally in the kernel (Remark~\ref{rem:internal-bbt}); either route
proves the same conclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-read reliability: an exponential recovery theorem}
\label{sec:finite-recovery}

The population theorem answers the structural question: when can the read
distribution identify the genome?  A second positive theorem quantifies how
reliably \emph{finite} iid read samples discriminate hypotheses once the
candidate family is finite and identifiable.  This result directly connects
the population and finite-sampling questions without claiming that a
finite-sample ML estimator always selects the truth.

\begin{theorem}[Exponential maximum-likelihood recovery for a finite family]
\label{thm:finite-recovery}
Fix a finite read alphabet $W$, a true probability distribution $p$ on $W$,
and a finite set $\Theta$ of competing probability distributions $q$,
each distinct from $p$.  Write
\[
  \rho(p,q)=\sum_{w\in W}\sqrt{p(w)q(w)}
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \rho_*=\max_{q\in\Theta}\rho(p,q)<1
\]
when $\Theta$ is nonempty (otherwise the error event is empty).  For $n$
independent reads drawn from $p$, let
$L_q(r_1,\ldots,r_n)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}q(r_j)$.
Then
\[
  \Pr_p\!\bigl\{\exists q\in\Theta:
       L_q(R_1,\ldots,R_n)\ge L_p(R_1,\ldots,R_n)\bigr\}
  \;\le\; \sum_{q\in\Theta}\rho(p,q)^n
  \;\le\;|\Theta|\rho_*^n.
\]
In particular, for every target error tolerance $\delta>0$ there is an $N$
such that the probability of any wrong candidate tying or beating the
truth is at most $\delta$ for every $n\ge N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any read tuple $r$, set $a=L_p(r)$ and $b=L_q(r)$.  Both are
nonnegative, and on the event $b\ge a$ we have
$a\le\sqrt{ab}$, including when $a$ or $b$ vanishes.
Summing over read tuples distributed according to $p$ bounds the
pairwise event probability by
\[
 \sum_{r\in W^n}\sqrt{L_p(r)L_q(r)}
 =\left(\sum_{w\in W}\sqrt{p(w)q(w)}\right)^n
 =\rho(p,q)^n.
\]
The first displayed bound follows by the union bound.  For probability
distributions, the squared-distance identity
$\sum_w(\sqrt{p(w)}-\sqrt{q(w)})^2=2(1-\rho(p,q))$
implies $\rho(p,q)<1$ exactly when $p\ne q$.
Thus a finite identifiable competitor family has a uniform
$\rho_*<1$, and its failure bound decays exponentially.
\end{proof}

\statusline{\kernelcheck{} for the finite-PMF pairwise, union, affinity, and
consistency theorems in \texttt{AssemblyP1.FiniteReadRecovery}.  This is a
finite-family theorem.  For the primitive-P2 class the finite-family
hypothesis is discharged in Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-finite-class}, so the same
bound applies to the entire unbounded-length candidate class; the general
finite-PMF statement remains a finite-family statement.  The normalized uniform-start circular-genome read law, its
read-occurrence equality, and finite-genome-family Hellinger bound are
also kernel checked in \texttt{AssemblyP1.FiniteReadRecoveryGenome}.
The primitive-P2 transport to strict separation is kernel-checked in
Section~\ref{sec:p2-finite-recovery}.}

For circular genomes with uniformly sampled start positions and oriented
error-free length-$L$ reads, the intended distributions are
$p_D(w)=\spec_L(D)(w)/|D|$.  The concrete read-law adapter and its finite-genome-family
bound are now kernel checked, under an explicit Hellinger separation
hypothesis. The completed primitive-P2 theorem above now supplies a fully proved
read-law distinguishability criterion for nonrotational, primitive-P2
admissible candidates, as formalized in
Section~\ref{sec:p2-finite-recovery}.  The general finite-PMF recovery
theorem still requires a finite competitor family, but for the complete
primitive-P2 admissible class Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-finite-class} proves that
this family is finite, without imposing an arbitrary shortlist.  The reverse-
complement-collapsed and product-binomial models require their own read-law
interfaces.  Neither the theorem nor its proof conditions on the
truth-dependent information-feasibility event, and neither contradicts the
strict finite-sample counterexamples of Section~\ref{sec:finite}.

\subsection{Strong consistency: eventual correctness almost surely}
\label{sec:strong-consistency}

The exponential bound controls the error probability at each fixed $n$.
The classical strong law gives the complementary asymptotic statement
directly, through the KL gap rather than through the affinity. We state it
precisely because the phrase ``infinite reads'' invites misreading: it
denotes a limit of finite samples, not an evaluation of an infinite product
of probabilities.

\begin{proposition}[Strong consistency of exact ML on a finite identifiable family]
\label{prop:strong-consistency}
Let $S$ be the true circular genome with uniform-start read law
$p_S(w)=\spec_L(S)(w)/|S|$, let $\Theta$ be a finite set of circular genomes
containing $S$ and closed under rotation, and assume every
$D\in\Theta$ not rotation-equivalent to $S$ has $p_D\neq p_S$
(identifiability modulo rotation).
Let $R_1,R_2,\dots$ be independent reads drawn from $p_S$. Then with
probability one there exists $N$ such that for all $n\ge N$,
\[
  L_S(R_1,\dots,R_n) \;>\; L_D(R_1,\dots,R_n)
  \qquad\text{for every } D\in\Theta
  \text{ not rotation-equivalent to } S.
\]
Consequently the exact-ML selection set over $\Theta$ is eventually exactly
the rotation class of $S$ --- rotations tie the truth at every $n$, since
identical laws give identical likelihoods --- and
$\Pr_S\{\text{$S$ is the strict maximizer at $n$}\}\to 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a wrong candidate $D$. The per-read log-likelihood ratio is a sample
average of iid terms,
\[
  \frac1n\log\frac{L_S}{L_D}(R_1,\dots,R_n)
  \;=\;\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \log\frac{p_S(R_i)}{p_D(R_i)},
\]
whose expectation is
$\sum_w p_S(w)\log(p_S(w)/p_D(w))=\KL(p_S\|p_D)\ge0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:gibbs}). The strong law of large numbers
gives almost-sure convergence of the average to $\KL(p_S\|p_D)$.
Identifiability gives $p_D\neq p_S$, so $\KL(p_S\|p_D)>0$; hence almost
surely the average is eventually positive, i.e.\ $L_S>L_D$ for all large
$n$. There are finitely many wrong candidates, so almost surely all of them
are beaten simultaneously eventually. Rotations of $S$ satisfy $p_D=p_S$ and
therefore tie $S$ at every $n$; no other candidate can persist in a tie,
because the same argument forces $L_S>L_D$ eventually whenever
$p_D\neq p_S$. The last claim follows since the displayed event has
probability one.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Zero-probability reads and the support convention]
\label{rem:support-convention}
With the convention $0\log 0=0$, words with $p_S(w)=0$ never occur and
contribute nothing to the KL sum or to the likelihood ratios; impossible
reads are handled exactly as in the finite theorems, where they form
zero-probability events. If instead $p_S(w)>0$ but $p_D(w)=0$ for some
wrong candidate $D$, then $\KL(p_S\|p_D)=+\infty$: every sample containing
$w$ has $L_D=0<L_S$, and such samples recur eventually almost surely, so
$D$ is beaten eventually as well. This matches the population convention
$\ellpop_S(D)=-\infty$ of Definition~\ref{def:population}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What the limit does and does not say]
\label{rem:limit-scope}
The statement is asymptotic: it supplies no finite $n$, no rate, and no
per-sample guarantee, and it covers neither the unrestricted candidate
universe nor non-identifiable families --- the sample-size question of
Remark~\ref{rem:sample-complexity} remains open, and per-truth success need
not be monotone (Proposition~\ref{prop:nonmonotone}). It is also distinct
from population uniqueness (Theorem~\ref{thm:population}): that theorem
fixes the exact read distribution and proves law-level separation with no
sampling at all, whereas this proposition concerns a growing sample and
concludes only eventual, almost-sure selection. The a.s.\ conclusion is
also implied by the summable exponential bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-recovery} via Borel--Cantelli; the strong-law route
above exhibits the mechanism, the KL gap, directly.
\end{remark}

\statusline{\mathfact{} proved here from the strong law of large numbers and
KL nonnegativity, both classical and cited (\cite{durrett2019,cover2006});
not kernel-checked. The a.s.\ statement concerns a finite, identifiable
candidate family and exact iid reads; it upgrades to no unrestricted or
non-identifiable class and supplies no finite sample size.}

\subsection{Primitive-P2 structure gives finite-read maximum-likelihood recovery}
\label{sec:p2-finite-recovery}

The previous finite-sample theorem assumes that each wrong candidate has a
distinct read distribution.  For the paper's main structural class, that
\emph{informational} condition is not an independent assumption: it follows
from the completed primitive-P2 population uniqueness theorem.

\begin{corollary}[Finite-sample ML recovery in the primitive-P2 class]
\label{cor:p2-finite-recovery}
Let $S$ be a primitive oriented circular genome satisfying $P2$ at read
length $L\ge 2$.  Fix \emph{any finite family} $\mathcal C$ of primitive
$P2$-admissible oriented circular genomes, possibly of lengths different
from $|S|$, none rotation-equivalent to $S$.  Observe $n$ independent
length-$L$ error-free reads with uniformly sampled circular start
positions in $S$.  Then there is a $\rho_*<1$ such that
\[
 \Pr_S\!\left\{
   \exists D\in\mathcal C:
       L_D(R_1,\dots,R_n)\ge L_S(R_1,\dots,R_n)
 \right\}
 \le |\mathcal C|\,\rho_*^{n},
\]
and for every $\delta>0$ there exists $N$ such that the displayed
wrong-competitor event has probability at most $\delta$ for all $n\ge N$.
Ties are counted as errors, so this covers every maximum-likelihood
tie-breaking rule.  For maximum-likelihood selection over the augmented
candidate family $\{S\}\cup\mathcal C$, the truth's rotation class therefore
wins with probability tending to one.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The uniform-start read distribution is exactly
$p_D(w)=\spec_L(D)(w)/|D|$ for every circular genome $D$, including the
case of multiple copies of a read, origin-crossing windows, and $L>|D|$.
The population-uniqueness theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:population}) says that equality of
population likelihood between an admissible $D$ and $S$ forces rotation
equivalence.  Gibbs equality says that two normalized read laws have
equal population likelihood precisely when those laws agree.  Hence
every $D\in\mathcal C$ has $p_D\neq p_S$.
The finite-PMF Hellinger theorem then yields
$\rho(p_S,p_D)<1$ for each such candidate.  Since $\mathcal C$ is
finite, its affinities admit a common maximum $\rho_*<1$ (the empty
family has zero error).  Applying the finite union bound above proves
the displayed inequality and the confidence conclusion.
\end{proof}

\texttt{AssemblyP1.FiniteReadRecoveryP2Bridge} and

The result is conditional on a finite, primitive-P2 admissible,

Corollary~\ref{cor:p2-finite-recovery-all} discharges the finiteness

This closes the \emph{finite-class} population-to-sampling implication
for caller-supplied families.  Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-finite-class} then shows
that the whole primitive-P2 admissible class, over all unbounded genome
lengths, is finite, so Corollary~\ref{cor:p2-finite-recovery-all} extends the
exponential bound to it.  These results also do not classify ordinary finite
likelihood maximizers for every observed sample, which can differ from
the truth even under structural identifiability.  The RC-collapsed
double-strand model and MB09's binomial surrogate remain separate.

\subsection{Finiteness of the primitive-P2 class: the finite-family premise is discharged}
\label{sec:p2-finite-class}

Corollary~\ref{cor:p2-finite-recovery} requires the competitor family
$\mathcal C$ to be finite, and requires the caller to supply it.  The
following theorem constructs the complete primitive-P2 admissible family
from an explicit genome-length bound, exponential in $L$.  Its finiteness
does not imply that enumeration is computationally small.

\begin{theorem}[Finiteness of the primitive-P2 admissible class]
\label{thm:p2-finite-class}
Fix a finite alphabet $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|=q$ and a read length
$L\ge2$, and set $K=L-1$.  Let $D$ be an oriented circular genome over
$\alpha$ of length $G$, primitive, with no maximal triple repeat of length
$\ge K$.  Then
\[
  G\;\le\;2\,q^{K}.
\]
Consequently the class of primitive P2-admissible oriented circular genomes
over $\alpha$, over \emph{all} positive lengths, is finite, and the bound is
tight: at $q=2$, $L=2$ the circular word $1100$ attains $G=4=2\cdot2^{1}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
Two regimes, both needed because a length-$K$ window wraps when $G<K$.

\emph{Wrap-around regime $G<L$.}  A length-$K$ window with $K\ge G$ sees the
whole circle, so two occurrences at distinct starts make the word
shift-invariant by a nonzero shift below $G$, hence non-primitive.  Every
$K$-window therefore occurs exactly once and $G\le q^{K}$.

\emph{Ordinary regime $L\le G$.}  Three starts sharing a $K$-window are
extended to a maximal common window: maximally backwards (a bounded maximum
over the $G$ possible back-shifts) and then forwards.  Two things can
happen.  Some extension phase reaches total length $\ge G$, which again
forces shift-invariance and contradicts primitivity.  Or both flanks
differ, and the extension is a maximal triple repeat of length $\ge K$,
which P2 forbids.  Both alternatives are impossible, so no $K$-window occurs
at three starts, and counting the fibers of the $K$-window map gives
$G\le2\,q^{K}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-sample ML recovery over the entire primitive-P2 class]
\label{cor:p2-finite-recovery-all}
Let $S$ be a primitive oriented circular genome satisfying $P2$ at read
length $L\ge2$.  Observe $n$ independent length-$L$ error-free reads with
uniformly sampled circular start positions in $S$.  Then the competitor
family is a \emph{constructed} finite set --- the admissible genomes of
length at most $2\,|\alpha|^{L-1}$, with the rotations of $S$ removed --- and
there is a $\rho_*<1$ such that
\[
 \Pr_S\!\left\{\exists D\ \text{admissible, nonrotational}:
       L_D(R_1,\dots,R_n)\ge L_S(R_1,\dots,R_n)\right\}
 \;\le\;|\mathcal C_S|\,\rho_*^{n},
\]
and for every $\delta>0$ there exists $N$ such that the displayed
wrong-competitor event has probability at most $\delta$ for all $n\ge N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-finite-class} every admissible candidate has length at
most $2\,|\alpha|^{L-1}$, so the candidate class is a finite set
$\mathcal C_S$ built from that bound.  Rotations of $S$ share $S$'s read law
and are removed, so every remaining competitor is admissible and
nonrotational.  Corollary~\ref{cor:p2-finite-recovery} applies to
$\mathcal C_S$ with no finite-family hypothesis left.
\end{proof}

This discharges the \emph{finite-family} premise of the primitive-P2

finite-sample ML maximizer for every data realization, and it does not cover
the reverse-complement-collapsed double-strand model or MB09's binomial
surrogate, whose candidate classes and read laws differ.


\subsection{An explicit uniform exponential rate for the primitive-P2 class}
\label{sec:p2-uniform-rate}

Corollary~\ref{cor:p2-finite-recovery-all} proves that a uniform
$\rho_*<1$ exists but leaves it abstract.  For the primitive-P2 class the gap
can be quantified from first principles, without invoking population
uniqueness.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit uniform separation and exponential error decay]
\label{thm:p2-uniform-rate}
Fix a finite alphabet $\alpha$ with $q=|\alpha|$ and read length $L\ge2$, and
set $B=2q^{L-1}$.  Let $S$ be a primitive oriented circular genome satisfying
$P2$ at read length $L$, with $G_S=|S|$.  Every nonrotational admissible
competitor $D$ with $G_D=|D|$ has
\[
  1-\rho(p_S,p_D)\;\ge\;\frac{1}{8\,(G_S G_D)^2}\;\ge\;\frac{1}{8\,B^4}.
\]
Consequently, after $n$ independent oriented length-$L$ reads with uniform
circular starts,
\[
  \Pr_S\!\left\{\exists D\ \text{nonrotational admissible}:
       L_D(R_1,\dots,R_n)\ge L_S(R_1,\dots,R_n)\right\}
  \;\le\; N_C\,\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{n}{8B^4}\Bigr),
\]
where $N_C$ is the number of distinct competitor read laws,
$N_C\le\sum_{g=1}^{B}q^{g}$; and
$n\ge 8B^4\log(N_C/\delta)$ suffices for error at most $\delta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
The read law of a genome of length $G$ is $p(w)=c_w/G$ with $c_w$ the number
of circular starts spelling $w$, so a nonzero difference of two such laws has
the form $(c_w G_D-c'_w G_S)/(G_S G_D)$ with a nonzero integer numerator, hence
absolute value at least $1/(G_SG_D)$.  The squared-distance identity
$\sum_w(\sqrt{p(w)}-\sqrt{q(w)})^2=2(1-\rho)$ and the coordinate inequality
$|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|\ge|p-q|/2$ on $[0,1]$ turn this into
$1-\rho\ge 1/(8(G_SG_D)^2)$.  The primitive-P2 length bound $G\le B$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-finite-class} gives the uniform form, and the finite union
bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-recovery} yields the displayed exponential
bound.
\end{proof}

\texttt{abs\_sub\_le\_two\_abs\_sqrt\_sub}, the gap lemma

multinomial likelihood, distinct from any Bayes-optimality statement.}

Unlike Corollary~\ref{cor:p2-finite-recovery-all}, this bound does not use the
population uniqueness theorem: it is a direct finite-sample separation.  It is
also compatible with the non-monotonicity of Section~\ref{sec:finite}: the
exponential statement concerns the eventual rate, not a pointwise monotone
success curve.  The competitor class here is restricted to primitive-P2
admissible, oriented exact iid reads; it is not universal maximum likelihood
over all genomes and not the MB09 product-binomial approximation.

\begin{remark}[Sharpness of the rate, and the unrestricted universe]
\label{rem:rate-sharpness}
The separation bound $1/(8B^4)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-uniform-rate} is valid
but not tight.  Complete exact enumeration of the primitive-P2 class at
$B=4,6,8,16$ gives exact worst-case separations $1-\rho$ larger than the
proved bound by factors $274$, $903$, $2116$, and $16648$; the observed extremal pairs
are one-symbol insertions, suggesting separation of order $1/B$; the sharp
exponent is conjecturally $1$ rather than $4$.  The finite-class
non-tightness is an exact finite computation and the asymptotic statement is a
conjecture whose missing ingredient is a separation lower bound
$1-\rho\ge c_1/B$, not yet proved in any form; nothing in this remark is
kernel-checked.  The exact arithmetic
is reproducible with \path{scripts/verify\_exponent\_265.py}, and the record
is \path{docs/restricted-vs-unrestricted-exponent-265.md}.  Over the
\emph{unrestricted} candidate universe no uniform positive separation exists
at all: the circular genomes $AB$ and $ABAB$ induce the identical oriented
read law at every read length, so once such a competitor is admissible the
exact-ML error probability is $1$ for every $n$.  The length bound and the
exclusion of read-law duplicates are therefore necessary hypotheses of the
theorem, not conveniences.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scope of the population theorem}

The theorem concerns oriented read types. The corresponding question for
reverse-complement-collapsed molecule classes involves a different read
distribution and is not claimed here.

The population model isolates structural identifiability by using the true
read distribution instead of a finite sample. Section~\ref{sec:finite}
treats the original finite-frequency question.

\begin{remark}[Finite-space consistency is a different theorem]
\label{rem:consistency-distinction}
Asymptotic recovery for a \emph{finite} candidate space --- per-truth success
tending to $1$ as $n\to\infty$ when candidates are pairwise distinguishable ---
is a consistency theorem about a growing sample
(Remark~\ref{rem:sample-complexity}). The population theorem above instead
fixes the exact read distribution and allows an arbitrarily large, even
unbounded, candidate class; it involves no sampling and no finiteness, and it
says nothing about uniqueness within a tie class. The two are related only
through identifiability, which each needs in its own way, and neither is
replaced by conditioning on information feasibility.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Finite-sample success is not monotone in the number of reads}
\label{sec:nonmonotone}

Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-recovery} bounds the \emph{error} of exact
maximum-likelihood selection, and the bound decreases monotonically in $n$.
The success probability itself need not: because the exact objective compares
likelihoods on the realized sample, adding one more read can move the decision
boundary and lower the chance that the truth is the strict maximizer. When every
competitor has disjoint support from the truth, the Lean-checked pairwise bound
gives zero error as soon as $n>0$; the generic positive-rate formula handles
the remaining cases without assigning a finite value to $-\log 0$. The
following two-genome witness makes non-monotonicity exact. The qualitative
sawtooth effect in discrete tests is well known
\cite{chernick2002}; here the result instead concerns
\emph{per-truth} exact-ML success for two circular genomes.
The mechanisms are analogous but the decisions and losses differ.

\begin{proposition}[Non-monotonicity of exact finite-sample ML success]
\label{prop:nonmonotone}
Let the read length be $L=2$ over the binary alphabet $\{A,C\}$, and let the
candidate class consist of the two circular genomes
\[
  S = AAC \ (\text{truth, length }3), \qquad D = AAAC \ (\text{length }4),
\]
sampled with a uniformly random start. The uniform-start read laws are
\[
  p_S : AA\mapsto \tfrac13,\ AC\mapsto \tfrac13,\ CA\mapsto \tfrac13,\ CC\mapsto 0,
\]
\[
  p_D : AA\mapsto \tfrac12,\ AC\mapsto \tfrac14,\ CA\mapsto \tfrac14,\ CC\mapsto 0,
\]
so the two distributions are distinct and Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-recovery}
applies with affinity $\rho(p_S,p_D) = (1+\sqrt2)/\sqrt6 < 1$. Writing
$\mathrm{succ}(n)=\Pr_S\{L_S(R_1,\dots,R_n) > L_D(R_1,\dots,R_n)\}$ for the
probability that the truth is the strict exact-ML maximizer,
\[
  \mathrm{succ}(1) = \tfrac23, \qquad \mathrm{succ}(2) = \tfrac49,
  \qquad\text{so}\qquad \mathrm{succ}(1) > \mathrm{succ}(2).
\]
The oscillation is repeated:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
n & 1&2&3&4&5&6&7\\ \hline
\mathrm{succ}(n)&\frac23&\frac49&\frac{20}{27}&\frac{16}{27}&
\frac{64}{81}&\frac{496}{729}&\frac{416}{729}
\end{array}
\]
Thus finite-sample success is not monotone in $n$, although it tends to $1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On any read tuple containing $a$ copies of $AA$ and no $CC$, the exact
likelihood ratio is
\[
  \frac{L_D}{L_S} = \frac{3^n}{2^{\,2n-a}},
\]
so $L_D > L_S$ exactly when $a > n\log_2(4/3)$. For $n\ge1$ no positive-probability tuple ties: the ratio is a positive
power of $3$ divided by a power of $2$, so it cannot equal $1$.
At $n=0$ the empty likelihoods tie, and tuples containing $CC$ have
probability zero under $p_S$. Under $p_S$ each read is $AA$ with probability $1/3$. For $n=1$
the truth wins exactly when the single read is not $AA$, with probability
$2/3$. For $n=2$ the truth wins exactly when $a=0$, with probability
$(2/3)^2 = 4/9$. The competitor rewards the $AA$ count, so a single read is
more favorable to the truth than two.
\statusline{\kernelcheck{} for $\mathrm{succ}(1)=2/3$, $\mathrm{succ}(2)=4/9$,
and $\mathrm{succ}(1)>\mathrm{succ}(2)$ against the actual genome, read, and
likelihood definitions
(the Lean theorem \texttt{success\_not\_monotone}); \computed{} for an
independent exact-rational cross-check over all read tuples
(\texttt{scripts/verify\_nonmonotonicity\_witness\_281.py}).}
\end{proof}

This is the precise sense in which finite-sample ML success is not monotone:
the exponentially small error bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-recovery} is
about the eventual rate, not a pointwise monotone curve. It also isolates why
the population statement (Lemma~\ref{lem:gibbs}) and the finite statement have
different shapes: the population maximizer is fixed, whereas a finite
maximizer is a random variable whose law can move non-monotonically with the
sample size. The affinity here, $(1+\sqrt2)/\sqrt6 \approx 0.9856$, is close
to $1$, so the exponential bound is weak at small $n$ --- another reason the
finite curve is not the population one.
