\section{Finite results: rigidity, reweighting, and the source predicate}
\label{sec:finite}

We first prove rigidity of the truth's complete window-spectrum
circulation under the placement-based bridging hypothesis. The
same-length rotation consequence also requires that the truth and
competitors be genuine candidates with the observed full-overlap support.
We then give strict finite-sample counterexamples showing three distinct
ways the conclusion can fail: dropping truth candidacy, allowing different
candidate lengths, or changing to reverse-complement molecule types.
The last failures persist under the source's matched-string bridging
predicate (Section~\ref{sec:finite:historical}). Strict likelihood
inequalities are unaffected by equivalence or tie-breaking conventions.

\subsection{Same-length oriented circulation rigidity}

This subsection states a \emph{project-level} circulation rigidity theorem.
It is not a restatement of the 2016 Not-So-Greedy result: the 2016 theorem
concerns a graph built from the \emph{observed reads} with edge-multiplicity
modification and its unique Eulerian spelling, whereas the theorem below
concerns the graph $X_S$ built from the \emph{complete} length-$L$ spectrum of
the truth and asserts uniqueness of the positive circulation (Remark~\ref{rem:2016-vs-rigidity}).
The two results share the bridging hypothesis but differ in the graph, the
candidate universe, and the conclusion.

We work under the strict oriented single-strand convention: read types are the
oriented length-$L$ windows of $S$ with no reverse-complement collapse, and a
same-length MB09 spelled candidate is a circular word $D$ of length
$G=|S|$ with $\supp\spec_L(D)=\supp\spec_L(S)$. These restrictions ---
oriented reads, equal candidate length, and the full-overlap support proxy ---
are \projchoice{} additions of this paper; they are not supplied by the
published sentence, and the counterexamples of
Section~\ref{sec:finite:length} show why equal length is essential;
$AABB\to ABAB$ independently demonstrates the need for truth candidacy.

\begin{definition}[Window-support graph]
\label{def:graph}
Given a circular word $S$ and read length $L$, the graph $X_S$ has one node for
each distinct length-$(L-1)$ window of $S$, and one directed edge for each
distinct length-$L$ window $w\in\supp\spec_L(S)$, running from
$\operatorname{prefix}_{L-1}(w)$ to $\operatorname{suffix}_{L-1}(w)$ and carrying
multiplicity $A(w)=\spec_L(S)(w)$. A \emph{positive circulation of total $G$} is
a map $f:\supp\spec_L(S)\to\ints_{>0}$ balanced at every node with
$\sum_w f(w)=G$.
\end{definition}

The truth's spectrum $A$ is one such circulation, and the graph is strongly
connected because its edges are the edges of the single closed walk
$t\mapsto W_t(S)$.

\begin{lemma}[Same-length spelled candidates are positive circulations]
\label{lem:reduction}
Same-length MB09 spelled candidates with observed support $V$ are exactly
the positive circulations of total $G$ on $X_S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
A circular word $D$ of length $G$ has a balanced positive spectrum of total $G$;
if $\supp\spec_L(D)=V$ it is such a circulation. Conversely a positive
circulation on a strongly connected balanced multigraph makes it Eulerian, so an
Eulerian circuit spells a circular word $D$ of length $G$ with
$\spec_L(D)=f$. The rigidity theorem below proves uniqueness on the larger
circulation set, so it covers the spelled-circuit subclass a fortiori.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Short-repeat rigidity]
\label{thm:short-repeat}
If every length-$(L-1)$ window of $S$ occurs at most twice, then
$A=\spec_L(S)$ is the unique positive circulation of total $G$ on $X_S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $B$ be a positive circulation of total $G$ and $\delta=B-A$, a circulation
with $\sum_w\delta(w)=0$. Since $A(w)\le$ (occurrences of
$\operatorname{prefix}_{L-1}(w))\le 2$ and $B(w)\ge 1$, we have
$\delta(w)\ge-1$ on every edge.

Suppose $\delta\ne 0$. Then $N=\{w:\delta(w)<0\}$ is nonempty, and on $N$ we
have $\delta(w)=-1$, $A(w)=2$, $B(w)=1$. Take $w\in N$ with tail $v$. As
$A(w)=2$ and $\mathrm{out}_A(v)\le 2$, $w$ is the only outgoing edge at $v$, so
$\mathrm{out}_\delta(v)=-1$ and balance gives $\mathrm{in}_\delta(v)=-1$. Each
incoming edge has $\delta\ge-1$; if $v$ had two incoming edges they would each
have $A=1$ and hence $\delta\ge0$, contradicting $\mathrm{in}_\delta(v)=-1$. So
$v$ has a single incoming edge $e$ with $A(e)=2$, $\delta(e)=-1$, i.e.\ $e\in N$.
The symmetric argument at the head of $w$ shows every edge incident to a node
touched by $N$ lies in $N$; thus $N$ is a union of connected components of $X_S$
with no edge to $V\setminus N$. Strong connectivity forces $N=\emptyset$ or
$N$ to contain all edges; the latter gives $\sum_w\delta(w)=-|E|<0$, contradicting
$\sum_w\delta(w)=0$. Hence $\delta\ge0$, and a nonnegative circulation with
$\sum_w\delta(w)=0$ is zero.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:short-repeat} is exactly what the bridging
condition supplies. We record the two extension lemmas that transfer bridging
into short-repeat structure.

\begin{lemma}[Primitive extension]
\label{lem:primitive-extension}
Let $S$ be primitive and suppose some length-$(L-1)$ window occurs at least three
times. Then $S$ has a triple repeat of length at least $L-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Choose three starts carrying the same length-$(L-1)$ window and extend the three
copies in both directions as long as all three remain equal. The process cannot
reach length $G$, since three equal length-$G$ windows would make $S$ invariant
under a nonzero shift, contradicting primitivity. At termination the preceding
symbols are not all equal and the following symbols are not all equal, which is
exactly the three-copy maximality condition; the length is at least $L-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Periodic extension]
\label{lem:periodic-extension}
Let $S=P^k$ with minimal period $p<G$ and suppose some factor of $P$ of length at
least $L-1$ occurs at least twice in the circular word $P$. Then $S$ has a triple
repeat of length at least $L-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
A repeated factor of $P$ of length at least $L-1$ occurs at least four times in
$S$. Extending its two occurrence families maximally in both directions is
uniform by $p$-periodicity and cannot reach length $G$, because that would make
$r_2-r_1$ a period of $S$ and hence a smaller period of $P$. At termination the
three-copy maximality condition holds for any three occurrences meeting both
families.
\end{proof}

A length-$L$ read bridges a copy of a repeat of length $\ell$ only if
$\ell\le L-2$. Hence $R\in\Is$ forbids every triple repeat of length
$\ge L-1$. Combining this with the extension lemmas yields the main theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Rigidity under $\Is$]
\label{thm:rigidity}
If $S$ admits a read realization $R\in\Is$, then $A=\spec_L(S)$ is the unique
positive circulation of total $G$ on $X_S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $S$ is primitive: $R\in\Is$ gives no triple repeat of length $\ge L-1$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive-extension} no length-$(L-1)$ window occurs three times;
Theorem~\ref{thm:short-repeat} applies. If $S=P^k$ with minimal period
$p<G$: by Lemma~\ref{lem:periodic-extension} every factor of $P$ of length
$\ge L-1$ occurs at most once, so the $p$ length-$L$ and length-$(L-1)$ windows
of $P$ are pairwise distinct and $X_S$ is a single directed $p$-cycle with
$A(w)=k$ on every edge. A positive circulation on a directed cycle is constant
by balance, and total $G=kp$ forces the value $k$, so $B=A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No strict same-length MB09 counterexample]
\label{cor:same-length-zero}
Suppose $R\in\Is$ and the truth is MB09-admissible, so
$\supp x=\supp\spec_L(S)$. Then every same-length MB09 spelled candidate
$D$ satisfies $\spec_L(D)=\spec_L(S)$, and for every objective that is a function
of $(\spec_L(D),x)$,
\[
  \frac{\Lik(D;x)}{\Lik(S;x)}=1 .
\]
There is no strict same-length counterexample for any $G$, $L$, or alphabet.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} the only positive circulation of total $G$ on
$X_S$ is $A$, so every same-length spelled candidate has spectrum $A$. Both
objectives depend on the candidate only through its spectrum, and the
observation is common, so the scores coincide.
\end{proof}

Only the triple-repeat clause of $\Is$ feeds the spectrum-rigidity and
no-strict-counterexample conclusions; the interleaved-repeat and coverage clauses
are not needed there. Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-rotation} below does use the
interleaved clause, since it invokes the complete-spectrum uniqueness input of
Section~\ref{sec:population}, whose hypothesis is Ukkonen's full condition.

\subsubsection{The maximizer theorem on this slice}

The rigidity theorem proves spectrum equality, which forces a tie for
every spectrum-based objective. The maximizer theorem below explicitly
\emph{assumes} MB09 candidacy of the truth and competitor:
it does not derive truth candidacy from bridging.

\begin{theorem}[Same-length maximizer, oriented read types, full $\Is$]
\label{thm:maximizer}
Let $S$ be a circular word of length $G$, and let the realized read placement
set $R$ (the set of starts at which reads were actually drawn) be information
feasible, $R\in\Is$. Suppose the truth is a genuine MB09 candidate for the
observed read set, and let $D$ be any same-length genuine MB09 candidate for the
same observation. Then, for both the exact
Medvedev--Brudno multinomial and the §6.1 binomial approximation at $N=G$,
\[
  \Lik(D;x) \le \Lik(S;x).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The exact realized-start feasibility condition forbids
length-$(L-1)$ triple repeats, so Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}
identifies the complete spectrum of $S$ as the only positive
circulation of total $G$ on its full-overlap support graph.
Genuine candidacy of both $S$ and $D$ supplies the shared support,
and equal length gives the same total $G$; hence
$\spec_L(D)=\spec_L(S)$. Both objective functions depend on the
candidate through these multiplicities, so their scores tie.
The statement makes no claim about candidates lacking the
same support.
\end{proof}

The hypotheses deliberately go beyond the historical source: $2 \le L \le G$,
the project's sampled-placement $\Is$, oriented reads, equal-length genomes,
and genuine \S6.2 spelled candidacy of both truth and competitor. This theorem
does not claim that the historical matching-string predicate
$\Is^{\mathrm{match}}$ implies these project-specific assumptions. There is no long-triple-repeat premise, no primitivity or period
premise, and no ``escape'' or culprit hypothesis; $\Is \Rightarrow \neg
\textsc{LongTripleRepeat}$ is a consequence proved inside the chain. The start
set is the \emph{range} of the realization, not a superset: because every clause
of $\Is$ is a ``some $r \in R$ does \dots'' or a coverage condition, a start set
$R \supseteq \range\rho$ that contains starts at which no read was drawn is a
strictly weaker hypothesis, and the theorem is stated using only the actually realized starts.
For genuine same-length candidates with truth candidacy, cyclic-rotation
uniqueness is proved in the Lean kernel, without an external
BBT obstruction premise (Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-rotation}).

\subsubsection{The MB09 acceptance boundary is a separate question}

Theorem~\ref{thm:maximizer} requires \emph{both} the truth and the competitor to
be genuine MB09 candidates. Dropping the conjunct on the truth is a different
statement, and it is false at equal length: with $S=AABB$ ($G=4$, $L=2$) and the
realized reads $AB,BA$ at starts $1,3$, full $\Is$ holds, while the same-length
genome $ABAB$ is a literal MB09 \texttt{SpelledFeasible62} candidate whose exact
likelihood is $1/2$ against the truth's $1/8$ (both include the
multinomial coefficient $\binom{2}{1,1}=2$). So the MB09 candidate
restriction does not rescue the maximum-likelihood claim unless the truth is
itself admissible, which is precisely what distinguishes the molecule-class
witnesses below from this positive row.

\kernelcheck{}

\begin{corollary}[Uniqueness up to rotation on the truth-admissible same-length row]
\label{cor:same-length-rotation}
Under the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-zero}, every same-length
MB09 spelled candidate $D$ is a cyclic rotation of $S$. Consequently the
truth is not merely a maximizer on this row: it is the unique maximizer up to
cyclic rotation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The bridging condition excludes triple repeats of length at least $L-1$
and interleaved repeat pairs violating Ukkonen's condition at $K=L-1$.
By Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-zero}, every candidate has the
truth's complete length-$L$ spectrum.

For a primitive truth, complete-spectrum uniqueness follows from
the internally verified primitive-P2 support-descent argument.
For a periodic truth $S=P^k$ with minimal period $p<G$, the
periodic-extension argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} shows
that its support graph is a single directed $p$-cycle; traversal
spells only rotations of $S$. Thus in either case every candidate
with the same complete spectrum is a rotation of $S$.
The classical Theorem~\ref{thm:BBT} records the corresponding
complete-spectrum principle, but the Lean endpoint in
\texttt{AssemblyP1.SameLength62Uniqueness} does not postulate
it as an unproved axiom.
\end{proof}

\kernelcheck{}

Equal length is also an independent, essential restriction; see
Section~\ref{sec:finite:length}.

\begin{proposition}[Beatability criterion]
\label{prop:beatability}
Fix $S,L$ and an observation $x$ with $\supp x=\supp\spec_L(S)$, and write
$A=\spec_L(S)$. If $A$ is the unique positive circulation of total $G$ on
$X_S$, the truth ties every same-length spelled candidate. If $B\ne A$ is
another positive circulation of total $G$, choose $w_0$ with $B(w_0)>A(w_0)$ and
put $x_M=x+M e_{w_0}$. Then the \emph{exact multinomial} likelihood ratio is
$\mathrm{ratio}_0\cdot(B(w_0)/A(w_0))^M$, which exceeds $1$ for
all sufficiently large $M$. In the fixed-$N$ binomial surrogate the
multiplier is instead
\[
Q_{w_0}=\frac{B(w_0)}{A(w_0)}
\prod_{v\ne w_0}\frac{N-B(v)}{N-A(v)}.
\]
Its amplification requires $Q_{w_0}>1$ and a valid binomial domain;
the exact multiplier alone does not prove the binomial conclusion.
\end{proposition}

Thus non-rigidity allows exact same-length likelihood reversals for
some observations. Under $\Is$, the stated support and candidacy
hypotheses rule this mechanism out. The minimum
same-length non-rigid pair, found by exact search, is $S=AAAAB$,
$D=AABAB$ ($G=5$, $L=2$); its truth carries a long triple repeat and is
therefore not $\Is$-admissible. Skirting the triple-repeat clause is precisely
what permits the multiplicity reallocation.
\kernelcheck{}

\subsection{Negative results transfer; positive results do not}

\begin{lemma}[Negative transfer]
\label{lem:negative-transfer}
Let $\Lik(\cdot;x)$ be an objective defined on a candidate class $\cands$, let
$\cands'\subseteq\cands$, and let $S,D\in\cands'$. If
$\Lik(D;x) > \Lik(S;x)$ then $S$ is not a maximizer of $\Lik(\cdot;x)$ over
$\cands$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$D\in\cands$ is a candidate with strictly larger value than $S$, so the maximum
over $\cands$ exceeds $\Lik(S;x)$.
\end{proof}

The converse fails: a positive result over a restricted class does not imply the
same over a superclass, because the superclass may contain a better candidate.
This asymmetry is why a same-length \emph{negative} witness that is
candidate-set closed settles the unrestricted question negatively, while a
same-length \emph{positive} theorem is a restricted result unless the restriction
is itself source-justified.

\subsection{Strict finite counterexamples: empirical read reweighting}

The rigidity theorem is a positive same-length statement. Everything else in
this subsection is negative, and each witness is strict, hence robust to the
unresolved equivalence and tie conventions.

The mechanism is the same in every witness. The exact objective scores the
\emph{observed} read-type frequencies: a candidate is rewarded for assigning
high probability to the types that were sampled often. A competitor can
therefore improve its score by reallocating multiplicity toward
over-represented observed types --- weighting the spectrum toward the types
the sample happened to favor --- while the bridging constraint on the
realized reads is still satisfied. This is ordinary finite-sample fluctuation,
not a paradox: the sample is unrepresentative, and likelihood ranks candidates
by how well they fit the sample, not the population.

\begin{theorem}[Exact-multinomial counterexample over all circular candidates]
\label{thm:cex-exact}
Let $\Alphabet=\{A,B\}$, $L=3$, and let the truth be
\[
  S=AAABB \quad (G=5),
\]
with realized starts $0,1,4$, so the observed reads are $\{AAA,AAB,BAA\}$, each
once. Let
\[
  D=AAAAB \quad (|D|=5).
\]
Then $R\in\Is$ holds non-vacuously, and
\[
  \frac{\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(D;x)}{\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(S;x)} = 2 > 1 .
\]
Consequently the truth is not a maximizer for the exact multinomial over all
circular candidates, so both the maximizer and uniqueness schemas are false for
this objective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The spectra on the observed types are $\spec_3(S)=(AAA:1,AAB:1,BAA:1)$ and
$\spec_3(D)=(AAA:2,AAB:1,BAA:1)$; $\spec_3$ has total $5$ for both. The
multinomial coefficient $\binom{3}{1,1,1}=6$ is common, and all candidates have
length $5$, so
\[
  \Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(S;x)=6\cdot(1/5)^3=\frac{6}{125},\qquad
  \Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(D;x)=6\cdot(2/5)(1/5)(1/5)=\frac{12}{125}.
\]
For $\Is$: the starts cover all five positions; the three $A$'s at positions
$0,1,2$ form a maximal length-$1$ triple repeat, bridged respectively by the
reads at starts $4,0,1$; and no two maximal repeat pairs have four alternating
starts, so the interleaved clause is vacuous. The all-bridged triple-repeat
clause is therefore genuinely exercised. The transfers to all circular
candidates by Lemma~\ref{lem:negative-transfer} because the witness is
same-length. \kernelcheck{}
\end{proof}

Repeating the start-$0$ read $k$ times gives the observed multiset
$\{AAA^k,AAB,BAA\}$ and ratio $2^k$, so the failure is a one-parameter family,
not a coincidental tie. A second, independent same-length instance with a
larger separation is $S=AAABCBC$, $D=AAAAABC$ ($G=7$, $L=3$), realized starts
$(0,0,0,1,2,5,6)$ and observed $x=\{AAA:3,AAB:1,ABC:1,BCA:1,CAA:1\}$, for which
$\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(D;x)/\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(S;x)=27$ and $\Is$ holds
non-vacuously (coverage, the all-bridged maximal length-$1$ triple repeat at
$(0,1,2)$, and no interleaved pair).


\begin{theorem}[Fixed-length binomial counterexample]
\label{thm:cex-binomial}
Let $\Alphabet=\{A,C\}$ inside the four-letter DNA alphabet, $L=3$, truth
$S=AAACC$ with realized starts $0,1,4$ (observed reads $AAA,AAC,CAA$), and
competitor $D=AAAAC$, both of length $5$. Under the literal Section~6.1 product
of binomial marginals with $N=5$, the zero-count factors retained,
\[
  \frac{\Lik_{\mathrm{bin}}(D;x)}{\Lik_{\mathrm{bin}}(S;x)}
  = \frac{1125}{512} > 1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The instance is Theorem~\ref{thm:cex-exact} with $B$ relabeled to $C$, so the
same $\Is$ certificate transfers. The spectra are $S=(AAA:1,AAC:1,CAA:1)$ and
$D=(AAA:2,AAC:1,CAA:1)$ on the observed types. Carrying the factors for the
zero-multiplicity types changes the ratio from $2$ to $1125/512$:
\[
  \Lik_{\mathrm{bin}}(S;x)=\frac{452984832}{30517578125},\qquad
  \Lik_{\mathrm{bin}}(D;x)=\frac{7962624}{244140625}.
\]
\kernelcheck{}
\end{proof}

\subsection{The candidate-length boundary}
\label{sec:finite:length}

The same-length restriction in the positive theorem is not cosmetic. On the next
slice, where $|D|>G$ is allowed, the truth need not be optimal even under $\Is$,
and this is an infinite-family phenomenon rather than a bounded search.

\begin{theorem}[Oriented variable-length MB09 classification]
\label{thm:cex-variable-length}
Let $\Alphabet=\{A,T\}$, $L=3$, truth $S=AAATT$ ($G=5$), realized starts
$R=\{0,1,2,3,4\}$, so $R\in\Is$ holds at full strength (coverage, the unique
maximal length-$1$ triple repeat all-bridged, the interleaved clause vacuous).
Write $x^{(M)}=\spec_3(S)+M e_{AAA}$ ($n=5+M$). For the growing competitor
$D_M=A^{3+M}TT$ ($|D_M|=5+M$),
\[
  \frac{\Lik_E(D_M;x^{(M)})}{\Lik_E(S;x^{(M)})}
    = \Bigl(\tfrac{5}{5+M}\Bigr)^{5+M}(1+M)^{1+M} > 1
  \qquad\text{for every } M\ge 1 ;
\]
for the fixed competitor $D=AAAATT$ ($|D|=6$) the exact ratio is
$(\tfrac{3125}{3888})(\tfrac53)^M>1$ and the fixed-$N$ binomial ratio
($N=5$) is $(\tfrac{81}{128})2^M>1$, for every $M\ge 1$. Every candidate is a
genuine MB09 candidate under the per-vertex, spelled-support and
single-circuit readings. The truth wins exactly at $n=G$; a single extra
observation of the over-represented $AAA$ type overturns it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
For the exact objective the observation-only coefficient is common, so the
competitor with $d_{D_M}=x^{(M)}$ and $|D_M|=n$ attains the AM--GM upper bound
$\Lik^*(x^{(M)})=\binom{n}{x}\prod_w(x_w/n)^{x_w}$, while the truth's normalized
spectrum differs at the $AAA$ coordinate for every $M\ge1$. The fixed-competitor
families are the amplification lemma made concrete: each additional observation
of an observed type $w$ multiplies the exact odds by
$p_D(w)/p_S(w)$ and the fixed-$N$ binomial odds by
$Q_w=(p_D(w)/p_S(w))\prod_{i\ne w}(1-p_D(i))/(1-p_S(i))$; here
$p_D(AAA)/p_S(AAA)=5/3>1$ and $Q_{AAA}=2>1$.
\kernelcheck{} at $M=1,2$; general families \mathfact{} (Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unrestricted finite-sample ML failure for a fixed primitive genome]
\label{thm:aaatt-unrestricted}
Fix the primitive circular truth $S=\texttt{AAATT}$ and oriented
length-three reads. For any sample $r$ of $n\ge1$ independent
uniform-start reads from $S$, let $a$ be the number of observations
equal to $\texttt{AAA}$. If $5a\ne n$, a (possibly longer) circular
genome $D$ has \emph{strictly greater} exact likelihood than $S$.
Consequently, when ML compares \emph{all} circular genomes of
arbitrary positive length, with no length penalty,
\[
 \Pr_S\{S\text{ is a maximum-likelihood genome at sample size }n\}
 \le
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle {n\choose n/5}(1/5)^{n/5}(4/5)^{4n/5},&5\mid n,\\
 0,&5\nmid n.
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular, this probability is $O(n^{-1/2})$ and tends to zero,
although the probability of observing every truth read type and
satisfying historical coverage and bridging tends to one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For integers $j\ge0$ and $k\ge1$, the circular genome
\[
 D_{j,k}=\texttt{A}^{j+2}\texttt{TT}\,
                (\texttt{AATT})^{k-1}
\]
has $j$ copies of the read $\texttt{AAA}$ and $k$ copies of
\emph{each} of the other four reads of $S$, with total length
$j+4k$. Writing $t=j/(j+4k)$, its read distribution is
$(t,(1-t)/4,(1-t)/4,(1-t)/4,(1-t)/4)$.
Up to a term common to all candidates, the log likelihood
of the observed sample is therefore
\[
 f(t)=a\log t+(n-a)\log((1-t)/4).
\]
For $0<a<n$ this strictly concave function is uniquely maximized
at $t=a/n$, whereas the truth has $t=1/5$. The maximizing law
is \emph{realized by a finite circular genome}: take
$j=4a$ and $k=n-a$. Thus $5a\ne n$ gives a strict
competitor. When $a=0$, $j=0$ gives a strict improvement;
when $a=n$, take $j=4n$ and $k=1$.
Finally $a\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,1/5)$, giving the stated
upper bound and its $n^{-1/2}$ order by Stirling's formula.
With fixed $S$ each of the five reads has probability $1/5$,
so complete read support, and hence its historical matched-string
coverage and bridging certificate, occurs with probability tending
to one.
\end{proof}

This continuous-parameter optimization argument is paper-level,
not an additional Lean theorem.}

\begin{theorem}[Branching support prevents unrestricted finite-read ML recovery]
\label{thm:unrestricted-branching-rate}
Let $S$ be a fixed circular genome, $L\ge2$, and let $E$ be the
distinct oriented length-$L$ reads in its complete spectrum.
Let $V$ be the corresponding length-$(L-1)$ overlap vertices.
Put $d=|E|-|V|$. If $d>0$, then for $n$ independent
uniform-start reads from $S$,
\[
  \Pr_S\{S\text{ maximizes unrestricted exact likelihood}\}
      = O_{S,L}(n^{-d/2}).
\]
The condition $d>0$ is equivalent to branching of the connected
read-support graph. Thus for any fixed branching truth, the
probability that the truth is an unrestricted exact-ML maximizer
tends to zero, even though its read distribution is learned
arbitrarily accurately. The implicit constant depends on the
fixed truth and read length.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A circular genome gives a strictly positive, balanced
normalized read-count circulation $p$ on its support graph:
the outgoing and incoming masses at every vertex agree and
$\sum_{e\in E}p_e=1$. The incidence matrix of the strongly
connected support graph has rank $|V|-1$. Hence the real
vector space
\[
 H=\left\{h\in\mathbb R^E:
   \operatorname{div}h=0,\ \sum_e h_e=0\right\}
\]
has dimension $d=|E|-|V|$ and admits a basis of integer vectors
$h^1,\ldots,h^d$.

For any $h\in H$ and sufficiently small rational $\varepsilon$,
$q=p+\varepsilon h$ is a positive rational balanced circulation
of mass one. Clear its denominators to obtain positive integer
edge multiplicities. Strong connectivity and Euler's theorem give
a single circular spelling with this complete read spectrum;
scaling all multiplicities if necessary makes its length at least
$L$. Therefore \emph{every such rational perturbation} occurs
as the read law of a genuine circular candidate. If $S$ maximizes
the observed exact likelihood over all genome lengths, its
positive probability vector $p$ must be stationary under both
signs of each of these perturbations:
\[
 \sum_{e\in E} X_e\,\frac{h^j_e}{p_e}=0,
       \qquad j=1,\ldots,d,
\]
where $X_e$ is the observed count of read type $e$.
The multinomial coefficient is common to all candidates.

For a sampled read $R$, let
$Y(R)=(h^1_R/p_R,\ldots,h^d_R/p_R)$.
It is a finitely supported rational random vector with mean
zero. Its covariance is nonsingular: if a linear combination
of the coordinates of $Y$ were constant, the corresponding
$h\in H$ would satisfy $h_e=c p_e$ on every support edge;
summing gives $c=0$, hence $h=0$. After multiplying by a
common denominator we may regard $Y$ as integer-valued.
The stationarity event is exactly
$\sum_{i=1}^nY(R_i)=0$.

For completeness, the usual lattice anti-concentration bound
gives $\sup_z\Pr(\sum_{i=1}^nY(R_i)=z)=O(n^{-d/2})$
for any finitely supported integer random vector with
nonsingular covariance. By Fourier inversion this probability
is bounded by a constant times
$\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^d}|\mathbb E e^{it\cdot Y}|^n\,dt$.
The modulus is strictly below one away from finitely many
points of the torus, and decays quadratically near each such
point because the covariance is nonsingular; each
neighbourhood contributes $O(n^{-d/2})$. Applying the bound
to the necessary stationarity event proves the result.
Finally, for connected balanced support, $|E|>|V|$ exactly
when some vertex has at least two outgoing edge types.
\end{proof}

The conclusion is for each \emph{fixed} genome and read length,
with an unrestricted candidate-length universe. It does not
provide a rate uniform over growing genomes, nor does it
settle the high-probability ML-versus-bridging threshold for
a growing sequence of genomes in
Section~\ref{sec:open-questions-final}. It also does not apply
as stated when candidates are restricted to the finite
primitive-P2 class, where Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-uniform-rate}
gives an opposite asymptotic conclusion.
\statusline{\mathfact{} by the circulation and lattice
anti-concentration argument above. The theorem is not
independently Lean-formalized; it applies to genuine
circular spellings, not arbitrary unspellable flows.}




\subsection{A flow characterization of unrestricted likelihood}
\label{sec:finite:flow-supremum}

Over unrestricted genome lengths, maximum likelihood is best
understood through the compact space of balanced read laws,
rather than by presupposing that an optimal \emph{genome} exists.

\begin{theorem}[The unrestricted supremum and its attainment]
\label{thm:flow-supremum-attainment}
Fix a finite alphabet $\Alphabet$ of size at least two,
an oriented read length $L\ge2$, and a nonempty sample
with read counts $x_e$, $e\in E=\Alphabet^L$.
Let $V=\Alphabet^{L-1}$ and define the compact
normalized circulation polytope
\[
 \mathcal P_L=\left\{p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^E:
    \sum_e p_e=1,\quad
    \sum_{e:\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}p_e
    =\sum_{e:\operatorname{head}(e)=v}p_e
    \text{ for every }v\in V
 \right\}.
\]
Set $F_x(p)=\sum_{e:x_e>0}x_e\log p_e$,
taking the value $-\infty$ if any observed coordinate
has zero mass. Then
\[
 \sup_{\text{circular genomes }D}\log\mathcal L(D;x)
   = C_x+\max_{p\in\mathcal P_L}F_x(p),
\]
where $C_x$ is independent of $D$ and accounts
for the multinomial coefficient (zero for ordered reads).
The maximum on the right is always attained and finite.

There is a finite circular genome attaining the likelihood
supremum \emph{if and only if} at least one maximizing
circulation $p^*$ is \emph{rational} and its positive
edge-support graph is connected on nonisolated vertices.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every finite circular genome induces a unit-mass,
rational balanced circulation whose positive support
is connected, since its consecutive windows traverse
a closed walk. This establishes the upper bound
and necessity.

Conversely, multiply a rational optimizer with connected
positive support by a common positive denominator to
obtain integer positive edge multiplicities. Its support
is directed Eulerian: each vertex has equal in-degree
and out-degree, and the nonisolated support is connected.
An Euler circuit spells a single circular word realizing
those counts. If the circuit has fewer than $L$ edges,
repeat it enough times; the resulting genome realizes
the same normalized read law. Thus the supremum is
attained whenever a maximizing rational, connected
circulation exists.

To prove equality of suprema even without such an optimizer,
take any $p\in\mathcal P_L$ with finite score.
A balanced nonnegative circulation is a nonnegative
linear combination of directed cycle flows. Every such
cycle is integral; approximating its coefficients by
positive rational numbers yields rational balanced
unit-mass circulations converging to $p$.
Mix each approximation with a vanishing rational
positive uniform read law on all of $E$.
The result is rational, balanced, and has full
strongly connected de Bruijn support, so a single
circular genome realizes it. Its log likelihood
converges to $F_x(p)$ because all observed coordinates
of $p$ are strictly positive. Finally $\mathcal P_L$
is compact and $F_x$ is upper semicontinuous, while
the uniform law has finite score; hence a finite
maximizing flow exists.
\end{proof}

The criterion separates three phenomena: the objective
always has an optimal \emph{real} balanced read law;
its maximizing law may contain irrational frequencies;
and an optimizer with disconnected positive support
may not be spellable by a single circle. An irrational
or disconnected optimizer does not, by itself, prove
non-attainment if \emph{another} maximizing flow is
rational and connected. The theorem therefore uses
the existence of at least one suitable optimizer,
not a condition on an arbitrarily chosen one.
The proof is mathematical rather than Lean-formalized.



\begin{theorem}[An exact finite-sample unrestricted-ML certificate]
\label{thm:unrestricted-kkt-certificate}
Let $S$ be \emph{any} finite circular genome,
let $L\ge2$, and let $x=(x_e)_{e\in E}$
be a nonempty oriented read sample on the
complete de Bruijn graph $E=\Alphabet^L$,
$V=\Alphabet^{L-1}$. Put $p=p_S$,
$n=\sum_e x_e$.
Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The truth $S$ is an exact maximum-likelihood
genome among \emph{all} finite circular genomes
of arbitrary length.
\item Every observed edge has positive truth
probability ($x_e>0\Rightarrow p_e>0$), and
there are vertex potentials $\phi_v\in\mathbb Q$
satisfying the finite system
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \displaystyle
 \phi_{\operatorname{head}(e)}
       -\phi_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}
       = n-\frac{x_e}{p_e},
   &p_e>0,\\[0.8ex]
 \displaystyle
 \phi_{\operatorname{head}(e)}
       -\phi_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}\le n,
   &p_e=0.
 \end{array}
\]
\end{enumerate}
Thus exact unrestricted maximality of a \emph{specified}
candidate genome can be decided by rational
difference-constraint feasibility. On the explicit
de Bruijn graph this requires $O(|V||E|)$
rational relaxation operations after the read counts
are computed; no search over candidate genomes
or genome-length bound is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If an observed read has $p_e=0$, the truth has
zero likelihood, while a circular genome obtained
by concatenating all observed read strings has
positive likelihood. Hence maximality requires
positive truth probability for every observed read.

Assume this holds. Theorem~
\ref{thm:flow-supremum-attainment} and density of
finite genome laws show that $S$ is an unrestricted
maximizer exactly when its read law $p$ maximizes
$F_x$ over the compact balanced-flow polytope.
Since the uniform positive read law is feasible,
the usual constraint qualification holds. The
Karush--Kuhn--Tucker optimality conditions
for the concave function $F_x$ are therefore
necessary and sufficient.

Write the Lagrange multiplier of the unit-mass
constraint as $\lambda$ and that of flow balance
as $\phi_v$. The stationary-coordinate equality on
every $p_e>0$ and the boundary inequality on
$p_e=0$ read
\[
  \frac{x_e}{p_e}
    =\lambda+\phi_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}
                     -\phi_{\mathrm{head}(e)}
       \quad(p_e>0),
 \qquad
 0\le\lambda+\phi_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}
                     -\phi_{\mathrm{head}(e)}
       \quad(p_e=0).
\]
Multiplying the first equation by $p_e$ and
summing over edges cancels the potential terms
by balance, yielding $\lambda=n$. This is
precisely the displayed difference-constraint
system.

All its constants are rational because $p$ is
a finite genome law and $x$ is integral.
Each equality is represented by two opposite
difference inequalities and each absent-edge
condition by one difference inequality.
A feasible system of rational difference
constraints has a rational solution (for example
the shortest-path potentials from a new zero-cost
source in the associated weighted graph).
Bellman--Ford detects negative cycles and
constructs a solution using $O(|V||E|)$
rational additions and comparisons.

Sufficiency also follows directly: the displayed
constraints provide an upper bound
$x_e/p_e\le n+\phi_{\rm tail(e)}-\phi_{\rm head(e)}$
for every edge, with equality where $p_e>0$.
For every balanced $q$, concavity gives
\[
 F_x(q)-F_x(p)
 \le \sum_{e:x_e>0}\frac{x_e}{p_e}(q_e-p_e)
 \le \sum_e(n+\phi_{\rm tail(e)}
                 -\phi_{\rm head(e)})(q_e-p_e)=0.
\]
Thus no circular genome has higher likelihood.
\end{proof}

This is a truth-dependent \emph{decision criterion}
for finite samples, not an algorithm for identifying
the unknown truth from reads alone. Its cost is
polynomial in the explicitly represented $q^L$
read-type graph, which is generally exponential
in the read length $L$. The theorem does not
require a maximum to exist \emph{for arbitrary
samples}; it decides whether the specified
genome itself attains one.
The rational certificate is paper-level and is
independently tested by
\path{scripts/verify_unrestricted_KKT_potentials.py};
it is not a new Lean theorem.

\begin{proposition}[A complete criterion for balanced samples]
\label{prop:balanced-sample-attainment}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:flow-supremum-attainment}, suppose
the \emph{observed count vector itself} is balanced:
for every overlap vertex $v$,
\[
 \sum_{e:\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}x_e
 =\sum_{e:\operatorname{head}(e)=v}x_e.
\]
Then the unique likelihood-maximizing balanced law is
the rational empirical distribution $p^*=x/n$, where
$n=\sum_e x_e>0$. A finite circular maximum-likelihood
genome exists \emph{if and only if} the observed
positive-edge support is connected.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any probability vector $p$ (balanced or not),
Gibbs' inequality gives
\[
 \sum_{e:x_e>0}x_e\log p_e
 \le\sum_{e:x_e>0}x_e\log(x_e/n),
\]
with equality only at $p=x/n$. The equality case
is feasible because $x$ is balanced, and it is
therefore the unique maximizing law on
$\mathcal P_L$. It is rational. Theorem~
\ref{thm:flow-supremum-attainment} now proves
the equivalence with connected positive support.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Non-attainment with a rational optimizer]
\label{ex:unattained-disconnected}
Let $L=3$ and observe just the two oriented DNA
reads $\texttt{AAA}$ and $\texttt{CCC}$, once each.
This sample occurs with positive probability,
for example under the primitive circular truth
$\texttt{AAACCC}$. Its observed count vector is
balanced, but its two loop edges lie in
\emph{disconnected} support components. The unique
optimal real read law is rational:
$p^*_{\texttt{AAA}}=p^*_{\texttt{CCC}}=1/2$.
No finite circular genome has this read law:
a circle containing both types must also contain
transitional read types. Consequently the exact
likelihood has supremum $1/4$ for ordered reads,
but no maximizer. The concrete genomes
$\texttt{A}^m\texttt{C}^m$ ($m\ge3$)
approach it, with likelihood
\[
 \mathcal L(\texttt{A}^m\texttt{C}^m;x)
       =\left(\frac{m-2}{2m}\right)^2
       \longrightarrow\frac14.
\]
(The candidate-independent multinomial coefficient
is $2$ if the two reads are treated as an
unordered multiset.) Thus irrational frequencies
are \emph{not} the only cause of non-attainment.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[The unrestricted likelihood supremum need not be attained]
\label{thm:unattained-ML}
Let the primitive circular truth be $S=\texttt{AAACAC}$, with
oriented read length $L=3$, over the DNA alphabet. Observe every
circular read of $S$ plus one additional $\texttt{AAC}$:
\[
 x_{\texttt{AAA}}=x_{\texttt{CAA}}=x_{\texttt{CAC}}=1,\qquad
 x_{\texttt{AAC}}=x_{\texttt{ACA}}=2.
\]
Over \emph{all} circular genomes of all positive lengths,
the exact multinomial likelihood has a finite supremum
but \emph{no maximizing genome}.
The unique optimal balanced read law on the full de Bruijn
graph, in the order
$(\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AAC},\texttt{ACA},
\texttt{CAA},\texttt{CAC})$, is
\[
 q^*=\left(\frac17,\frac{17-\sqrt{73}}{42},
           \frac{19+\sqrt{73}}{84},
           \frac{17-\sqrt{73}}{42},
           \frac{\sqrt{73}-5}{28}\right),
\]
with zero mass on every other read type.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Any circular genome defines a rational, nonnegative balanced
3-mer distribution of total mass one. The five observed reads
form a connected support graph on vertices
$\texttt{AA},\texttt{AC},\texttt{CA}$, and the displayed
$q^*$ is a positive balanced circulation on this graph.
For every balanced distribution $p$ on the \emph{entire}
four-letter de Bruijn graph, write
$F(p)=\sum_w x_w\log p(w)$, with $F(p)=-\infty$ when
an observed coordinate vanishes.
Define vertex potentials
\[
 \psi_{\texttt{AA}}=0,\quad
 \psi_{\texttt{AC}}=7-\frac2{q^*_{\texttt{AAC}}},\quad
 \psi_{\texttt{CA}}=\frac1{q^*_{\texttt{CAA}}}-7,
 \qquad \psi_u=0\text{ for all remaining vertices}.
\]
Direct algebra with $\sqrt{73}$ verifies that for
each observed edge $e$,
\[
   \frac{x_e}{q^*_e}=7+\psi_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}
                        -\psi_{\operatorname{head}(e)}.
\]
Both nonzero potentials lie strictly between $-3$ and $0$.
Consequently, for every \emph{unobserved} edge $e$,
\[
   0<7+\psi_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}
           -\psi_{\operatorname{head}(e)}.
\]
These inequalities hold for all 64 possible DNA 3-mers,
not merely the truth's support. Concavity and the balance
constraints give, for any feasible $p$,
\[
 F(p)-F(q^*)\le
   \sum_{e:x_e>0}\frac{x_e}{q^*_e}(p_e-q^*_e)
 \le \sum_e (7+\psi_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}
                     -\psi_{\mathrm{head}(e)})(p_e-q^*_e)=0 .
\]
Strict concavity on the observed coordinates and strict
dual slack on the others make $q^*$ the \emph{unique}
optimal balanced law.

Nonetheless, $q^*_{\texttt{AAC}}$ is irrational, while
every finite genome has rational frequencies. To see that
the supremum really is approachable by genomes, choose
positive integers $a,b,c$ with
\[
 (a,b,c)/(a+3b+2c)\longrightarrow
 (q^*_{\texttt{AAA}},q^*_{\texttt{AAC}},
  q^*_{\texttt{CAC}}).
\]
The integer edge counts
$\texttt{AAA}:a$,
$\texttt{AAC}:\!b$,
$\texttt{CAA}:\!b$,
$\texttt{ACA}:\!b+c$,
$\texttt{CAC}:\!c$
are balanced and strongly connected, hence admit a
single circular Eulerian spelling. Their read laws tend
to $q^*$, so the likelihood tends to its supremum.
No finite genome attains that value.
\end{proof}

The truth $S$ satisfies P2: no length-two read occurs
three times, and its long repeat pairs do not interleave.
Thus structural repeat restrictions on the \emph{truth}
alone do not make unrestricted maximum likelihood
well-defined. The statement applies to the exact oriented
read law; it neither asserts that non-attainment occurs
for all samples nor extends automatically to the MB09
surrogate. The displayed dual equations, all 64 strict
inequalities and explicit approximate circular spellings
are independently checked by
\path{scripts/verify_irrational_ml_nonattainment.py}.
This is a paper-level proof, not a Lean theorem.

\begin{remark}[Sampling instability is the mechanism]
\label{rem:amplification}
The variable-length failure is not a coincidence of one word pair. The
amplification lemmas show that whenever a competitor over-weights an observed
type ($p_D(w)>p_S(w)$), repeating that type in the sample drives the odds in the
competitor's favour without bound; symmetrically, a tie ($Q_w=1$) stays a tie
and a multiplier below one drives the odds to zero. The
exact-vs-fixed-$N$ distinction matters: the two multipliers differ because the
fixed-$N$ product moves the $(n-x_i)$ exponents at every other type. The same
word pair also appears as a reverse-complement variable-length witness; here the
orientation is fixed by observing every truth type plus an extra $AAA$, so the
mechanism is observed-multiplicity skew rather than strand collapse.
\end{remark}

\subsection{General certificates for rigidity and finite-count rankings}
\label{sec:finite:certificates}

The bridge-based theorem above gives a strong sufficient condition for
rigidity. A separate, general algebraic theorem describes \emph{exactly}
what rigidity means for any finite support graph, independent of repeats.

\begin{theorem}[Graver certificate for unique positive circulation]
\label{thm:graver-rigidity}
Fix a finite directed support graph, a positive integer balanced
circulation $A$ with total mass $G$, and the lattice of integer
circulations with total sum zero. Let $\mathcal G$ be its Graver basis
(the nonzero conformally indecomposable lattice elements).
Then $A$ is the \emph{unique} positive integer circulation of total
$G$ if and only if
\[
  \forall g\in\mathcal G,\qquad
  \neg\bigl(\forall e,\ g_e\ge 1-A_e\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, none of these elementary perturbations $A+g$
is itself a feasible positive circulation of mass $G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If an elementary feasible perturbation $A+g$ exists, it
is a different positive circulation with total $G$.
Conversely, let $B\ne A$ be another feasible positive
circulation. The integer circulation $B-A$ has sum zero
and admits a sign-conformal decomposition into Graver
elements. Choose a nonzero component $g$ of that
decomposition. On coordinates where $g$ is negative,
$|g_e|\le |B_e-A_e|$, so
$A_e+g_e\ge B_e\ge1$; on nonnegative coordinates,
$A_e+g_e\ge A_e\ge1$. The component remains a
balanced integer circulation of zero total, and therefore
$A+g$ is a feasible distinct positive circulation.
\end{proof}

The sign-conformal decomposition is also verified in Lean;
its mathematical role is explicit in the proof above.


There is a parallel exact criterion on the statistical side for the
fixed-external-$N$ independent-binomial \emph{surrogate}. This criterion
is separate from the exact likelihood of the actual iid read sample.

\begin{theorem}[Effective weights classify all-count surrogate dominance]
\label{thm:binomial-weights}
Fix a finite read-type universe $E$ and external size $N$. For a
candidate spectrum $B$ put
\[
 q_i(B)=B_i\prod_{\substack{j\in E\\j\ne i}}(N-B_j).
\]
After removing factors independent of the candidate, the
product-binomial score for any nonnegative count vector
$x=(x_i)_{i\in E}$ is proportional to
$\prod_{i\in E}q_i(B)^{x_i}$. Furthermore, for any two candidate
spectra $A,B$, candidate $A$ has at least as large a surrogate
score as $B$ for \emph{every} nonnegative count vector $x$ if and
only if
\[
  \forall i\in E,\qquad q_i(B)\le q_i(A).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Rearrange the numerator
$\prod_i B_i^{x_i}(N-B_i)^{n-x_i}$ using
$n=\sum_i x_i$. The factor multiplying each $x_i$ is exactly
$q_i(B)$. Coordinatewise weight domination implies domination
of every product of nonnegative integer powers. Conversely,
take the count vector consisting of one observation of type
$i$ to recover the $i$th inequality.
\end{proof}



These two theorems provide complementary structural and
statistical certificates. Neither replaces the source-faithful
finite-sample investigation, but neither should be dismissed as
a mere proof implementation detail.

\subsection{What a reduced graph preserves: spelling versus flow}
\label{sec:finite:spelling}

The distinction between a graph flow and a spelled genome is itself
mathematically consequential. It is not enough for a reduction rule to
preserve connectivity or to retain edges with longer shared nucleotide
overlaps; a claimed \emph{sequence} equivalence must preserve the sequence
spelled by a path.

\begin{proposition}[A longer-overlap-only reduction can change spelling]
\label{prop:reduction-mismatch}
For reads of length $L=4$ at minimum overlap $1$, take oriented read
strings $u=\texttt{AAAT}$, $v=\texttt{TAAA}$, and
$m=\texttt{ATTA}$. The direct edge $u\to v$ has overlap $1$ and spells
$\texttt{AAATAAA}$. A two-edge detour through $m$ has overlaps $2$ and
$2$ but spells $\texttt{AAATTAAA}$. Thus a
\emph{longer-overlap-only} deletion predicate removes an edge that is
\emph{not} spelling-equivalent to its alleged detour; a
spelling-preserving reduction keeps it.
\end{proposition}



There is also a positive, global result rather than merely this
counterexample.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-walk spelling preservation in the candidate reduction]
\label{thm:global-spelling}
For the repository's explicitly specified
\texttt{transitivelyReducedSpelling} graph of equal-length oriented
overlap strands, every nonempty signed walk in the complete overlap
graph can be expanded to a signed walk of reduced-graph edges with
the same endpoints and the same spelled sequence. The expansion
respects the required opposing incidences at successive signed edges.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each deleted overlap edge is accompanied by a replacement
path with the same endpoints, compatible signed incidences
and exactly the same spelled word. Replace the first such
edge in a signed walk by its path. The reduction is defined
so that this operation strictly decreases the number of
unreduced edges under its well-founded ordering, and hence
terminates. By induction on that measure, the resulting
walk lies entirely in the reduced graph, retains the
original endpoints and signed-incidence compatibility, and
spells the same sequence. This is a claim about the
explicit spelling-preserving reduction, not about a
longer-overlap-only heuristic.
\end{proof}

The combinatorial reason for this requirement is precise: a
length-$L$ read-overlap step with shared overlap $\ell$ advances
$L-\ell$ genomic positions and can skip unvisited internal
length-$L$ windows. On a coherent circular spelling, the count of
each read type equals its visited-vertex count \emph{plus} its
skipped internal-window count. Thus flow counts and complete-genome
spectra need not coincide without an additional proof that the
skipped windows include no observed read type. The general oriented
counting identity and a finite exhaustive regression are recorded in
\texttt{docs/section62-observation7-skipped-windows.md}; its
bidirected source-faithful Lean generalization is \emph{not} claimed
here. This is a substantive boundary of the optimization model, not a
technicality to discard.

\subsection{Scope summary}

The positive theorem requires equal-length oriented candidates with
the truth's complete read support; it is not an unrestricted ML
statement. The exact oriented counterexamples show that dropping
truth candidacy or the length restriction permits strict failures.
The reverse-complement and bidirected-flow variants require
different objectives and feasible sets. A full comparison of those
variants is recorded in Table~\ref{tab:finite} in
Appendix~\ref{app:molecular-flow}.
