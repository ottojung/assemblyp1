\section{Finite results for the literature-derived formulations}
\label{sec:finite}

This section reports what is known about the question when the observation model
is a finite sample and the objective is one of the Medvedev--Brudno objects of
Section~\ref{sec:model}. The results are of three kinds. First a \emph{positive}
rigidity theorem for same-length oriented Section~6.2 candidates under
Shomorony's $\Is$, which makes the truth's spectrum the only positive
circulation of total $G$; the maximizer statement on that slice, and the
uniqueness statement obtained by adding the external complete-spectrum
uniqueness input, come next. Then a sequence of \emph{negative}
witnesses, each refuting a named, precisely scoped statement. Finally, the row
that stays open is identified precisely, together with the two rows that are not
readings of the 2016 sentence at all
(Remark~\ref{rem:surfaces}, Table~\ref{tab:finite}). We begin with the logical
device that makes the negatives robust.

\subsection{Negative results transfer; positive results do not}

\begin{lemma}[Negative transfer]
\label{lem:negative-transfer}
Let $\Lik(\cdot;x)$ be an objective defined on a candidate class $\cands$, let
$\cands'\subseteq\cands$, and let $S,D\in\cands'$. If
$\Lik(D;x) > \Lik(S;x)$ then $S$ is not a maximizer of $\Lik(\cdot;x)$ over
$\cands$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$D\in\cands$ is a candidate with strictly larger value than $S$, so the maximum
over $\cands$ exceeds $\Lik(S;x)$.
\end{proof}

The converse fails: a positive result over a restricted class does not imply the
same over a superclass, because the superclass may contain a better candidate.
This asymmetry is why a same-length \emph{negative} witness that is
candidate-set closed settles the unrestricted question negatively, while a
same-length \emph{positive} theorem is a restricted result unless the restriction
is itself source-justified.

\subsection{A positive same-length oriented rigidity theorem}

We work under the strict oriented single-strand convention: read types are the
oriented length-$L$ windows of $S$ with no reverse-complement collapse, and a
same-length Section~6.2 spelled candidate is a circular word $D$ of length
$G=|S|$ with $\supp\spec_L(D)=\supp\spec_L(S)$.

\begin{definition}[Window-support graph]
\label{def:graph}
Given a circular word $S$ and read length $L$, the graph $X_S$ has one node for
each distinct length-$(L-1)$ window of $S$, and one directed edge for each
distinct length-$L$ window $w\in\supp\spec_L(S)$, running from
$\operatorname{prefix}_{L-1}(w)$ to $\operatorname{suffix}_{L-1}(w)$ and carrying
multiplicity $A(w)=\spec_L(S)(w)$. A \emph{positive circulation of total $G$} is
a map $f:\supp\spec_L(S)\to\ints_{>0}$ balanced at every node with
$\sum_w f(w)=G$.
\end{definition}

The truth's spectrum $A$ is one such circulation, and the graph is strongly
connected because its edges are the edges of the single closed walk
$t\mapsto W_t(S)$.

\begin{lemma}[Same-length spelled candidates are positive circulations]
\label{lem:reduction}
Same-length Section~6.2 spelled candidates with observed support $V$ are exactly
the positive circulations of total $G$ on $X_S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
A circular word $D$ of length $G$ has a balanced positive spectrum of total $G$;
if $\supp\spec_L(D)=V$ it is such a circulation. Conversely a positive
circulation on a strongly connected balanced multigraph makes it Eulerian, so an
Eulerian circuit spells a circular word $D$ of length $G$ with
$\spec_L(D)=f$. The rigidity theorem below proves uniqueness on the larger
circulation set, so it covers the spelled-circuit subclass a fortiori.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Short-repeat rigidity]
\label{thm:short-repeat}
If every length-$(L-1)$ window of $S$ occurs at most twice, then
$A=\spec_L(S)$ is the unique positive circulation of total $G$ on $X_S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $B$ be a positive circulation of total $G$ and $\delta=B-A$, a circulation
with $\sum_w\delta(w)=0$. Since $A(w)\le$ (occurrences of
$\operatorname{prefix}_{L-1}(w))\le 2$ and $B(w)\ge 1$, we have
$\delta(w)\ge-1$ on every edge.

Suppose $\delta\ne 0$. Then $N=\{w:\delta(w)<0\}$ is nonempty, and on $N$ we
have $\delta(w)=-1$, $A(w)=2$, $B(w)=1$. Take $w\in N$ with tail $v$. As
$A(w)=2$ and $\mathrm{out}_A(v)\le 2$, $w$ is the only outgoing edge at $v$, so
$\mathrm{out}_\delta(v)=-1$ and balance gives $\mathrm{in}_\delta(v)=-1$. Each
incoming edge has $\delta\ge-1$; if $v$ had two incoming edges they would each
have $A=1$ and hence $\delta\ge0$, contradicting $\mathrm{in}_\delta(v)=-1$. So
$v$ has a single incoming edge $e$ with $A(e)=2$, $\delta(e)=-1$, i.e.\ $e\in N$.
The symmetric argument at the head of $w$ shows every edge incident to a node
touched by $N$ lies in $N$; thus $N$ is a union of connected components of $X_S$
with no edge to $V\setminus N$. Strong connectivity forces $N=\emptyset$ or
$N=V$; the latter gives $\sum_w\delta(w)=-|V|<0$, contradicting
$\sum_w\delta(w)=0$. Hence $\delta\ge0$, and a nonnegative circulation with
$\sum_w\delta(w)=0$ is zero.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:short-repeat} is exactly what the bridging
condition supplies. We record the two extension lemmas that transfer bridging
into short-repeat structure.

\begin{lemma}[Primitive extension]
\label{lem:primitive-extension}
Let $S$ be primitive and suppose some length-$(L-1)$ window occurs at least three
times. Then $S$ has a triple repeat of length at least $L-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Choose three starts carrying the same length-$(L-1)$ window and extend the three
copies in both directions as long as all three remain equal. The process cannot
reach length $G$, since three equal length-$G$ windows would make $S$ invariant
under a nonzero shift, contradicting primitivity. At termination the preceding
symbols are not all equal and the following symbols are not all equal, which is
exactly the three-copy maximality condition; the length is at least $L-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Periodic extension]
\label{lem:periodic-extension}
Let $S=P^k$ with minimal period $p<G$ and suppose some factor of $P$ of length at
least $L-1$ occurs at least twice in the circular word $P$. Then $S$ has a triple
repeat of length at least $L-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
A repeated factor of $P$ of length at least $L-1$ occurs at least four times in
$S$. Extending its two occurrence families maximally in both directions is
uniform by $p$-periodicity and cannot reach length $G$, because that would make
$r_2-r_1$ a period of $S$ and hence a smaller period of $P$. At termination the
three-copy maximality condition holds for any three occurrences meeting both
families.
\end{proof}

A length-$L$ read bridges a copy of a repeat of length $\ell$ only if
$\ell\le L-2$. Hence $R\in\Is$ forbids every triple repeat of length
$\ge L-1$. Combining this with the extension lemmas yields the main theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Rigidity under $\Is$]
\label{thm:rigidity}
If $S$ admits a read realization $R\in\Is$, then $A=\spec_L(S)$ is the unique
positive circulation of total $G$ on $X_S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $S$ is primitive: $R\in\Is$ gives no triple repeat of length $\ge L-1$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive-extension} no length-$(L-1)$ window occurs three times;
Theorem~\ref{thm:short-repeat} applies. If $S=P^k$ with minimal period
$p<G$: by Lemma~\ref{lem:periodic-extension} every factor of $P$ of length
$\ge L-1$ occurs at most once, so the $p$ length-$L$ and length-$(L-1)$ windows
of $P$ are pairwise distinct and $X_S$ is a single directed $p$-cycle with
$A(w)=k$ on every edge. A positive circulation on a directed cycle is constant
by balance, and total $G=kp$ forces the value $k$, so $B=A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No strict same-length Section~6.2 counterexample]
\label{cor:same-length-zero}
Suppose $R\in\Is$ and the truth is Section~6.2-admissible, so
$\supp x=\supp\spec_L(S)$. Then every same-length Section~6.2 spelled candidate
$D$ satisfies $\spec_L(D)=\spec_L(S)$, and for every objective that is a function
of $(\spec_L(D),x)$,
\[
  \frac{\Lik(D;x)}{\Lik(S;x)}=1 .
\]
There is no strict same-length counterexample for any $G$, $L$, or alphabet.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} the only positive circulation of total $G$ on
$X_S$ is $A$, so every same-length spelled candidate has spectrum $A$. Both
objectives depend on the candidate only through its spectrum, and the
observation is common, so the scores coincide.
\end{proof}

Only the triple-repeat clause of $\Is$ feeds the spectrum-rigidity and
no-strict-counterexample conclusions; the interleaved-repeat and coverage clauses
are not needed there. Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-rotation} below does use the
interleaved clause, since it invokes the complete-spectrum uniqueness input of
Section~\ref{sec:population}, whose hypothesis is Ukkonen's full condition.

\subsubsection{The maximizer theorem on this slice}

The rigidity theorem proves \emph{spectrum} equality, and equal spectra give an
exact \emph{tie} for every objective that factors through the spectrum. The
maximizer statement itself is proved separately, and is stronger than the
hypothesis surface used above: the theorem below takes the full source $\Is$
at the realized read placement set and \emph{derives} §6.2 candidate membership
rather than assuming it.

\begin{theorem}[Same-length maximizer, oriented read types, full $\Is$]
\label{thm:maximizer}
Let $S$ be a circular word of length $G$, and let the realized read placement
set $R$ (the set of starts at which reads were actually drawn) be information
feasible, $R\in\Is$. Suppose the truth is a genuine §6.2 candidate for the
observed read set, and let $D$ be any same-length genuine §6.2 candidate for the
same observation. Then, for both the exact
Medvedev--Brudno multinomial and the §6.1 binomial approximation at $N=G$,
\[
  \Lik(D;x) \le \Lik(S;x).
\]
\end{theorem}

The hypothesis set is exactly the source's: $2 \le L \le G$, full $\Is$, and the
§6.2 data. There is no long-triple-repeat premise, no primitivity or period
premise, and no ``escape'' or culprit hypothesis; $\Is \Rightarrow \neg
\textsc{LongTripleRepeat}$ is a consequence proved inside the chain. The start
set is the \emph{range} of the realization, not a superset: because every clause
of $\Is$ is a ``some $r \in R$ does \dots'' or a coverage condition, a start set
$R \supseteq \range\rho$ that contains starts at which no read was drawn is a
strictly weaker hypothesis, and the theorem is stated at the faithful one.
For genuine same-length candidates with truth candidacy, cyclic-rotation
uniqueness is now proved directly in the Lean kernel, without an external
BBT obstruction premise (Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-rotation}).

\subsubsection{The §6.2 acceptance boundary is a separate question}

Theorem~\ref{thm:maximizer} requires \emph{both} the truth and the competitor to
be genuine §6.2 candidates. Dropping the conjunct on the truth is a different
statement, and it is false at equal length: with $S=AABB$ ($G=4$, $L=2$) and the
realized reads $AB,BA$ at starts $1,3$, full $\Is$ holds, while the same-length
genome $ABAB$ is a literal §6.2 \texttt{SpelledFeasible62} candidate whose exact
likelihood is $1/4$ against the truth's $1/16$. So the §6.2 candidate
restriction does not rescue the maximum-likelihood claim unless the truth is
itself admissible, which is precisely what distinguishes the molecule-class
witnesses below from this positive row.

\begin{corollary}[Uniqueness up to rotation on the truth-admissible same-length row]
\label{cor:same-length-rotation}
Under the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-zero}, every same-length
Section~6.2 spelled candidate $D$ is a cyclic rotation of $S$. Consequently the
truth is not merely a maximizer on this row: it is the unique maximizer up to
cyclic rotation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $R\in\Is$, every copy of a triple repeat is bridged and every interleaved
maximal-repeat pair is bridged; a length-$L$ read bridges a copy of a repeat of
length $\ell$ only if $\ell\le L-2$. Hence $S$ satisfies Ukkonen's condition at
$K=L-1$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-zero},
$\spec_L(D)=\spec_L(S)$, so $D$ is an Eulerian circuit of the same $K$-mer graph
built from that complete $L$-spectrum. Theorem~\ref{thm:BBT} at $K=L-1$ gives a
unique Eulerian cycle for this graph, spelling $S$ up to cyclic rotation; hence
$D$ is a cyclic rotation of $S$. Since every candidate in the class has
likelihood ratio $1$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-zero}, the maximizer set is
exactly the rotation class of $S$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Beatability criterion]
\label{prop:beatability}
Fix $S,L$ and an observation $x$ with $\supp x=\supp\spec_L(S)$, and write
$A=\spec_L(S)$. If $A$ is the unique positive circulation of total $G$ on
$X_S$, the truth ties every same-length spelled candidate. If $B\ne A$ is
another positive circulation of total $G$, choose $w_0$ with $B(w_0)>A(w_0)$ and
put $x_M=x+M e_{w_0}$. Then the likelihood ratio equals
$\mathrm{ratio}_0\cdot(B(w_0)/A(w_0))^M$, which exceeds $1$ for all sufficiently
large $M$; the fixed-length binomial's domain is respected because $B(w_0)<G=N$
whenever $B$ is positive and non-constant.
\end{proposition}

Thus non-rigidity is exactly the same-length obstruction, and by
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} the hypothesis $\Is$ removes it. The minimum
same-length non-rigid pair, found by exact search, is $S=AAAAB$,
$D=AABAB$ ($G=5$, $L=2$); its truth carries a long triple repeat and is
therefore not $\Is$-admissible. Skirting the triple-repeat clause is precisely
what permits the multiplicity reallocation.

\subsection{Strict finite counterexamples}

The rigidity theorem is a positive same-length statement. Everything else in
this subsection is negative, and each witness is strict, hence robust to the
unresolved equivalence and tie conventions.

\begin{theorem}[Exact-multinomial counterexample over all circular candidates]
\label{thm:cex-exact}
Let $\Alphabet=\{A,B\}$, $L=3$, and let the truth be
\[
  S=AAABB \quad (G=5),
\]
with realized starts $0,1,4$, so the observed reads are $\{AAA,AAB,BAA\}$, each
once. Let
\[
  D=AAAAB \quad (|D|=5).
\]
Then $R\in\Is$ holds non-vacuously, and
\[
  \frac{\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(D;x)}{\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(S;x)} = 2 > 1 .
\]
Consequently the truth is not a maximizer for the exact multinomial over all
circular candidates, so both the maximizer and uniqueness schemas are false for
this objective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The spectra on the observed types are $\spec_3(S)=(AAA:1,AAB:1,BAA:1)$ and
$\spec_3(D)=(AAA:2,AAB:1,BAA:1)$; $\spec_3$ has total $5$ for both. The
multinomial coefficient $\binom{3}{1,1,1}=6$ is common, and all candidates have
length $5$, so
\[
  \Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(S;x)=6\cdot(1/5)^3=\frac{6}{125},\qquad
  \Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(D;x)=6\cdot(2/5)(1/5)(1/5)=\frac{12}{125}.
\]
For $\Is$: the starts cover all five positions; the three $A$'s at positions
$0,1,2$ form a maximal length-$1$ triple repeat, bridged respectively by the
reads at starts $4,0,1$; and no two maximal repeat pairs have four alternating
starts, so the interleaved clause is vacuous. The all-bridged triple-repeat
clause is therefore genuinely exercised. The transfers to all circular
candidates by Lemma~\ref{lem:negative-transfer} because the witness is
same-length.
\statusline{\kernelcheck{} in the repository's
\texttt{Fixed\allowbreak{}Length\allowbreak{}Exact\allowbreak{}Counterexample} module.}
\end{proof}

Repeating the start-$0$ read $k$ times gives the observed multiset
$\{AAA^k,AAB,BAA\}$ and ratio $2^k$, so the failure is a one-parameter family,
not a coincidental tie. A second, independent same-length instance with a
larger separation is $S=AAABCBC$, $D=AAAAABC$ ($G=7$, $L=3$), realized starts
$(0,0,0,1,2,5,6)$ and observed $x=\{AAA:3,AAB:1,ABC:1,BCA:1,CAA:1\}$, for which
$\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(D;x)/\Lik_{\mathrm{ex}}(S;x)=27$ and $\Is$ holds
non-vacuously (coverage, the all-bridged maximal length-$1$ triple repeat at
$(0,1,2)$, and no interleaved pair).

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-length binomial counterexample]
\label{thm:cex-binomial}
Let $\Alphabet=\{A,C\}$ inside the four-letter DNA alphabet, $L=3$, truth
$S=AAACC$ with realized starts $0,1,4$ (observed reads $AAA,AAC,CAA$), and
competitor $D=AAAAC$, both of length $5$. Under the literal Section~6.1 product
of binomial marginals with $N=5$, the zero-count factors retained,
\[
  \frac{\Lik_{\mathrm{bin}}(D;x)}{\Lik_{\mathrm{bin}}(S;x)}
  = \frac{1125}{512} > 1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The instance is Theorem~\ref{thm:cex-exact} with $B$ relabeled to $C$, so the
same $\Is$ certificate transfers. The spectra are $S=(AAA:1,AAC:1,CAA:1)$ and
$D=(AAA:2,AAC:1,CAA:1)$ on the observed types. Carrying the factors for the
zero-multiplicity types changes the ratio from $2$ to $1125/512$:
\[
  \Lik_{\mathrm{bin}}(S;x)=\frac{452984832}{30517578125},\qquad
  \Lik_{\mathrm{bin}}(D;x)=\frac{7962624}{244140625}.
\]
\statusline{\kernelcheck{} in \texttt{Fixed\allowbreak{}Length\allowbreak{}Binomial\allowbreak{}Counterexample}. The
difference from the exact ratio shows that dropping or retaining the zero-count
factors changes the objective. By Lemma~\ref{lem:negative-transfer} the witness
refutes the maximizer statement over every circular candidate on which the
binomial objective is defined, since both $S$ and $D$ lie in the domain
($\spec_3(D)(AAA)=2<5$).}
\end{proof}

We now turn to the reverse-complement molecular reading of the Section~6.2 flow.
Here read types are molecule classes, the truth and competitor must both be
admissible bidirected circuits, and the objective is the literal Section~6.1
product with external $N$. The mechanism is different from the sequence-level
witnesses: reverse complementarity \emph{creates} multiplicity that the
observation does not force, and support equality then permits reallocation.

\begin{theorem}[Section~6.2 variable-length molecule counterexample]
\label{thm:cex-molecule}
Let $\Alphabet=\{A,T\}$ with involution $A\leftrightarrow T$, $L=3$, truth
$S=AAATT$ ($G=5$) with realized starts $0,1,4$, and competitor
$D=AAAATT$ ($|D|=6$). Then $R\in\Is$ holds; both $S$ and $D$ induce admissible
Section~6.2 bidirected circuits; and with external $N=5$,
\[
  \frac{\Lik_{6.1}(D)}{\Lik_{6.1}(S)} = \frac{9}{8} > 1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Under $A\leftrightarrow T$ the molecule classes of $S$ are
$d_S=(AAA:1,AAT:2,TAA:2)$ and the observed classes are
$x=(AAA:1,AAT:1,TAA:1)$; for $D$ they are $d_D=(AAA:2,AAT:2,TAA:2)$. Both
spectra have the same support as $x$. The ratio of the $AAA$ factor is
$[(2/5)(3/5)^2]/[(1/5)(4/5)^2]=9/8$, and the other two classes have equal
multiplicities, so they contribute factor $1$. Bidirected-circuit feasibility and
the $\Is$ certificate are checked by exact computation and by an explicit
bidirected graph.
\statusline{\kernelcheck{} for the finite certificate and the strict inequality;
\computed{} for the bidirected graph certificate, which is checked by script and
not reproduced in Lean.}
\end{proof}

Allowing the lengths to be equal does not save the Section~6.2 reading. The
smallest same-length witness is $S=AAATAT$, $D=AAAAAT$ ($G=6$), for which the
exact ratio is $3$ and the binomial ratio is $5$, with both genomes
Section~6.2-feasible under the source's per-vertex lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[Section~6.2 same-length counterexample under the per-occurrence
strengthening]
\label{thm:cex-peroccurrence}
Let $\Alphabet=\{A,C,G,T\}$ with the DNA involution $A\leftrightarrow T$,
$C\leftrightarrow G$ (no self-complementary base), $L=3$, truth
$S=ATATACAC$ ($G=8$) with realized starts $1,3,4,5,6,7$, and competitor
$D=ATACACAC$ ($|D|=|S|=8$), external $N=8$. Then $R\in\Is$ holds; both $S$ and
$D$ induce admissible Section~6.2 bidirected circuits; both spectra have the same
support as the observation $x$; \emph{and} both are per-occurrence feasible,
$x(w)\le d_S(w)$ and $x(w)\le d_D(w)$ for every observed molecule class $w$ (the
tight coordinate is $ACA/TGT$, where $x=d_S=2$, while $x_{ATA/TAT}=1<3=d_S$, so
$n=6<G=8$ and the instance is not read-tiled). With both same-length objectives
the competitor is strictly better:
\[
  \frac{\Lik_E(D)}{\Lik_E(S)}=\frac{3}{2}>1,
  \qquad
  \frac{\Lik_{6.1}(D)}{\Lik_{6.1}(S)}=\frac{9}{5}>1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The molecule classes are $x=(ATA/TAT{:}1,\allowbreak TAC/GTA{:}1,\allowbreak ACA/TGT{:}2,\allowbreak CAC/GTG{:}1,\allowbreak CAT/ATG{:}1)$,
$d_S=(ATA/TAT{:}3,\allowbreak TAC/GTA{:}1,\allowbreak ACA/TGT{:}2,\allowbreak CAC/GTG{:}1,\allowbreak CAT/ATG{:}1)$ and
$d_D=(ATA/TAT{:}1,\allowbreak TAC/GTA{:}1,\allowbreak ACA/TGT{:}3,\allowbreak CAC/GTG{:}2,\allowbreak CAT/ATG{:}1)$; all three have the
same support, and $d_S,d_D\ge x$ coordinate-wise. Since $|D|=|S|$, the exact
factors are $3\cdot2$ against $1\cdot3$ on the redistributed coordinates, giving
$3/2$, and the binomial marginals give $9/5$. The $\Is$ certificate, the explicit
sixteen-edge bidirected overlap graph, the vacuous transitive reduction, the
signed-incidence balances and the vertex lower bound $1$ are all checked exactly,
for both genomes.
\statusline{\kernelcheck{} for the whole certificate, including the literal
Section~6.2 feasibility of both genomes and both strict inequalities.}
\end{proof}

The per-occurrence rule is what makes this witness necessary. The same-length
witness $AAATAT\to AAAAAT$ refutes the source's per-vertex reading, but its truth
is \emph{not} per-occurrence feasible ($d_S(AAA)=1<2=x_{AAA}$), so that
refutation does not transfer. On the binary alphabet $A\leftrightarrow T$ the
mechanism does not arise at all in the searched scope: with a self-complementary
base, a same-length per-occurrence instance has zero beats at
$(G,L)=(6,3),(7,3),(8,3)$ and $(6,4),(8,4)$, and the first beats appear at
$(G,L)=(8,3)$ on the real four-letter alphabet. The strengthening is a
repository-added surface, so this refutation settles a determinate mathematical
question rather than a reading of the 2016 sentence; it is reported because the
matrix commits to resolving determinate rows one way or the other.

\begin{remark}[Which assumption surfaces are still open, and which are not the
source's]
\label{rem:surfaces}
The molecule witnesses above use the source's per-vertex lower bound, i.e.\
support equality. Medvedev--Brudno §6.2 states only ``Each vertex has a lower
bound of $1$ since it represents a read that must be present in the genome at
least once''; the strictly stronger per-occurrence requirement $d_D(w)\ge x(w)$
for every observed type is a \emph{repository-added} assumption surface, not a
source definition. That distinction matters for what may be reported as
settled:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Refuted.} Same-length §6.2 candidate class, molecule read types,
    per-occurrence strengthening. The witness is $S=ATATACAC$, $D=ATACACAC$
    ($G=8$, $L=3$, realized starts $(1,3,4,5,6,7)$, $n=6<N=8$): exact ratio
    $3/2$, binomial ratio $9/5$, with both genomes literal §6.2 bidirected
    circuits and both per-occurrence feasible. The mechanism needs the real
    four-letter DNA alphabet: with a self-complementary base the searched scope
    contains no same-length instance at all
    (Thm.~\ref{thm:cex-peroccurrence}). This is a \emph{determinate} row, so it
    is reported as settled; it is not a reading of the 2016 sentence, because the
    strengthening is a repository-added surface.
  \item \textbf{Open in both directions, and not a determinate reading of the
    2016 sentence at all.} The doubled-strand $2G$ \emph{concatenation}
    convention of \cite{bresler2013}, i.e.\ one length-$2G$ circle
    $S\cdot\rc(S)$. No bounded search is used as evidence in either direction:
    the absence of a known beat in the searched scopes is recorded as
    \emph{absence of a known witness}, not as evidence of openness, and two of
    those scopes are vacuous because the remapped $\Is$ is unsatisfiable there.
    The known $AAATAT\to AAAAAT$ witness is \emph{inadmissible} under this
    remap: the doubled circle carries a length-$4$ triple repeat that no
    length-$3$ read can bridge, for \emph{every} read set
     (\texttt{doubled\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}information\_\allowbreak{}feasible}). The row is therefore open in
     both directions rather than refuted. The \emph{compatibility} of this
     convention with the oriented one is now kernel-checked in both directions:
     the remap is exactly compatible with the oriented reading iff read-seat
     preservation, seam--wrap agreement, and doubled-circle feasibility all hold,
     and off that locus the two models are incomparable --- $GGGA$ satisfies the
     remapped $\Is$ while failing the oriented $\Is$ (spurious wrapping-read
     seats), and $AAATAT$ is the reverse
     (\texttt{Bresler\allowbreak{}Remap\allowbreak{}Compatibility.\allowbreak{}remap\_\allowbreak{}natural\_\allowbreak{}seat\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}sound},

\begin{remark}[The general §6.2 flow domain, and two optima and an overlap
floor that must not be conflated]
\label{rem:flow-domain}
The §6.2 refutations above are stated for \emph{spelled} bidirected circuits.
Leaf~\#214 upgrades them to the \emph{whole} §6.2 flow domain, at two values of
the explicit overlap floor $o_{\min}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item at $o_{\min}=1$ ($L=3$) the truth is still not a maximizer, and the flow
        optimum $d^*=(AAA{:}2,AAT{:}1,TAA{:}1)$ (with
        $d^*_3=(AAA{:}3,AAT{:}1,TAA{:}1)$) spells no circular molecule of any
        length, so the maximum-likelihood \emph{object} of the flow domain is a
        non-sequence and the maximum-likelihood \emph{sequence} does not exist
        there;
  \item at the full overlap $o_{\min}=L-1=2$ ($L=3$) the truth $AAATAT$ is
        likewise not a maximizer, but here the unique integer optimum
        $d^*=(AAA{:}2,AAT{:}1,ATA{:}1,TAA{:}1)$ has \emph{disconnected positive
        support} (a doubled $AAA$ self-overlap together with the circuit
        $AAT\to ATA\to TAA\to AAT$), so positive-support connectedness of all
        optima is false, and it strictly beats the truth
        ($\Lik(d^*)/\Lik(d_S)=1280/243$). Its throughput, however, \emph{is}
        spellable: it is the spectrum of $AAAAT$ and also has a connected
        realization. This is disconnectedness of the \emph{flow}, not
        non-spellability of the \emph{throughput}; ``positive-support
        connectedness of all optima'' is a project-level strengthening, not a
        Medvedev--Brudno notion, and the flow-to-sequence gap is untouched by
        it.
\end{itemize}
Two optima, one parameter, and one distinction must be kept separate:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \textbf{integer flow optimum} --- the argmax of the §6.1 objective
        over integer throughput vectors $1\le d\le N$ that are genuine §6.2 flows
        (the kernel-checked $d^*$, $d^*_3$); this is the §6.2 object the source
        defines;
  \item the \textbf{half-integral relaxation} --- the argmax when $d$ ranges over
        half-integers (the per-coordinate maximizer of
        $(d/N)^x((N-d)/N)^{n-x}$ is $d=xN/n$, e.g.\ $5/2$ for the $AAA$ coordinate
        here, which is neither an integer nor a §6.2 flow throughput);
  \item the \textbf{overlap floor} $o_{\min}$ --- the $o_{\min}=1$ and the
        $o_{\min}=L-1$ witnesses live on different graphs (the shorter overlaps
        exist only when $o_{\min}\le L-2$), so neither may be read as the other;
        the merged spelled witnesses (Thm.~\ref{thm:cex-molecule} and the rows
        below it) are stated at $o_{\min}=L-1=2$;
  \item the \textbf{throughput versus the flow realization} --- the
        $o_{\min}=L-1$ optimum's throughput is spellable ($AAAAT$) while its
        flow support is disconnected, whereas the $o_{\min}=1$ optimum's
        throughput is not spellable at all. ``Non-spellable spectrum'' and
        ``disconnected flow'' are \emph{different} properties and must not be
        conflated.
\end{itemize}
The relaxation is a different, larger upper bound; substituting it for the
integer flow optimum would overstate what the §6.2 domain refutes. The board
records the integer optimum as the §6.2 result and keeps the relaxation distinct.
Both general-flow results are kernel-checked:
\texttt{Assembly\allowbreak{}P1/\allowbreak{}Section62Non\allowbreak{}Spelled\allowbreak{}Flow.\allowbreak{}lean}
(\texttt{spelled\_\allowbreak{}subset\_\allowbreak{}general}, \texttt{star\_argmax},
\texttt{no\_\allowbreak{}sequence\_\allowbreak{}has\_\allowbreak{}star\_\allowbreak{}spectrum},
\texttt{nonspelled\_\allowbreak{}se62\_\allowbreak{}flow\_\allowbreak{}domain\_\allowbreak{}countermodel}) for $o_{\min}=1$, which
certifies that a spelled candidate is a genuine §6.2 flow and proves no circular
molecule realizes $d^*$. The second,
\texttt{Assembly\allowbreak{}P1/\allowbreak{}Section62Full\allowbreak{}Overlap\allowbreak{}Disconnected.\allowbreak{}lean},
for $o_{\min}=L-1$, proves the disconnected full-overlap optimum and its
strict beat
(\texttt{full\_\allowbreak{}overlap\_\allowbreak{}disconnected\_\allowbreak{}optimum},
\texttt{disc\_\allowbreak{}support\_\allowbreak{}disconnected}, \texttt{dStar\_argmax},
\texttt{star\_better}). Both audit to the three standard axioms.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The physical duplex is two disjoint circles, not a concatenation]
\label{rem:duplex}
The doubled-strand convention must not be conflated across its two readings. The
$2G$ \emph{concatenation} of \cite{bresler2013} glues $S$ and $\rc(S)$ into one
circle; the alternative --- the one matching the physical double-stranded
circle --- is \emph{two disjoint} cyclic strands $S$ and $\rc(S)$. The
distinction is material rather than cosmetic. The reverse-complement map
$\rho(i)=(G-1-i)\bmod G$ reverses order and preserves strict bridging, so
\emph{circle-by-circle} $\Is$ on the disjoint duplex is exactly equivalent to
$\Is(S)$, and the $AAATAT\to AAAAAT$ witness survives (ratios $3$ and $5$). The
\emph{duplex-as-a-whole} reading is ill-defined (there is no cyclic order on two
disjoint circles) and strictly stronger: six mixed cross-strand triple repeats
of length $\ge L-1$ are unbridgeable for every read set. The concatenation and
the disjoint duplex are genuinely inequivalent: their candidate spectra differ
exactly when the seam words are not matched by wrapping words, which happens for
half of the binary words in the searched scope. Finally, when strand orientation
is \emph{unobserved}, the disjoint-duplex molecule-class distribution
$2\,m_S(C)/(2G)=m_S(C)/G$ is identical to the Medvedev--Brudno molecule
distribution (self-reverse-complementary palindromes counted once), so it is not
a distinct likelihood objective there; only \emph{oriented} reads differ. All of
this is a disclosed modelling decision, not a reading of the 2016 sentence, and
it settles neither the concatenation row nor the referent.

mixed-repeat obstruction are \kernelcheck{} in
\texttt{Assembly\allowbreak{}P1/\allowbreak{}Double\allowbreak{}Strand\allowbreak{}Bridging\allowbreak{}Transfer.\allowbreak{}lean} and
\texttt{Assembly\allowbreak{}P1/\allowbreak{}Two\allowbreak{}Disjoint\allowbreak{}Circles\allowbreak{}Duplex.\allowbreak{}lean}; the spectrum/seam arithmetic
and the witness ratios are \computed{} by
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak{}verify\_\allowbreak{}two\_\allowbreak{}disjoint\_\allowbreak{}circles\_\allowbreak{}duplex.\allowbreak{}py} and
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak{}verify\_\allowbreak{}oriented\_\allowbreak{}molecule\_\allowbreak{}bridging.\allowbreak{}py}. The representation
choice is a \projchoice{} and the historical strand convention stays open.}
\end{remark}

\subsection{The candidate-length boundary}
\label{sec:finite:length}

The same-length restriction in the positive theorem is not cosmetic. On the next
slice, where $|D|>G$ is allowed, the truth need not be optimal even under $\Is$,
and this is an infinite-family phenomenon rather than a bounded search.

\begin{theorem}[Oriented variable-length Section~6.2 classification]
\label{thm:cex-variable-length}
Let $\Alphabet=\{A,T\}$, $L=3$, truth $S=AAATT$ ($G=5$), realized starts
$R=\{0,1,2,3,4\}$, so $R\in\Is$ holds at full strength (coverage, the unique
maximal length-$1$ triple repeat all-bridged, the interleaved clause vacuous).
Write $x^{(M)}=\spec_3(S)+M e_{AAA}$ ($n=5+M$). For the growing competitor
$D_M=A^{3+M}TT$ ($|D_M|=5+M$),
\[
  \frac{\Lik_E(D_M;x^{(M)})}{\Lik_E(S;x^{(M)})}
    = \Bigl(\tfrac{5}{5+M}\Bigr)^{5+M}(1+M)^{1+M} > 1
  \qquad\text{for every } M\ge 1 ;
\]
for the fixed competitor $D=AAAATT$ ($|D|=6$) the exact ratio is
$(\tfrac{3125}{3888})(\tfrac53)^M>1$ and the fixed-$N$ binomial ratio
($N=5$) is $(\tfrac{81}{128})2^M>1$, for every $M\ge 1$. Every candidate is a
genuine Section~6.2 candidate under the per-vertex, spelled-support and
single-circuit readings. The truth wins exactly at $n=G$; a single extra
observation of the over-represented $AAA$ type overturns it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
For the exact objective the observation-only coefficient is common, so the
competitor with $d_{D_M}=x^{(M)}$ and $|D_M|=n$ attains the AM--GM upper bound
$\Lik^*(x^{(M)})=\binom{n}{x}\prod_w(x_w/n)^{x_w}$, while the truth's normalized
spectrum differs at the $AAA$ coordinate for every $M\ge1$. The fixed-competitor
families are the amplification lemma made concrete: each additional observation
of an observed type $w$ multiplies the exact odds by
$p_D(w)/p_S(w)$ and the fixed-$N$ binomial odds by
$Q_w=(p_D(w)/p_S(w))\prod_{i\ne w}(1-p_D(i))/(1-p_S(i))$; here
$p_D(AAA)/p_S(AAA)=5/3>1$ and $Q_{AAA}=2>1$.
\statusline{\kernelcheck{} at $M=1,2$ in
\texttt{Assembly\allowbreak{}P1/\allowbreak{}Oriented\allowbreak{}Variable\allowbreak{}Length\allowbreak{}Se62.\allowbreak{}lean}
(\texttt{oriented\_\allowbreak{}variable\_\allowbreak{}length\_\allowbreak{}se62\_\allowbreak{}counterexample}, exact
$15625/11664$, $2109375/823543$; binomial $81/64$, $27/16$), which also
discharges $\Is$ by \texttt{decide} on the shared predicate. The general
families are \mathfact{} (the note
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak{}source-notes/\allowbreak{}oriented-variable-length-se62.\allowbreak{}md}); the identities are
\computed{} for $M=0..200$ by
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak{}verify\_\allowbreak{}oriented\_\allowbreak{}variable\_\allowbreak{}length\_\allowbreak{}se62*.\allowbreak{}py}.}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sampling instability is the mechanism]
\label{rem:amplification}
The variable-length failure is not a coincidence of one word pair. The
amplification lemmas show that whenever a competitor over-weights an observed
type ($p_D(w)>p_S(w)$), repeating that type in the sample drives the odds in the
competitor's favour without bound; symmetrically, a tie ($Q_w=1$) stays a tie
and a multiplier below one drives the odds to zero. The
exact-vs-fixed-$N$ distinction matters: the two multipliers differ because the
fixed-$N$ product moves the $(n-x_i)$ exponents at every other type. The same
word pair also appears as a reverse-complement variable-length witness; here the
orientation is fixed by observing every truth type plus an extra $AAA$, so the
mechanism is observed-multiplicity skew rather than strand collapse. The
general sample-multiplicity formalization drafted for issue~\#216 is
\emph{redundant} and is deliberately not integrated (it does not compile); the
result is already kernel-checked through the instances above, whose module
\texttt{Oriented\allowbreak{}Variable\allowbreak{}Length\allowbreak{}Se62} is the canonical carrier.
\end{remark}
