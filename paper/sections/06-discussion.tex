\section{Discussion: structure, sampling, and what is identifiable}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The shape of the answer}

The finite picture in Table~\ref{tab:finite} is neither a proof nor a refutation
of a single sentence. It is a map of an interface. Bridging conditions are a
\emph{structural} hypothesis; maximum likelihood is an \emph{optimization}
objective; the published sentence asks whether the first implies the second. Our
study shows that the answer depends on choices the sentence does not make, and
that the mathematically clean content lives in two distinct failure modes.

\paragraph{Structural/model failure.}
Without a repeat/read-length boundary the genome need not be identifiable at all
(Example~\ref{ex:ABAB}). Bridging conditions repair the structural ambiguity, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} shows that on the same-length oriented Section~6.2
slice they are strong enough to make the truth's spectrum the only positive
circulation --- an exact rigidity, not just a reconstruction claim. Requiring
the candidate's length to equal the true length and requiring the truth
to be a genuine candidate are two independent extra assumptions, neither
supplied by bridging alone. The same-length $AABB\to ABAB$ witness
shows that truth candidacy matters, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:cex-variable-length} shows that even truth-admissible
full-support samples need the length restriction.

\paragraph{Finite-sampling failure.}
Even structurally admissible genomes can lose to the truth's competitors because
empirical read frequencies fluctuate (Example~\ref{ex:frequencies}). This is a
different mechanism and needs a different repair. Population maximum likelihood
removes it at the maximizer level by construction
(Lemma~\ref{lem:gibbs}), and for the oriented primitive P2 class it also yields
uniqueness (Theorem~\ref{thm:population}).

The combined picture is mathematically direct: repeat structure determines
which circular spellings are distinguishable from reads; a graph algorithm
recovers the source when the reads are sufficiently informative; and a
likelihood objective additionally depends on sampled frequencies.
Positive spectrum rigidity identifies one finite regime where structure
controls the ranking. Population likelihood removes frequency fluctuations,
reducing uniqueness to complete-spectrum identifiability.

\subsection{What the proved results contribute}
\label{sec:discussion:proofs}

The results form a collection of distinct mathematical advances,
not a ranking in which a general theorem about ML eclipses the
assembly work.

The Bayes/MAP optimality theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bayes-optimal}) is the \emph{reason to enter} the
likelihood investigation: with a correct model and uniform prior,
ML attains the best prior-average reconstruction probability of
any algorithm. That theorem does not tell us which particular
read samples make the truth win, or whether a genome can be
distinguished from another. Those are the paper's principal
research questions.

The finite rigidity theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}) is a
\emph{combinatorial classification result}: bridging constrains
the true support graph so strongly that it admits only one
positive integer circulation of the prescribed total. The
same-length maximizer and rotation-uniqueness results are
genuine deductions from this structure, under explicit
candidate and strand assumptions. The Graver certificate
(Theorem~\ref{thm:graver-rigidity}) gives a separate general
if-and-only-if criterion for rigidity, and the effective-weight
theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:binomial-weights}) does the analogous
job for score dominance in the fixed-$N$ surrogate's all-count
domain. These deserve attention for their own results, not
merely because they support ML.

The strict historical counterexamples
(Theorems~\ref{thm:cex-historical-W1} and
\ref{thm:cex-historical-W2}) are equally decisive in a different
direction: they establish exactly where a tempting universal
implication fails, even under the source's own read-string
bridging notion. The variable-length families
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cex-variable-length}) establish a general
sampling-reweighting mechanism rather than an isolated anomaly.

The population uniqueness theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:population}) is not just the elementary
Gibbs inequality. The nontrivial proof removes possible scaling
of normalized spectra and establishes complete-spectrum
identifiability for primitive P2 genomes; the once-external
uniqueness step has been discharged in Lean. Finally,
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction-mismatch} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-spelling} distinguish unsound
overlap deletion from a genuinely spelling-preserving graph
reduction. That distinction determines whether the optimized
object even represents a sequence.

A further result connects such a graph spelling to the actual
\emph{multiplicities} of observed reads, rather than only to the existence
of a spelling.

\begin{theorem}[Circular occurrences in reduced signed circuits]
\label{thm:reduced-circuit-occurrences}
Let $p$ be a nonempty closed signed walk of the implemented
spelling-preserving reduced overlap graph on equal-length oriented reads
of length $L$. Let $D$ be its spelled circular genome, of length
$G=\sum_{e\in p}(L-\ell_e)$, where $\ell_e$ is the overlap length of
edge $e$. For every observed oriented read word $w$,
\[
  \#\{0\le s<G:\operatorname{window}_L(D,s)=w\}
  =
  \#\{e\in p:\operatorname{read}(\operatorname{sx}(e))=w\}.
\]
The equality also holds when $G<L$, so that a read wraps around the
circular genome more than once.
\end{theorem}

\texttt{AssemblyP1.Section62GlobalSkippedBridge}
(\texttt{observation7\_reduced\_signed\_circuit\_range\_count});
the circular spelling, anchors, and no-skipped-observed-window property
are constructed from the actual reduced walk, not postulated.}

This is the oriented read-word occurrence statement corresponding to
Observation~7 for \emph{our explicitly defined reduction}. It does not
identify that reduction with every step of Myers's approximate-overlap
algorithm, equate reverse-complement molecule-class counts, or assert
that every feasible balanced flow is realizable by one closed signed walk.
For \emph{observed reverse-complement molecule classes}, the oriented
identity also yields a kernel-checked class-count equality: add the two
distinct strand-word counts, or count only once when the read word is its
own reverse complement. This is independent of the strand chosen to
represent the class
(\texttt{Section62ObservedMoleculeCount.observation7\_observed\_rc\_class\_count}).
This biological interpretation assumes the strand involution supplied to
the model really acts as DNA reverse complement on read words; the abstract
count identity itself only needs an involution. It does not supply the
counts of unobserved molecule classes in the full Section~6.1 likelihood.

The finite-read recovery theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-recovery}) is a
\emph{error-exponent} result rather than a structural
classification: it converts population identifiability of a finite candidate
family into an explicit exponential bound on the probability of an exact-ML
error. Its hypotheses are deliberately minimal --- a finite, pairwise
distinguishable family --- and its conclusion is about the error rate, not the
likelihood, so it neither contradicts the finite counterexamples of
Section~\ref{sec:finite} nor claims monotonicity of finite-sample success
(Proposition~\ref{prop:nonmonotone}). Together with the population uniqueness theorem and the primitive-P2
length bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-finite-class}), it gives
finite-read recovery for the \emph{entire} oriented primitive-P2
class (Corollary~\ref{cor:p2-finite-recovery-all}), not merely
an arbitrary finite shortlist. For this class the affinity is not merely
existential: Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-uniform-rate} makes the rate explicit,
bounding the exact-ML error probability by $N_C\,e^{-n/(8B^4)}$ with
$B=2q^{L-1}$ and a matching sample-size threshold $n\ge8B^4\log(N_C/\delta)$.
The candidate class may be enormous
and the constants are exponential in the read length, so this is not a practical
read-depth guarantee.

Each proof therefore has its own mathematical claim, scope,
and consequence. The central investigation remains the
relationship between read information, feasible assembly,
and the likelihood of the \emph{realized} reads.

\subsection{What remains open}

\paragraph{Source questions.}
Which Medvedev--Brudno object the 2016 sentence denotes; which read-type
convention (and hence which equivalence) it intends; whether candidate length is
restricted; whether the conclusion is maximizer or uniqueness; and whether the
unretrieved publisher-hosted supplement archive contains a definition that
narrows any of these. These are not bookkeeping: each changes which formal
statement the published sentence asserts. The full audit is
Appendix~\ref{app:referents}.

\paragraph{The source's own read-string predicate.}
The finite witnesses of Section~\ref{sec:finite} were first stated for the
placement-based $\Is$. Under the source's own supplement-\S6.4 read-string
predicate $\Is^{\mathrm{match}}$, the same-length molecule slice is likewise
\emph{false}, with all historical predicates and both likelihood ratios
kernel-checked (\S\ref{sec:finite:historical}): $W_1$ ($AAATAT\to AAAAAT$) and $W_2$
($ATATACAC\to ATACACAC$) satisfy historical coverage and matched-string bridging
at full strength, are genuine Section~6.2 molecule flows of equal length, and
beat the truth under both the exact multinomial and the fixed-$N$ binomial. This
does \emph{not} settle the referent of the 2016 sentence or the general
$o_{\min}$ Section~6.2 flow domain.

\paragraph{Mathematical questions.}
The same-length Section~6.2 statement under the per-occurrence strengthening
($d_D\ge x$) is \emph{no longer open}: it is false, by the same-length witness
$ATATACAC\to ATACACAC$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cex-peroccurrence}) whose truth is
itself per-occurrence feasible. That this needed the real four-letter DNA
alphabet, where no base is its own reverse complement, is a reminder that the
small-counterexample search space for a modeling choice can be non-monotone in
the alphabet. It remains true that the strengthening is \emph{not}
Medvedev--Brudno's §6.2 rule, which states only a per-vertex lower bound of $1$,
so this is an added assumption surface rather than a reading of the 2016
sentence; it is refuted because the question is determinate, not because a source
asks it. With it, no determinate row of the finite landscape remains open:
what is left is the doubled-strand $2G$ convention of a different paper, which
is open in \emph{both} directions under a convention the 2016 text does not
state (the only proved facts are the known witness's inadmissibility and the
compatibility classification; no bounded search is offered as evidence), and the
unspecified-principle reading, which is not a determinate proposition. The
single-strand reading of the variable-length Section~6.2 statement is
\emph{no longer open}: it is false, by the oriented
variable-length boundary witness, which beats the truth as soon as $n>G$ under
both objectives. The
population uniqueness theorem is now a \emph{fully kernel-checked} Lean theorem
with no external premise: \popml{}
proves that, for $L\ge2$ and a primitive P2 truth, the truth maximizes the
population log-likelihood over \emph{every} positive-length primitive P2
candidate, and that every population tie is a cyclic rotation of the truth. The
classical complete-spectrum analysis of Ukkonen and Pevzner
\cite{ukkonen1992,pevzner1995}, developed in
\cite{bresler2013}, remains a mathematical explanation of the identifiability step, but it
is no longer a required external premise of either the population theorem or
the \emph{truth-candidate-conditional} finite rotation theorem
(Corollary~\ref{cor:same-length-rotation}). Without truth candidacy, a distinct result treats a
\emph{nonprimitive truth} positively, while a primitive truth
can have a certified nonrotation competitor. These cases concern
the truth's primitivity, not the competitor's. Neither is an unrestricted
finite likelihood result. The historical referent
of the 2016 sentence remains a source gap. The population model for
\emph{molecule} read types --- as opposed to oriented types --- is not addressed
here. The finite-read rate of Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-uniform-rate} is provably
not tight for the primitive-P2 class --- the exact worst-case separation
exceeds the proved bound by a factor growing like $B^3$, and the sharp
exponent is conjecturally $1$, not $4$ --- and no uniform exponential rate
extends to the unrestricted genome universe, where read-law collisions such as
$AB$ versus $ABAB$ make the exact-ML error probability $1$ at every sample
size (Remark~\ref{rem:rate-sharpness}). A sharp uniform sample size over the
unrestricted universe therefore remains a motivating open problem, not a
quantity the limit theorems supply.

\paragraph{What would count as settlement.}
A positive settlement of the published question is a Lean proof of a formally
justified version of the published implication after an argument fixes the axes
audited in Appendix~\ref{app:referents} (Table~\ref{tab:axes}).
A negative settlement is a mathematically valid
counterexample satisfying the faithfully formalized bridging hypotheses while
violating the faithfully formalized likelihood conclusion. A computational
discovery is useful but not a settlement until the finite instance is
kernel-checked and the source correspondence is documented.

\subsection{Relation to the literature}

The classical spectrum-reconstruction foundations are Ukkonen's repeat
condition and Pevzner's analysis of equivalent cyclic traversals
\cite{ukkonen1992,pevzner1995}, subsequently adapted to modern
assembly by Bresler, Bresler and Tse \cite{bresler2013}.
Myers \cite{myers2005} developed the string-graph formulation for
overlap-based assembly. Motahari, Bresler and Tse
\cite{motahari2013} studied information-theoretic sequencing thresholds
under stochastic genome models, and the variable-length overlap
results of Hui et al.\ \cite{hui2016} are another adjacent
contribution. These do not answer the finite-sample likelihood
question here.

The closest published result directly relating bridge
conditions to reconstruction predates the 2016 question:
Bresler, Bresler, and Tse prove a \emph{necessary} condition --- if a
problematic interleaved pair or triple repeat has all copies unbridged, another
same-length genome has the same read likelihood. Shomorony et al.\ strengthen
the hypothesis to sufficient conditions for exact reconstruction and then ask
whether those conditions also imply ML optimality. A 2025 correctness-guarantee
paper still presents the information/correctness line and the
maximum-likelihood line as separate \cite{salmela2025}, which is meaningful
evidence that the 2016 question had not become a standard solved result. Our
negative witnesses are consistent with that picture: the implication is not
merely unproved but false under several precise readings, while the positive
same-length and population results show where the intuition does survive.

\subsection{Conclusion}

A collection of short reads has two complementary mathematical structures.
Their overlaps define a graph that can reconstruct a circular genome;
their observed type counts define a statistical objective for comparing
candidate genomes. The repeat-bridging theorem of Shomorony et al.\
explains when graph reconstruction is information-feasible and efficient.
Our positive rigidity theorem shows that these structural conditions can
also force a unique complete-spectrum circulation on an explicitly defined
same-length oriented slice.

Finite likelihood introduces a new phenomenon: sampling can over-represent
some read types, and a competitor can score higher even when the source is
reconstructible. Exact counterexamples establish that boundary under
several observation conventions. With the population distribution instead,
the truth always maximizes expected likelihood; for primitive P2 genomes,
complete-spectrum uniqueness makes that maximum unique up to rotation. On the entire finite oriented primitive-P2 class, the exact-ML
error admits an exponential bound in the number of independent
reads --- with an explicit, deliberately loose rate
$N_C\,e^{-n/(8B^4)}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-uniform-rate}) --- even though its
finite-sample success curve need not be monotone
(Proposition~\ref{prop:nonmonotone}).
The proofs and finite certificates are formalized in Lean. Together,
these results explain what graph structure can determine, what read
frequencies contribute, and how the two interact. Throughout, the
reconstruction guarantee and the likelihood comparison are kept apart:
conditioning on the feasible fiber is a corollary of reconstruction, while
maximizing the unrestricted likelihood over the original candidate universe is
the genuinely nontrivial question, and the finite counterexamples show where the
two diverge. The support matrix of Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}
(Table~\ref{tab:ledger}) records, headline by headline, which of these claims
is kernel-checked, which is a paper-level proof, which rests on a classical or
2016 external premise, and which remains open, so that no conditional
derivation is mistaken for an unconditional maximum-likelihood result.
