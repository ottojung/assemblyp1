\section{Discussion: structure, sampling, and what is identifiable}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The shape of the answer}

The most consequential distinction is between a genome
being \emph{reconstructible} from the overlaps in its reads
and being \emph{most likely} under the observed read
frequencies. Two obstacles separate them.

\paragraph{Structure.}
Distinct genomes can produce the same distribution of
reads, so they cannot be distinguished even with unlimited
data (Example~\ref{ex:ABAB}). Repeat bridging can remove
this ambiguity. For oriented reads and equal-length,
full-support candidates, it also forces the true genome's
complete read counts and its circular spelling
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity},
Corollary~\ref{cor:historical-oriented-same-length}).
The historical reverse-complement molecule observations
belong to a different candidate and observation model.

\paragraph{Sampling and model flexibility.}
Even distinguishable genomes can be ranked incorrectly
by a finite sample. More strikingly, for every fixed truth
with branching oriented read overlaps, its probability
of winning exact likelihood against \emph{all} genome
lengths tends to zero (Theorem~
\ref{thm:unrestricted-branching-rate}).
The flow characterization explains this: arbitrarily long
circular genomes approximate balanced read-frequency
assignments densely, so the unrestricted optimizer
overfits sampling fluctuations. If the candidate class
is instead identifiable and suitably restricted,
finite-sample likelihood errors become exponentially
rare (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-recovery}).
This does not contradict the unrestricted failure,
because the sets of competing genomes differ.

\subsection{What the proved results contribute}
\label{sec:discussion:proofs}

The results have different scopes. The finite counterexamples
and the structural uniqueness theorems should not be
conflated with standard decision-theoretic optimality.

The Bayes/MAP optimality theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bayes-optimal}) is the \emph{reason to enter} the
likelihood investigation: with a correct model and uniform prior,
ML attains the best prior-average reconstruction probability of
any algorithm. That theorem does not tell us which particular
read samples make the truth win, or whether a genome can be
distinguished from another. Those are the paper's principal
research questions.

The finite rigidity theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}) is a
\emph{combinatorial classification result}: bridging constrains
the true support graph so strongly that it admits only one
positive integer circulation of the prescribed total. The
same-length maximizer and rotation-uniqueness results are
genuine deductions from this structure, under explicit
candidate and strand assumptions. The Graver certificate
(Theorem~\ref{thm:graver-rigidity}) gives a separate general
if-and-only-if criterion for rigidity, and the effective-weight
theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:binomial-weights}) does the analogous
job for score dominance in the fixed-$N$ surrogate's all-count
domain. They characterize two different feasible-set and objective questions.

The strict historical counterexamples
(Theorems~\ref{thm:cex-historical-W1} and
\ref{thm:cex-historical-W2}) show, under specified candidate classes, that the proposed implication fails, even under the source's own read-string
bridging notion. The variable-length families
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cex-variable-length}) exhibit
a sampling-reweighting mechanism. As a separate curiosity,
Theorem~\ref{thm:unattained-ML} proves that unrestricted
exact likelihood need not attain a maximum at all.

The population uniqueness theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:population}) is not just the elementary
Gibbs inequality. The nontrivial proof removes possible scaling
of normalized spectra and establishes complete-spectrum
identifiability for primitive P2 genomes; the full uniqueness step is proved in Lean. Finally,
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction-mismatch} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-spelling} distinguish unsound
overlap deletion from a genuinely spelling-preserving graph
reduction. That distinction determines whether the optimized
object even represents a sequence.

A further result connects such a graph spelling to the actual
\emph{multiplicities} of observed reads, rather than only to the existence
of a spelling.

\begin{theorem}[Circular occurrences in reduced signed circuits]
\label{thm:reduced-circuit-occurrences}
Let $p$ be a nonempty closed signed walk of the implemented
spelling-preserving reduced overlap graph on equal-length oriented reads
of length $L$. Let $D$ be its spelled circular genome, of length
$G=\sum_{e\in p}(L-\ell_e)$, where $\ell_e$ is the overlap length of
edge $e$. For every observed oriented read word $w$,
\[
  \#\{0\le s<G:\operatorname{window}_L(D,s)=w\}
  =
  \#\{e\in p:\operatorname{read}(\operatorname{sx}(e))=w\}.
\]
The equality also holds when $G<L$, so that a read wraps around the
circular genome more than once.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A signed overlap step of length $\ell_e$ advances
$L-\ell_e$ positions along the circular spelling. Its
interior positions may generate additional length-$L$
windows, but the spelling-preserving reduction ensures
that no \emph{observed} read type is first produced at
one of those skipped positions. Thus each occurrence
of an observed read in the spelled circle corresponds
to exactly one departure from a visited read vertex,
and vice versa. Summing the bijection over the edges
of the closed walk proves the count identity.
The argument indexes positions modulo $G$, so it
does not require $G\ge L$; repeated wraparound is
counted at each of the $G$ distinct starts.
The precise skipped-window invariant is
kernel-checked for this reduction.
\end{proof}

This is the oriented read-word occurrence statement corresponding to
Observation~7 for \emph{our explicitly defined reduction}. It does not
identify that reduction with every step of Myers's approximate-overlap
algorithm, equate reverse-complement molecule-class counts, or assert
that every feasible balanced flow is realizable by one closed signed walk.
For \emph{observed reverse-complement molecule classes}, the oriented
identity also yields a kernel-checked class-count equality: add the two
distinct strand-word counts, or count only once when the read word is its
own reverse complement. This is independent of the strand chosen to
represent the class
(see the exact Lean declaration in \path{docs/verification.md}).
This biological interpretation assumes the strand involution supplied to
the model really acts as DNA reverse complement on read words; the abstract
count identity itself only needs an involution. It does not supply the
counts of unobserved molecule classes in the full Section~6.1 likelihood.

The finite-read recovery theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-recovery}) is an
\emph{error-exponent} result rather than a structural
classification: it converts population identifiability of a finite candidate
family into an explicit exponential bound on the probability of an exact-ML
error. Its hypotheses are deliberately minimal --- a finite, pairwise
distinguishable family --- and its conclusion is about the error rate, not the
likelihood, so it neither contradicts the finite counterexamples of
Section~\ref{sec:finite} nor claims monotonicity of finite-sample success
(Proposition~\ref{prop:nonmonotone}). Together with the population uniqueness theorem and the primitive-P2
length bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-finite-class}), it gives
finite-read recovery for the \emph{entire} oriented primitive-P2
class (Corollary~\ref{cor:p2-finite-recovery-all}), not merely
an arbitrary finite shortlist. For this class the affinity is not merely
existential: the Lean-checked Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-uniform-rate}
gives a quartic separation bound, and the mathematical
Propositions~\ref{prop:p2-linear-rate} and
\ref{prop:p2-linear-sharpness} establish its sharp
$\Theta(1/B)$ exponent. In particular, the exact-ML
error probability is at most $N_C\,e^{-n/(64B)}$
with $B=2q^{L-1}$, and
$n\ge64B\log(N_C/\delta)$ suffices for error at most $\delta$.
The candidate class may be enormous
and the constants are exponential in the read length, so this is not a practical
read-depth guarantee.

Each proof therefore has its own mathematical claim, scope,
and consequence. The central investigation remains the
relationship between read information, feasible assembly,
and the likelihood of the \emph{realized} reads.


\subsection{Length penalties and an always-defined MAP estimator}
\label{sec:map-length}

Theorem~\ref{thm:flow-supremum-attainment}
characterizes the unrestricted likelihood supremum
as a compact balanced-flow optimization, with a
precise attainment criterion. The optimizing flow
can be irrational (Theorem~\ref{thm:unattained-ML})
or rational but disconnected
(Example~\ref{ex:unattained-disconnected}).
Thus the unrestricted exact-ML \emph{argmax} can be empty. This
does not invalidate likelihood comparisons, but it prevents
unrestricted ML from always defining an estimator. Penalizing
genome length or using a summable prior resolves existence.
It can also make statistical recovery consistent in the
one-sided-P2 setting.

\begin{proposition}[Existence and fixed-truth consistency of length-prior MAP]
\label{prop:length-prior-map}
Let the alphabet have size $q\ge2$, and place the
following prior on \emph{rotation classes} of all nonempty
circular genomes:
\[
  \pi([D])=Z^{-1}\beta^{|D|},
  \qquad 0<\beta<1/q,\qquad
  Z=\sum_{[D]}\beta^{|D|}<\infty.
\]
For every finite read sample of positive marginal
probability, the exact posterior has an attained MAP
maximum. Moreover, if the fixed truth $S$ is primitive
and satisfies P2 at the oriented read length $L\ge2$,
then with iid uniform-start reads the MAP estimate is
eventually the rotation class of $S$, almost surely.
The latter claim is \emph{not} uniform in $S$ or $L$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At length $G$ there are at most $q^G$ rotation classes,
so $Z\le\sum_{G\ge1}(q\beta)^G<\infty$.
At any sample with positive marginal, the nonnegative
posterior masses are summable and at least one is
positive. Such a sequence attains its positive
supremum: only finitely many masses can exceed
half the supremum. This proves MAP existence.

Fix $S$ and let $\Gamma=\{[D]:p_D=p_S\}$. All
models in $\Gamma$ have the same likelihood for
every sample. The strong law identifies the infinite
iid read distribution: under any class outside $\Gamma$,
its limiting empirical frequency vector differs
from $p_S$ almost surely. Since the model space is
countable, the mixture over those other classes
is singular to the iid law $p_S$. Equivalently, in
the Bayesian mixture the event
that empirical frequencies tend to $p_S$ agrees
almost surely with the event that the latent class
belongs to $\Gamma$. Martingale convergence of
conditional probabilities therefore gives
$\Pi_n(\Gamma)\to1$ under $p_S$, almost surely.
Within $\Gamma$, posterior masses stay in their
original prior ratios.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:one-sided-length-window}, every
class in $\Gamma$ other than $[S]$ has length at
least $2G$, where $G=|S|$. Hence
$\pi([D])<\pi([S])$ for all these classes,
and indeed their individual ratios are at most
$\beta^G<1$. The posterior mass of $[S]$
converges to $\pi([S])/\pi(\Gamma)>0$, while
the total posterior mass outside $\Gamma$ tends
to zero. Thus eventually no other class, inside
or outside $\Gamma$, ties or beats $[S]$.
\end{proof}

This is an explicit \emph{prior-dependent} solution,
not a repair of the unrestricted ML principle.
A summable prior by itself guarantees existence
but need not recover the shortest law-equivalent
genome: for example, one could assign its doubled
word a larger prior mass. The geometric prior
above gives every \emph{individual} longer candidate
a strict penalty; it does not assert that all
posterior mass on longer genomes vanishes.
Other likelihood models require separately specified priors.

\subsection{An ambiguity caused solely by discarding strand orientation}
\label{sec:rc-class-ambiguity}

Population uniqueness can fail even for primitive P2 genomes if
the observation law discards the orientation of every read.
Here the \emph{strand-collapsed read law} samples an oriented
length-$L$ read uniformly by circular start, then records only
its reverse-complement class
$[w]=\{w,\operatorname{rc}(w)\}$, counting each distinct
oriented read word once in that class.
This is a separately defined experiment; no equivalence with
the historical MB09 bidirected-flow model is asserted.

\begin{proposition}[Primitive P2 does not ensure strand-collapsed identifiability]
\label{prop:rc-p2-ambiguity}
For every $L\ge2$, the primitive length-$2L$ DNA circles
\[
 S_L=\texttt{A}^L\texttt{C}\texttt{T}^{L-1},
 \qquad
 D_L=\texttt{A}^L\texttt{G}\texttt{T}^{L-1}
\]
both satisfy the oriented maximal-repeat condition P2 at read
length $L$. They are neither rotations nor reverse-complement
rotations of one another, but their length-$L$ strand-collapsed
read laws agree.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every length-$L$ window not containing the single
$\texttt{C}$ or $\texttt{G}$ is unchanged between
the circles. The windows containing the unique special
base in $S_L$ are exactly
$\texttt{A}^j\texttt{C}\texttt{T}^{L-1-j}$
for $0\le j<L$. Their reverse complements are
$\texttt{A}^{L-1-j}\texttt{G}\texttt{T}^j$,
which are precisely the corresponding windows of $D_L$,
in reverse order. Thus the read-class multiplicities agree.

The sole $\texttt{C}$ or $\texttt{G}$ ensures
primitivity and rules out rotational equivalence.
The reverse complement of $S_L$ has an
$\texttt{A}$ run of length $L-1$, whereas $D_L$
has a run of length $L$, ruling out the remaining
dihedral equivalence.

For P2, consider any repeated circular window of length
$k\ge L-1$ with $k<2L$. A window containing the unique
special base occurs only once. Every other window is
a substring of the single special-base-free run
$\texttt{T}^{L-1}\texttt{A}^L$; a mixed window
crossing its unique $\texttt{T}$-to-$\texttt{A}$
boundary occurs once. For such $k$, a pure
$\texttt{T}$ window occurs at most once, and a pure
$\texttt{A}$ window occurs twice only when $k=L-1$.
Hence the only repeated long window is
$\texttt{A}^{L-1}$ at starts $0$ and $1$.
There is no long maximal triple repeat and no
interleaving of two long maximal repeat pairs:
these are exactly the P2 requirements.
\end{proof}

For the small instance $L=3$, the distinct circles are
$\texttt{AAACTT}$ and $\texttt{AAAGTT}$.
An independent finite verifier computes the exact
P2 predicates and read-class multiplicities for
$2\le L\le12$; see
\texttt{scripts/verify\_rc\_p2\_ambiguity.py}.
The proposition and its proof are mathematical, not
separately kernel-checked in Lean. It rules out
a naive transfer of \emph{oriented} P2 uniqueness
to strand-collapsed reads, but leaves the actual
source-faithful physical molecule model to be specified
and studied.

\subsection{Open questions after the proved results}
\label{sec:open-questions-final}

Our results settle several precisely scoped statements but not a universal
criterion for finite-read maximum likelihood.  In particular, the strict
historical read-string counterexamples
(Theorems~\ref{thm:cex-historical-W1} and~\ref{thm:cex-historical-W2})
refute certain proposed implications; the same-length oriented rigidity
theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}), the population uniqueness theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:population}), and the finite-read confidence theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:p2-uniform-rate}) give positive conclusions on their
respective, explicitly restricted domains.  None of these statements is
silently extended to a different read orientation, candidate universe,
likelihood, or graph model.

The following are \emph{open questions}, not unproved lemmas used by
the theorems above.  Their formulations describe the boundary of the
present work rather than an assertion that any particular answer is true.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*.},leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt,topsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{General finite-sample likelihood classification.}
Can one give informative necessary and sufficient conditions, in terms
of the read multiset and the admissible genome/flow structure, under
which the generating genome maximizes the exact finite-sample likelihood?
The distinction between a fixed realized count vector $x$ and
\emph{every} allowed $x$ is essential, as are truth candidacy,
candidate length, and whether the score is exact multinomial,
fixed-$N$ binomial, or another sampling law.  The proved special-case
rigidity and effective-weight criteria do not classify all these
domains.

\item \textbf{A faithful physical reverse-complement overlap model.}
Precisely when does the source's bidirected physical overlap graph,
including parallel matches, reversed edges, self-complementary reads,
and transitive reduction, correspond to the graph and incidence
conventions used in the formal development?
We prove spelling preservation for specified reductions, but neither
identify them with every step of the published construction nor give
a general equivalence of the physical and oriented flow domains.

\item \textbf{Genome spelling versus feasible flow.}
Under what extra hypotheses does an admissible nonnegative integral
(or half-integral, where allowed) flow represent a \emph{single}
circular spelled genome with exactly the stipulated read
multiplicities?  Balance and vertex lower bounds alone do not supply
such a realization; the exact relationship among the full flow
polytope, one closed signed walk, and physical molecule-count vectors
remains to be characterized.

\item \textbf{Optimality under graph reductions and flow relaxation.}
When can maximizers and \emph{unique} maximizers be transported between
spelled genomes, physical-edge-orbit flow vectors, visit-count vectors,
and a transitive-reduced graph?  The proved objective-factorization
results require specific hypotheses.  In particular, noninjectivity
of a flow-to-visit map obstructs automatic transport of uniqueness;
the full reverse-complement MB09 optimizer correspondence
remains open.

\item \textbf{Sharp finite-read recovery rates.}
For the oriented primitive-P2 class,
Propositions~\ref{prop:p2-linear-rate}
and~\ref{prop:p2-linear-sharpness} now give matching
$\Theta(1/B)$ separation bounds even for fixed $q$.
What is the best universal leading constant, and
which primitive-P2 genome pairs are exact or
asymptotic minimizers for their given $(q,L)$?
The original finite enumerations do not resolve
these optimal-constant questions.
An unrestricted-universe uniform rate is \emph{not} an open
possibility: distinct genomes such as $AB$ and $ABAB$ have identical
read laws, preventing uniform recovery.

\item \textbf{Population identifiability for physical molecule reads.}
Which structural restrictions make population maximum likelihood
unique, up to the appropriate rotational or dihedral equivalence,
when each observation is a reverse-complement \emph{molecule class}
rather than an oriented word?  Proposition~\ref{prop:rc-p2-ambiguity}
shows that primitive P2 alone is insufficient for the explicitly
strand-collapsed law; its correspondence to the historical
physical molecule experiment remains to be established.

\item \textbf{Minimal sufficient side information.}
For a finite collection of candidate genomes and a specified
observed-read experiment, characterize the weakest additional
read-string or reconstruction certificate that forces posterior
probability one on the truth's equivalence class.  The abstract
posterior-support criterion and the conditional MAP results are
proved, but neither identifies a minimal historical decoder
certificate nor makes ordinary unrestricted ML inherit the
conditional guarantee.
\item \textbf{Recovery at the bridging threshold.}
In a specified source-faithful candidate universe, consider a sequence
of genomes and read lengths for which the number of reads is only large
enough to make coverage and bridging hold with high probability. For a \emph{specified estimator}, such as
exact ML under a supplied length bound $G\le B<2G$,
MAP under a stated length prior, or a measurable
$\varepsilon_n$-maximizer of unrestricted likelihood,
what sampling threshold or additional hypotheses
relate successful bridging to correct selection?
Unrestricted exact ML alone is not an estimator
on every sample, by Theorem~\ref{thm:unattained-ML}. The finite counterexamples concern individual
samples, whereas our finite-read bounds apply to an identifiable
restricted class and may require far more reads than bridging itself.
Neither result settles this joint high-probability regime.
\end{enumerate}

A separate \emph{source-interpretation question} is which precise
likelihood universe and observation convention the 2016 discussion
intended; see Appendix~\ref{app:referents}.  Its unresolved historical
referent is not itself a missing mathematical proof.  Further,
the source's matched-string bridging predicate differs from the
sampled-placement predicate, and proved counterexamples to one
must not be attributed to the other without a separate argument.

\subsection{Relation to the literature}

The classical spectrum-reconstruction foundations are Ukkonen's repeat
condition and Pevzner's analysis of equivalent cyclic traversals
\parencite{ukkonen1992,pevzner1995}, subsequently adapted to modern
assembly by \textcite{bresler2013}.
\textcite{myers2005} developed the string-graph formulation for
overlap-based assembly. \textcite{motahari2013} studied information-theoretic sequencing thresholds
under stochastic genome models, and the variable-length overlap
results of \textcite{hui2016} are another adjacent
contribution. These do not answer the finite-sample likelihood
question here.

Related likelihood formulations and probabilistic
\emph{assembly scores} include the bidirected-flow
complexity study of \textcite{medvedev2007},
the length-range convex formulation of \textcite{varma2011},
and the assembly evaluation tools CGAL, ALE and LAP
\parencite{cgal2013,ale2013,ghodsi2013}.
These approaches use different experimental assumptions,
read-placement models, candidate representations or
normalizations; none is identified here with our exact
uniform-start circular-genome likelihood. In particular,
incorporating a length range or a prior is a substantive
modeling choice, not a contradiction of the
unrestricted-domain non-attainment theorem.

The closest published result directly relating bridge
conditions to reconstruction predates the 2016 question:
Bresler, Bresler, and Tse prove a \emph{necessary} condition --- if a
problematic interleaved pair or triple repeat has all copies unbridged, another
same-length genome has the same read likelihood. Shomorony et al.\ strengthen
the hypothesis to sufficient conditions for exact reconstruction and then ask
whether those conditions also imply ML optimality. A 2025 correctness-guarantee
paper still presents the information/correctness line and the
maximum-likelihood line as separate \parencite{salmela2025}, which is meaningful
evidence that the 2016 question had not become a standard solved result. Our
negative witnesses are consistent with that picture: the implication is not
merely unproved but false under several precise readings, while the positive
same-length and population results show where the intuition does survive.

\subsection{Conclusion}

Reconstructing a circular genome from overlaps and asking which
genome best explains observed read counts are different problems.
The reconstruction theorem of Shomorony et al.\ gives conditions
under which the overlaps determine the truth. In the oriented,
same-length, complete-support setting, those conditions also
force the read counts and a unique circular spelling
(Corollary~\ref{cor:historical-oriented-same-length}).
With other candidate or observation models,
the exact likelihood can favor a different genome even when
the original reconstruction conditions hold.

The principal negative theorem goes beyond finite
counterexamples. For \emph{every} fixed truth whose oriented
read-overlap graph branches, unrestricted exact likelihood
selects the truth with probability at most
$O(n^{-(E-V)/2})$, tending to \emph{zero} as the number of reads
increases (Theorem~\ref{thm:unrestricted-branching-rate}).
Arbitrarily long candidate genomes can closely fit
sampling fluctuations through their balanced read frequencies.
More data alone does not cure this freedom to overfit.

Positive guarantees come from making the candidate universe
informative. For suitable truths, knowing an upper bound on
genome length excludes all nonrotational competitors below twice
the true length. Restricting both truth and competitors by P2
gives exponential finite-sample recovery, with a sharp
paper-proved separation order $\Theta(1/B)$
(Propositions~\ref{prop:p2-linear-rate}
and~\ref{prop:p2-linear-sharpness}).
A geometric length prior also produces an
always-attained, eventually correct MAP estimate
under the stated fixed-truth assumptions.
These conclusions do not settle the near-bridging
sampling threshold or the general physical molecule-flow
model. Appendix~\ref{app:ledger} and
\path{docs/verification.md} distinguish their
Lean-checked proofs, paper-only arguments, and open questions.
