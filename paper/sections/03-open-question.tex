\section{The mathematical question}
\label{sec:open}

\subsection{The published sentence}

The target sentence is \cite[Discussion, p.~i501]{shomorony2016}:

\begin{quote}
``The maximum-likelihood formulation of the AP (Medvedev and Brudno, 2009), on
the contrary, seems to be robust to these issues, and thus a good candidate for
the `correct' formulation. Understanding whether bridging conditions can be used
to guarantee that the maximum-likelihood sequence is the true sequence is
currently an open question.''
\end{quote}

The sentence combines two named ingredients. The first is the family of
repeat-bridging conditions under which the reads determine the genome. The
second is the maximum-likelihood formulation of Medvedev and Brudno
(\cite{medvedev2009}). This manuscript studies the implication the sentence
asks about, for precise versions of both ingredients.

\subsection{The bridging ingredient: the source's own predicate}

The historical bridging conditions are stated on observed read
\emph{strings}, not on their hidden sampled starts. We record the source
predicate exactly as the author-hosted supplement defines it
(\cite[Supplement, \S6.4, Definitions~1--2]{shomorony2016}): in every circular
interval of $L-1$ possible starts, some observed read string matches a genomic
window (coverage); and every triple repeat has every copy, and every
interleaved repeat pair has a copy, strictly straddled by an observed string
matched at a suitable position (bridging). The project also uses a
placement-based variant, $\Is$, defined in Section~\ref{sec:model}; the two
predicates are not identical, and results are stated for each separately. Both
are in force in the positive theorem and in the historical witnesses of
Section~\ref{sec:finite}.

\subsection{The likelihood ingredient: three objectives, one flow}

Medvedev and Brudno's formulation contains three materially different objects:
the \emph{exact multinomial} over read-type counts with candidate-intrinsic
length $N(D)$ (\S6.1), the \emph{product-of-binomial-marginals} approximation
that replaces $N(D)$ by an external genome size $N$ (\S6.1), and the
\emph{Section~6.2 bidirected flow} over DNA-molecule read overlaps, whose
feasible objects are flows and need not be sequences. The paper never identifies
which object the 2016 sentence denotes; we therefore state every theorem for a
named objective, and keep oriented read strings separate from
reverse-complement molecule classes.

\subsection{What this paper proves}

For each ingredient we ask two questions: is the truth \emph{a} maximizer, and
is it the \emph{unique} maximizer up to a stated genome equivalence? The
answers, proved in Sections~\ref{sec:finite} and~\ref{sec:population}, are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Positive, finite sample.} Under bridging, the truth's spectrum
        is the unique positive circulation of its window-support graph; on the
        same-length oriented slice the truth is the unique maximizer up to
        cyclic rotation. The extra restrictions (oriented reads, equal length,
        full-overlap support, truth candidature) are project-level assumptions,
        labelled where they are used.
  \item \textbf{Negative, finite sample.} Once candidate length is allowed to
        grow, or read types are molecule classes, explicit finite observations
        make a competitor strictly more likely than the truth under the exact
        multinomial, its binomial approximation, and the Section~6.2 flow; the
        same-length failure survives under the source's own read-string
        predicate.
  \item \textbf{Positive, population.} With the read distribution known
        exactly, the truth is always a maximizer, and among primitive P2
        genomes it is the unique maximizer up to cyclic rotation.
\end{itemize}

Two framing points govern everything that follows. First, likelihood is a
scoring function on candidate genomes, not a procedure for reconstructing one:
our counterexamples are failed rankings, not failed executions of an
assembler. Second, the published sentence does not fix the objective, the
read-type convention, the candidate class, or the maximizer-versus-uniqueness
reading; we resolve each explicitly rather than presenting any single theorem
as ``the'' answer to it. The detailed audit of the sentence's referents is
Appendix~\ref{app:audit}.
