\section{Modeling choices and limitations}
\label{app:modeling}

\paragraph{Circular genomes and read orientation.}
A circle removes arbitrary sequence endpoints and makes genomes
identifiable only up to rotation. The exact experiment chooses independent,
uniform circular start positions and reads a fixed-length, error-free,
\emph{oriented} window. It isolates reconstruction and likelihood, not
sequencing errors, read-length mixtures, nonuniform coverage, or a mixture
of source genomes. These are modeling choices, not assertions that biological
data satisfy them. With reverse-complement molecule observations, the read
types and correct genome equivalence change.

\paragraph{Multiplicity and likelihood.}
A candidate's read-type probability is its number of matching circular
starts divided by its own genome length. Observed counts retain sampling
fluctuations; support alone does not determine likelihood. The exact
multinomial includes a shared factorial coefficient. The fixed-$N$
product of marginal binomials is a different objective, with its
own domain. Its variables remain dependent under multinomial sampling;
increasing the number of trials does not make them independent at
fixed read-type probabilities.

\paragraph{Bridging predicates.}
For a latent placement model, a read bridges a particular repeat copy
when the sampled read extends beyond it on both sides. The historical
source instead quantifies matches of observed read \emph{strings} at
genomic positions. These predicates are not interchangeable, and the
main theorems specify which they use. The full source-level arguments
and independent checks are indexed in \path{docs/verification.md}.

\subsection{Technical boundaries for finite examples}
\label{app:finite-domain-boundaries}

\begin{remark}[Source and repository assumption surfaces]
\label{rem:surfaces}
The MB09 §6.2 model imposes a \emph{per-vertex} lower bound of one
molecule occurrence. Requiring each observed type to occur at least
as many times in a candidate as it did in the sample,
$d_D(w)\ge x(w)$, is stronger and is not the source rule.
The historical same-length molecule witness $W_2$ refutes even this
extra requirement, with exact likelihood ratio $3/2$ and binomial
ratio $9/5$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cex-historical-W2}). Another paper's
$2G$ strand-concatenation model is not thereby settled: its bridging
condition can fail for the known witnesses, and a universal
statement with no specified candidate class or likelihood objective
is not a determinate mathematical proposition.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Physical flow and circular spelling]
\label{rem:flow-domain}
A balanced bidirected flow is not, by definition, a single circular
genome. The overlap threshold $o_{\min}$ changes the underlying
graph, and vertex-throughput counts need not determine a physical
edge realization. The integer-flow feasible set, a half-integral
relaxation, a disconnected flow and a non-spellable count vector are
four distinct notions. At $o_{\min}=1$ there is a checked
non-spellable optimal count vector; at the full-overlap floor
$o_{\min}=L-1=2$ a disconnected optimum can have a spellable
throughput. Neither justifies transporting all flow-maximizers into
genome-maximizers. The exact certificates and their different
likelihood ratios are in \path{docs/verification.md}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Two-circle duplex versus strand concatenation]
\label{rem:duplex}
A physical double-stranded circle is naturally represented by the
two disjoint cyclic strands $S$ and $\rc(S)$, not by joining their
ends into one length-$2G$ circle. The latter creates artificial seams
and a different bridging predicate. When orientation is unobserved,
the disjoint duplex induces the MB09 reverse-complement molecule
read distribution; when orientation is observed it does not.
The original source does not select a general two-circle theorem,
and the additional construction is a disclosed project model.
\end{remark}
