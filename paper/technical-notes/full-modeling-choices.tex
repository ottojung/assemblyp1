\section{Modeling choices explained}
\label{app:modeling}

This appendix explains why the model of Section~\ref{sec:model} has the shape it
does. It is written as a deliberate explanatory layer: it does not prove
anything, and each choice is labeled by its origin --- historical/source
mandated, mathematical idealization, operational approximation, or
project-level extension. Where an external treatment explains a convention
better than we can inline, we cite it rather than reproduce background.

\subsection{Why the genome is circular}

\paragraph{Source origin.}
Shomorony et al.\ work with a circular sequence ``for ease of exposition \dots
[to] avoid edge effects,'' so that every start position has a length-$L$ window
\parencite[\S2]{shomorony2016}. Medvedev and Brudno's exact likelihood is likewise
stated for a ``circular genome'' \parencite[\S6.1]{medvedev2009}.

\paragraph{What it buys.}
Circularity removes the two ends: the window map $t\mapsto W_t(D)$ is defined at
every position, the spectrum has total $|D|$ with no boundary correction, and the
window-support graph of Definition~\ref{def:graph} is balanced and strongly
connected because it is generated by a single closed walk. Several proofs would
need extra boundary terms without this.

\paragraph{What it approximates.}
A real chromosome is linear; the circular model is appropriate when the genome is
genuinely circular (many bacterial genomes) or when reads near the ends are rare
relative to the genome length, so edge effects are negligible. It is an
idealization, not a claim that genomes are circles.

\paragraph{Mandatory consequence.}
Cyclic-shift equivalence is forced by circularity (Remark~\ref{rem:cyclic}).
A formalization that models a circle but compares literal strings is internally
inconsistent.

\subsection{Why reads are fixed-length, error-free, and uniformly sampled}

\paragraph{Source origin.}
The 2016 exposition draws each of $N$ reads independently and uniformly from the
$G$ circular length-$L$ substrings of $s$ \parencite[\S2]{shomorony2016}. Bresler et
al.\ use the same noiseless uniform model with bridging \parencite{bresler2013}.

\paragraph{Why errors can be abstracted away in the source theory.}
Shomorony et al.\ deliberately separate an error-free information-theoretic phase
from sequencing-error handling; their algorithm uses exact overlaps, and their
double-strand implementation adds reverse-complement reads as preprocessing
\parencite[\S4.1]{shomorony2016}. In an information-feasibility analysis this is a
modeling choice: it isolates the repeat-limited obstructions that determine the
information boundary. What the abstraction omits is real: sequencing errors
create spurious overlaps, turn unique repeats ambiguous, and interact with
coverage in ways a fixed $L$ cannot express. We therefore do not claim the
error-free model describes production assemblers.

\paragraph{Why uniform and independent.}
Uniformity over start positions makes the exact likelihood a multinomial with
probabilities $d_i/N(D)$; a non-uniform or position-dependent coverage profile
would destroy that conjugacy and change the objective. Independence of the \emph{individual read draws} makes the probability of an
ordered sample a product and produces the exact multinomial likelihood after
aggregating read-type counts. It does \emph{not} make the read-type count
variables independent; the separable product-binomial objective of
\parencite[\S6.1]{medvedev2009} is an additional modeling approximation.
Uniformity and independent draws are mathematical assumptions, not biological
laws; their consequences differ from independence of read-type counts.

\paragraph{A limitation in the source's binomial justification.}
Medvedev and Brudno motivate the approximation by saying that the multinomial
category-count variables become independent as the number of trials increases
\parencite[\S6.1]{medvedev2009}. At fixed category probabilities this is false.
If the sample consists of $n$ independent categorical draws with
$p_i,p_j\in(0,1)$ and $i\ne j$, then
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=-np_ip_j,\qquad
 \operatorname{Corr}(X_i,X_j)
   =-\sqrt{\frac{p_ip_j}{(1-p_i)(1-p_j)}}.
\]
The correlation is independent of $n$. In the simplest two-category case,
$p_1=p_2=1/2$, the exact law has $X_1+X_2=n$ with probability $1$ and
$\operatorname{Corr}(X_1,X_2)=-1$ for every $n\ge1$. The independent product
of its two binomial marginals instead gives this event probability
$\binom{2n}{n}4^{-n}$, which tends to $0$. Thus, in this fixed-probability
example, the product law does not even approach the multinomial joint law
in total variation as $n$ grows.

A distinct \emph{rare-category} scaling, in which the relevant $p_i$
approach zero while $np_i$ stay bounded, can support a Poisson
approximation. Poissonization of the \emph{total read count} also leads to
independent category-wise \emph{Poisson} variables, not independent
binomials with a fixed total of $n$ trials. Neither justification follows
from high read coverage alone. Our theorems about the product-binomial
objective are statements about that well-defined surrogate; we do not
transfer its optimality claims to the exact joint likelihood without a
separate proof.

\subsection{Why multiplicity is retained}

The likelihood is a function of the observed read-type \emph{counts}
$x(w)$, not of the set of distinct read strings. This is source-mandated: the
exact objective is the multinomial
\[
  \frac{n!}{\prod_w x(w)!}\prod_w(d_w/N(D))^{x(w)}.
\]
Collapsing reads to a set
would discard the frequencies that the objective scores and would make the
finite-sampling failure of Example~\ref{ex:frequencies} invisible. Keeping the
latent true placements separate from the observable counts is equally important:
bridging is a property of $(S,R)$, where $R$ records placements, while the
likelihood estimator may not see them.

\subsection{Oriented reads versus reverse-complement molecules}

\paragraph{Two genuinely different sources.}
Shomorony's theory uses oriented length-$L$ substrings; reverse complement enters
only as experimental preprocessing that \emph{adds} reads \parencite[\S4.1]{shomorony2016}.
Medvedev and Brudno instead model a read as a DNA \emph{molecule}, an unordered
pair of reverse-complementary strands, represented once in the graph
\parencite[\S3.1,\S4.1]{medvedev2009}. This is not notation: the two models have
different read-type spaces.

\paragraph{Consequences.}
Under oriented types, reverse-complementing a candidate relabels its type counts
by $t\mapsto\rc(t)$, and the candidate and its reverse complement need not tie.
In fact they tie only when the observed count vector is reverse-complement
symmetric, which fails in general, e.g.\ $D=AAAT$ at $L=1$ has $A$-count $3$
while $\rc(D)=TTTA$ has $A$-count $1$. Under molecule classes, a candidate and
its reverse complement \emph{always} tie because class counts are
reverse-complement invariant. Hence the read-type convention and the genome
equivalence are coupled (Appendix~\ref{app:referents}): molecule types force dihedral
equivalence, oriented types force only cyclic shift. The internal tension in
Medvedev and Brudno's Section~6.1 --- the text writes ``$4^k$ such variables''
while its vocabulary is $k$-molecules --- must be resolved per formalization
rather than glossed.

\subsection{Candidate length, known genome size, and same-length restriction}

These are three distinct assumptions, and conflating them is the most common
source-faithfulness error:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
  \item the likelihood may have a \emph{candidate-intrinsic} length $N(D)$
        (exact multinomial);
  \item it may be supplied an \emph{external} genome-size parameter $N$, used in
        the per-type binomial marginals (the Section~6.1 approximation);
  \item the \emph{candidate class} may be restricted to $|D|=G$.
\end{enumerate}
(a) and (b) are different objectives; (b) does not imply (c). Medvedev and
Brudno say ``we assume that the genome size is known''
\parencite[\S6.1]{medvedev2009}, and a contemporaneous formulation fixes the assembly
length to the known genome length and justifies it by scale-degeneracy
\parencite{ghodsi2016}. But the 2016 sentence itself fixes neither, so a same-length
theorem is a restricted result unless a source argument fixes the restriction.
The fixed-length witnesses of Section~\ref{sec:finite} are same-length precisely
to be robust: by Lemma~\ref{lem:negative-transfer} they refute the statement over
all circular candidates as well.

\subsection{Finite sample versus population}

A finite sample is what a sequencing experiment provides; a population is the
distribution it is drawn from. The two answer different questions. In the finite
regime the empirical frequencies fluctuate, and a wrong candidate can win
(Example~\ref{ex:frequencies}); the question is whether bridging is strong enough
to prevent this anyway, which Section~\ref{sec:finite} answers in the negative
for precise readings. In the population regime the fluctuation is gone, so the
maximizer statement is automatic and only identifiability remains
(Section~\ref{sec:population}). The population model is a project-level
idealization used to isolate the identifiability question; it is not the
historical finite-data model, and it does not settle it.

\subsection{What this paper deliberately does not model}

To keep the main narrative readable we excluded several directions that the
repository has not studied in depth: linear genomes with genuine end effects;
variable-length reads; noisy or adversarial reads beyond the source's
abstraction; and the MB09 flow's non-contiguous assemblies as
sequence-valued candidates. We list them here rather than silently omitting
them, and we cite the adjacent literature on variable-length reads
\parencite{hui2016} and safe/complete assembly \parencite{tomescu2016,rahman2022} for
readers who want those directions.

\subsection{Technical model boundaries for the finite witnesses}
\label{app:finite-domain-boundaries}

The following details establish precise relationships between the
project-level candidate models and the historical physical model.
They are kept here rather than in the sequence of main theorems.

\begin{remark}[Which assumption surfaces are still open, and which are not the
source's]
\label{rem:surfaces}
The molecule witnesses above use the source's per-vertex lower bound, i.e.\
support equality. Medvedev--Brudno MB09 states only ``Each vertex has a lower
bound of $1$ since it represents a read that must be present in the genome at
least once''; the strictly stronger per-occurrence requirement $d_D(w)\ge x(w)$
for every observed type is a \emph{repository-added} assumption surface, not a
source definition. That distinction matters for what may be reported as
settled:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Refuted.} Same-length MB09 candidate class, molecule read types,
    per-occurrence strengthening. The witness is $S=ATATACAC$, $D=ATACACAC$
    ($G=8$, $L=3$, realized starts $(1,3,4,5,6,7)$, $n=6<N=8$): exact ratio
    $3/2$, binomial ratio $9/5$, with both genomes literal MB09 bidirected
    circuits and both per-occurrence feasible. The mechanism needs the real
    four-letter DNA alphabet: with a self-complementary base the searched scope
    contains no same-length instance at all
    (Thm.~\ref{thm:cex-peroccurrence}). This is a \emph{determinate} row, so it
    is reported as settled; it is not a reading of the 2016 sentence, because the
    strengthening is a repository-added surface.
  \item \textbf{Open in both directions, and not a determinate reading of the
    2016 sentence at all.} The doubled-strand $2G$ \emph{concatenation}
    convention of \parencite{bresler2013}, i.e.\ one length-$2G$ circle
    $S\cdot\rc(S)$. No bounded search is used as evidence in either direction:
    the absence of a known beat in the searched scopes is recorded as
    \emph{absence of a known witness}, not as evidence of openness, and two of
    those scopes are vacuous because the remapped $\Is$ is unsatisfiable there.
    The known $AAATAT\to AAAAAT$ witness is \emph{inadmissible} under this
    remap: the doubled circle carries a length-$4$ triple repeat that no
    length-$3$ read can bridge, for \emph{every} read set
     (\texttt{doubled\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}information\_\allowbreak{}feasible}). The row is therefore open in
     both directions rather than refuted. The \emph{compatibility} of this
     convention with the oriented one is now kernel-checked in both directions:
     the remap is exactly compatible with the oriented reading iff read-seat
     preservation, seam--wrap agreement, and doubled-circle feasibility all hold,
     and off that locus the two models are incomparable --- $GGGA$ satisfies the
     remapped $\Is$ while failing the oriented $\Is$ (spurious wrapping-read
     seats), and $AAATAT$ is the reverse
     (\texttt{Bresler\allowbreak{}Remap\allowbreak{}Compatibility.\allowbreak{}remap\_\allowbreak{}natural\_\allowbreak{}seat\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}sound},
     \texttt{remap\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}complete}). The physical duplex is better
     represented as \emph{two disjoint} cyclic strands $S$ and $\rc(S)$
     (Remark~\ref{rem:duplex}).
  \item \textbf{Not a determinate proposition.} The reading of the sentence as an
    unspecified general ML principle: the 2016 text fixes no objective, so no
    finite witness settles it as stated. Its three source-nameable members (the
    exact multinomial, the fixed-$N$ binomial, and the MB09 flow with §6.1
    costs) are each refuted above.
\end{itemize}
No determinate row of the matrix remains open. What lies outside the resolved set
is (i) the doubled-strand $2G$ concatenation convention, which is a different
paper's convention (a project-level convention, not a source fact), is
\emph{not a determinate source row}, and is open in \emph{both} directions with
no bounded search used as evidence because the known witness is inadmissible
there, and (ii) the unspecified-principle reading, which is not a determinate
proposition and cannot be settled from source. Neither is a determinate
source-supported reading of the 2016 sentence. We flag these because they are
exactly the kind of assumption-surface change that separates a refuted
statement from an open one, so any claim that ``all source-supported interpretations are resolved''
requires those qualifications.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The general MB09 flow domain, and two optima and an overlap
floor with distinct meanings]
\label{rem:flow-domain}
The MB09 refutations above are stated for \emph{spelled} bidirected circuits.
The failure persists over the \emph{whole} MB09 flow domain, at two values of
the explicit overlap floor $o_{\min}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item at $o_{\min}=1$ ($L=3$) the truth is still not a maximizer, and the flow
        optimum $d^*=(AAA{:}2,AAT{:}1,TAA{:}1)$ (with
        $d^*_3=(AAA{:}3,AAT{:}1,TAA{:}1)$) spells no circular molecule of any
        length, so the maximum-likelihood \emph{object} of the flow domain is a
        non-sequence and the maximum-likelihood \emph{sequence} does not exist
        there;
  \item at the full overlap $o_{\min}=L-1=2$ ($L=3$) the truth $AAATAT$ is
        likewise not a maximizer, but here the unique integer optimum
        $d^*=(AAA{:}2,AAT{:}1,ATA{:}1,TAA{:}1)$ has \emph{disconnected positive
        support} (a doubled $AAA$ self-overlap together with the circuit
        $AAT\to ATA\to TAA\to AAT$), so positive-support connectedness of all
        optima is false, and it strictly beats the truth
        ($\Lik(d^*)/\Lik(d_S)=1280/243$). Its throughput, however, \emph{is}
        spellable: it is the spectrum of $AAAAT$ and also has a connected
        realization. This is disconnectedness of the \emph{flow}, not
        non-spellability of the \emph{throughput}; ``positive-support
        connectedness of all optima'' is a project-level strengthening, not a
        Medvedev--Brudno notion, and the flow-to-sequence gap is untouched by
        it.
\end{itemize}
Two optima, one parameter, and one distinction must be kept separate:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \textbf{integer flow optimum} --- the argmax of the §6.1 objective
        over integer throughput vectors $1\le d\le N$ that are genuine MB09 flows
        (the $d^*$, $d^*_3$ above); this is the MB09 object the source
        defines;
  \item the \textbf{half-integral relaxation} --- the argmax when $d$ ranges over
        half-integers (the per-coordinate maximizer of
        $(d/N)^x((N-d)/N)^{n-x}$ is $d=xN/n$, e.g.\ $5/2$ for the $AAA$ coordinate
        here, which is neither an integer nor a MB09 flow throughput);
  \item the \textbf{overlap floor} $o_{\min}$ --- the $o_{\min}=1$ and the
        $o_{\min}=L-1$ witnesses live on different graphs (the shorter overlaps
        exist only when $o_{\min}\le L-2$), so neither may be read as the other;
        the spelled witnesses (Thm.~\ref{thm:cex-molecule} and the rows
        below it) are stated at $o_{\min}=L-1=2$;
  \item the \textbf{throughput versus the flow realization} --- the
        $o_{\min}=L-1$ optimum's throughput is spellable ($AAAAT$) while its
        flow support is disconnected, whereas the $o_{\min}=1$ optimum's
        throughput is not spellable at all. ``Non-spellable spectrum'' and
        ``disconnected flow'' are \emph{different} properties.
\end{itemize}
The relaxation is a different, larger upper bound; substituting it for the
integer flow optimum would overstate what the MB09 domain refutes.
\kernelcheck{}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The physical duplex is two disjoint circles, not a concatenation]
\label{rem:duplex}
There are two mathematically different doubled-strand models. The
$2G$ \emph{concatenation} of \parencite{bresler2013} glues $S$ and $\rc(S)$ into one
circle; the alternative --- the one matching the physical double-stranded
circle --- is \emph{two disjoint} cyclic strands $S$ and $\rc(S)$. The
distinction is material rather than cosmetic. The reverse-complement map
$\rho(i)=(G-1-i)\bmod G$ reverses order and preserves strict bridging, so
\emph{circle-by-circle} $\Is$ on the disjoint duplex is exactly equivalent to
$\Is(S)$, and the $AAATAT\to AAAAAT$ witness survives (ratios $3$ and $5$). The
\emph{duplex-as-a-whole} reading is ill-defined (there is no cyclic order on two
disjoint circles) and strictly stronger: six mixed cross-strand triple repeats
of length $\ge L-1$ are unbridgeable for every read set. The concatenation and
the disjoint duplex are genuinely inequivalent: their candidate spectra differ
exactly when the seam words are not matched by wrapping words, which happens for
half of the binary words in the searched scope. Finally, when strand orientation
is \emph{unobserved}, the disjoint-duplex molecule-class distribution
$2\,m_S(C)/(2G)=m_S(C)/G$ is identical to the Medvedev--Brudno molecule
distribution (self-reverse-complementary palindromes counted once), so it is not
a distinct likelihood objective there; only \emph{oriented} reads differ. All of
this is a disclosed modelling decision, not a reading of the 2016 sentence, and
it settles neither the concatenation row nor the referent.
\kernelcheck{}
\end{remark}
