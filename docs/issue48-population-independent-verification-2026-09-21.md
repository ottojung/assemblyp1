# Independent verification: population/exact-frequency identifiability for primitive `P2` (`WEAK`) genomes

_Status: independent mathematical verification plus exhaustive exact computation.
This note independently re-derives and checks the population identifiability
claim recorded on the issue-#48 branch artifacts
[`population-identifiability-intrinsic-genomes.md`](population-identifiability-intrinsic-genomes.md)
and the concurrent synthesis note `docs/synthesis-finite-rows-and-repairs-2026-09-21.md`
(on branch `synthesis/finite-rows-and-repairs-0921`),
and it reconciles that claim with the circular-`q`-gram source resolution in
[`literature/circular-qgram-identifiability-and-Is-threshold-2026-09-21.md`](literature/circular-qgram-identifiability-and-Is-threshold-2026-09-21.md).
It is **not** a Lean result, it does not touch the finite-sample open question of
[`open-problem.md`](open-problem.md), and it changes no source-faithful historical
result._

_Reproduction: `python3 scripts/verify_population_independent.py` (default) and
`... --full` (widened ranges). Self-contained, exact integer/`Fraction`
arithmetic, deterministic, exits non-zero on failure. Default run ≈ 20 s; full
run ≈ 6 min. This script shares no code with `scripts/verify_population_identifiability.py`
or `scripts/bridging_spectrum_uniqueness.py`._

## 0. Verdict

Let `p_D(w) = d_D(w)/|D|` be the population read law (normalized `L`-mer
spectrum) of a circular genome `D`, and let

* `P2(D,L) = WEAK(D,L) = TRF(D,L) ∧ ILF(D,L)` be the source-`I_s` shadow
  (`TRF`: no Bresler triple repeat of length `≥ L−1`; `ILF`: no interleaved
  maximal-repeat pair with both constituents of length `≥ L−1`);
* `primitive(D)` mean `D` is not a nontrivial power of a shorter circular word.

**Claim (under test).** If `S,T` are primitive and `P2`-admissible and
`p_T = p_S`, then `T` is a cyclic shift of `S`. Equivalently, the primitive
`P2` class is identifiable from its normalized `L`-spectrum, **including
proportional-spectrum mates** `d_T = c·d_S` with `c ≠ 1`.

**Verdict: the claim is TRUE.** The two halves have different epistemic status,
and this is the main content of this note.

1. **Cross-length mates (`c > 1` or `c < 1`) are impossible** — an independent
   mathematical proof (§3–§4), not a computation and not a citation. In fact the
   proof needs primitivity and `TRF` only on the **larger-spectrum side**
   (Theorem P′, §4); `WEAK` is more than enough. Primitivity alone is *not*
   sufficient: the primitive `TRF` truth `S = AAB` and the primitive but
   non-`TRF` mate `T = AAABAB` (`L = 2`) are proportional with `c = 2` (§6).
2. **Equal-length mates (`c = 1`)**, i.e. `P2(S) ⇒` the `L`-mer multiset
   determines `S` up to rotation, is the `K = L−1` instance of the published
   Bresler–Bresler–Tse 2013 theorem quoted in
   [`literature/circular-qgram-identifiability-and-Is-threshold-2026-09-21.md`](literature/circular-qgram-identifiability-and-Is-threshold-2026-09-21.md)
   §1.1/§3. I corroborate it exhaustively here (§5) but do **not** re-prove the
   classical characterization from scratch; its status is *source theorem*
   (modulo the circular Eulerian-cycle reading), not an independent proof of
   this note.
3. **Stronger one-sided observation (evidence + classical-generator argument).**
   In every range searched, a single `WEAK` side already isolates the spectrum
   fibre: there is no same-length, equal-spectrum, non-rotation pair with even
   *one* `WEAK` side (§5). This is consistent with the classical fact that the
   `k`-equivalence class is generated by swaps at obstructing repeats, which
   `WEAK` removes; I record it as an observed strengthening, not as a proved
   theorem of this note.

So the population/exact-frequency identifiability statement for primitive `P2`
is **not refuted**; the novel population content (exclusion of proportional
mates) is unconditional, and the equal-length residue rests on published
combinatorics.

## 1. The claim, stated exactly

Fix an alphabet `Σ` and read length `L ≥ 2`. A circular word `D` of length `n`
has spectrum `d_D(w) = #{t ∈ Z_n : win_L(D,t) = w}` and population law
`p_D(w) = d_D(w)/n`. Two words have the same population law iff
`d_T = c·d_S` for some rational `c > 0` (then `c = |T|/|S|`), iff their
normalized spectra are equal. The claim is injectivity of `D ↦ p_D` on the
class

```text
C = { D : D primitive and P2(D,L) },
```

up to cyclic shift. By the repository's full-read reduction
(`mathematics/bridging-and-spectrum-uniqueness.md` §1), a truth `S` admits some
`I_s` read set iff `P2(S,L)`; so `C` is exactly the candidate-intrinsic universe
of the population question.

The relation `c = 1` is the equal-length, equal-multiset case; `c ≠ 1` is the
**proportional-spectrum-mate** case that this note is asked to settle.

## 2. Conventions (fixed; none are weakened)

* Circular oriented single-strand genome, cyclic-shift equivalence
  (Shomorony et al. 2016 Eq. (1) panel). Reverse complement is handled only in
  the separate boundary of §7.
* Bresler maximal repeats / triple repeats / interleaved pairs exactly as in
  [`bridging-source-semantics.md`](bridging-source-semantics.md) and quoted in
  the circular-`q`-gram note: two copies at `a,b` are a maximal repeat pair iff
  the preceding symbols differ and the following symbols differ; a triple
  repeat requires the three preceding symbols not all equal and the three
  following symbols not all equal; an interleaved pair is two maximal pairs
  whose four starts alternate (`t₁<t₂<t₃<t₄` up to rotation).
* `TRF`, `ILF`, `WEAK`, `STRONG` as in §0; `STRONG` means no `(L−1)`-mer occurs
  twice. One has `STRONG ⇒ WEAK`.
* The script's predicates agree pointwise with the concurrent
  `scripts/verify_population_identifiability.py` on every primitive canonical
  word of the ranges tested (`WEAK/TRF/ILF/STRONG` all pairwise equal; checked
  separately during preparation), so the two computations are genuinely
  independent implementations of the *same* objects.

## 3. Independent proof of Lemma L* (multiplicity bound)

> **Lemma L*.** If `D` is primitive and `TRF(D,L)`, then every `(L−1)`-mer of
> `D` occurs at most twice.

**Proof.** Suppose an `(L−1)`-mer occurs at three distinct starts `t₁,t₂,t₃`.

*Extend right:* let `ℓ ≥ L−1` be the length of the maximal common right
extension of the three copies, so the three length-`ℓ` windows at `t₁,t₂,t₃` are
equal while their following symbols are not all equal.
*Extend left:* let `j* ≥ 0` be maximal such that the three length-`(ℓ+j)` windows
at `tᵢ−j` are equal for all `j ≤ j*`; then their preceding symbols are not all
equal.

`j*` exists because `j = 0` works, and `ℓ + j* < n`: if the three copies agreed
over a full period, `D` would be invariant under the nonzero shift `t₂−t₁` and
hence a nontrivial power, contradicting primitivity.

At `j*` the three equal windows have length `ℓ + j* ≥ L−1 ≥ L−1`, the following
symbols are not all equal, and the preceding symbols are not all equal. That is
a Bresler triple repeat of length `ℓ + j* ≥ L−1`, contradicting `TRF(D,L)`. ∎

The bound is attained: `D = AAAB` at `L = 3` has the `2`-mer `AA` twice and is
primitive `TRF`; and `D = AAB` at `L = 2` has the `1`-mer `A` twice. Primitivity
is essential: a power `(AB)^m` has `AB` occurring `m` times and is not primitive.
Only the triple-repeat half of `WEAK` is used.

_Independent confirmation._ Exhaustive check `[2]` of the verifier over
`{A,B}` (all primitive `TRF` words, `n ≤ 16`, `L ≤ 16`), `{A,B,C}`
(`n ≤ 11`, `L ≤ 6`), `{A,B,C,D}` (`n ≤ 9`, `L ≤ 5`): the maximum observed
`(L−1)`-mer multiplicity is exactly `2`, never `3`.

## 4. Independent proof of Theorem P (proportional mates are impossible)

> **Theorem P′.** Let `T` be a word that is primitive and `TRF(T,L)`. Then there
> is no word `S` with `d_T = c·d_S` for a rational `c > 1`.

**Proof.** Write `b_v, a_v` for the multiplicity of an `(L−1)`-mer `v` in `T`,
`S`. Since the `L`-mer spectra are proportional with ratio `c`, so are the
`(L−1)`-mer multiplicities: `b_v = c·a_v` for every `v`, and the `(L−1)`-mer
supports of `S` and `T` coincide.

By Lemma L*, `b_v ≤ 2`. For every `v` in the common support, `a_v ≥ 1`, so

```text
a_v = b_v / c ≤ 2 / c < 2,
```

and since `a_v` is a positive integer, `a_v = 1`; then `b_v = c` is an integer
with `c > 1` and `b_v ≤ 2`, so `c = 2` and `b_v = 2`.

Hence every `(L−1)`-mer of `S` occurs exactly once. Then every `L`-mer of `S`
occurs exactly once, and the `(L−1)`-mer de Bruijn graph of `S` has one incoming
and one outgoing edge at each vertex; as `S` is a single closed walk, that graph
is a **simple directed cycle** and `S` is its cycle word up to rotation. `T`
traverses the same simple cycle with every edge doubled, and every vertex of the
doubled cycle still has a unique distinct outgoing edge, so `T` is forced to be
that cycle word traversed twice — a nontrivial power, contradicting primitivity
of `T`. ∎

> **Theorem P.** If `S,T` are primitive and `TRF`, and `d_T = c·d_S` with
> `c > 0`, then `c = 1`. (Apply Theorem P′ to whichever side is larger; for
> `c < 1` swap the roles. `WEAK` in place of `TRF` is weaker and hence also
> sufficient.)

**Sharpening (why the hypotheses are what they are).**

* Theorem P′ uses `TRF` and primitivity only on the **larger-spectrum** side.
  Since `|T| = c|S|`, that side is `T` for `c > 1`.
* **Primitivity alone is not enough.** `S = AAB` and `T = AAABAB` (`L = 2`) are
  both primitive, satisfy `d_T = 2·d_S` (both have normalized spectrum
  `{AA:1/3, AB:1/3, BA:1/3}`), and are not rotations. Here `T` fails `TRF`
  (its `1`-mer `A` occurs four times), so the failed hypothesis is exactly the
  one Theorem P′ needs.
* In particular the population claim is **not** repairable by dropping the
  admissibility hypothesis on the candidate; both primitivity and admissibility
  are needed (and, by P′, for the cross-length half only the larger side needs
  admissibility).

_Independent confirmation._ Exhaustive check `[3]` of the verifier groups **all**
primitive canonical words by normalized spectrum across lengths and finds
**zero** cross-length proportional collisions among `TRF` words over `{A,B}`
(`n ≤ 16`), `{A,B,C}` (`n ≤ 11`), `{A,B,C,D}` (`n ≤ 9`) — `281 154` primitive
`TRF` instances in the full run (default run: `18 927`). Same-length spectral
collisions do occur (`8 822` in the full run, e.g. `AABABB`/`AABBAB` at `L=3`),
which is exactly why `TRF` alone does not finish the equal-length step.

## 5. The equal-length step (`c = 1`)

Theorem P reduces the population claim to: for primitive `P2` `S`, is any word
`T` of the same length and same `L`-mer multiset a rotation of `S`?

This is the repository's Conjecture 4, and the circular-`q`-gram note resolves
it as the `K = L−1` instance of Bresler–Bresler–Tse 2013, Theorem 3: their
"Ukkonen condition" at `K` ("no triple or interleaved repeats of length at least
`K`") is, at `K = L−1`, *literally* the conjunction `TRF ∧ ILF = WEAK`; the
theorem then gives a unique Eulerian cycle, i.e. `S` up to rotation.

**Epistemic status here.** I did **not** re-prove the classical
Ukkonen–Pevzner/Bresler–Bresler–Tse characterization. The equal-length half is
therefore a *source theorem* (with the circular Eulerian-cycle reading made
explicit in the circular-`q`-gram note, §3.2), corroborated by the computation
below — not an independent proof of this note. Cross-length is different: §4 is
self-contained.

**Corroboration.** Check `[4]` of the verifier enumerates all same-length pairs
of primitive canonical words with equal normalized spectrum, removes rotations,
and counts pairs with a `WEAK` side:

| range | same-length equal-spectrum non-rotation pairs | both sides `WEAK` | either side `WEAK` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `{A,B}`, `n ≤ 16`; `{A,B,C}`, `n ≤ 11`; `{A,B,C,D}`, `n ≤ 10` (full) | `1 604 510` | **0** | **0** |

The `0` in the "both `WEAK`" column is the required equal-length claim (both
`S,T` primitive `P2`). The `0` in the "either `WEAK`" column is the strictly
stronger one-sided observation: **a single `P2` side already suffices in the
searched ranges**. That strengthening is exactly what one expects from the
classical *generator* form of the characterization — the `k`-equivalence class
is generated by swaps each of which requires an interleaved/triple repeat of
length `≥ L−1` in the word being transformed, and `WEAK` has none — but
reproducing the generator theorem is outside this note, so the one-sided column
is recorded as observation, not asserted as a theorem.

## 6. Sharpness counterexamples (all exact)

| Hypothesis removed | `S` | `T` | `L` | `c` | `T` primitive | `S`/`T` `WEAK` | rotations? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| primitivity | `AAB` | `AABAAB = S²` | 3 | 2 | no | yes / yes | no |
| candidate admissibility (larger side) | `AAB` | `AAABAB` | 2 | 2 | yes | yes / **no** | no |
| candidate admissibility (larger side) | `AAAB` | `AAAABAAB` | 3 | 2 | yes | yes / **no** | no |

* Row 1 shows primitivity cannot be dropped even with `WEAK` on both sides (the
  tandem/scale ambiguity `k·d_S`, `k ≥ 2`).
* Rows 2–3 show admissibility cannot be dropped, and that the failure is on the
  **larger** side, matching Theorem P′: the larger primitive mate is precisely
  the one that fails `TRF`.
* Additionally, `TRF` (without `ILF`) is insufficient for the equal-length step:
  `AABABB`/`AABBAB` (`L = 3`) are primitive, `TRF`, have the same `L`-mer
  multiset, and are not rotations; both fail `ILF`. This is why the equal-length
  theorem needs the full `WEAK`, while the cross-length theorem needs only `TRF`.

## 7. Boundary: the molecule / reverse-complement panel (a refutation there)

The population claim is tied to the **oriented** read-type panel. On the
double-strand / `k`-molecule panel the read type is the reverse-complement class
and equivalence is dihedral. There the positive statement is **false**, even
for primitive `STRONG` (hence `WEAK`) equal-length genomes:

* `S = AACAGT`, `T = AACTGT`, `L = 3`. Both are primitive and `STRONG` (all
  `2`-mers distinct). Their molecule-class length-`3` laws are equal (each of
  the six classes has weight `1/6`), yet `T` is neither a cyclic shift nor a
  reverse-complement rotation of `S`.

This is verified exactly (check `[1]`, last line) and is a genuine boundary of
the claim, consistent with the population note §7. It does not affect the
oriented result.

**Overall**: the population/exact-frequency identifiability claim for primitive
`P2` — including proportional-spectrum mates — **stands**. Its cross-length half
is unconditionally proved; its equal-length half is a published combinatorial
theorem; no counterexample was found, and the only refutation in scope is on the
disjoint molecule panel.
